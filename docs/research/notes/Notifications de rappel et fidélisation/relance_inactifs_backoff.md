# Win-back of lapsed users and adaptive back-off of ignored reminders (research notes, Nov 2026)

Scope: how consumer apps re-engage inactive users with notifications (timing, number of messages, effectiveness, annoyance risk), how they stop reminders that are ignored, and what is doable with local (on-device) notifications only. Context: small "year in pixels" habit/journal app, ~200 accounts, ~19% active after D7, local notifications only, max one grouped daily reminder; current proposal = go silent after 3 ignored daily reminders, open question = one last gentle message ~7 days later.

Evidence labels used below: **[peer-reviewed]** = academic paper; **[company research]** = first-party data published by the company itself (not independently replicated); **[vendor]** = marketing content from an engagement/analytics vendor; **[weak vendor/blog]** = vendor or SEO blog with no methodology; **[anecdote]** = forum/user report or executive quote without numbers; **[policy/docs]** = official platform text.

---

## 1. Win-back timing, number of messages, reactivation rates and decay with time since last use

### Takeaway
There is no rigorous public study that pins the "best" win-back day for a habit/journaling app; the only quantitative day-7/14/30 benchmarks are from vendor blogs without methodology, and they all agree on one shape: response falls steeply with time since last use (roughly halving from day 7 to day 14 and again by day 30), and most playbooks cap a win-back sequence at about 3 messages. Industry state definitions (Duolingo: "reactivated" = back after 7–29 days, "resurrected" = 30+ days; Drink Less trial: "disengaged" = 7+ consecutive days without use) make ~day 7 the natural first checkpoint for a daily-use app.

### Cited Findings
**Definitions / thresholds used by credible sources**
- Duolingo's growth model (Jorge Mazal, ex-CPO, Lenny's Newsletter, 2023-02-28) defines user states by days of absence: *current* = active today and at least once in the prior 6 days; *reactivated* = "first engagement after being away for 7-29 days"; *resurrected* = "first day of engagement after being away for 30 days or longer"; *at-risk WAU* = inactive today but active in one of the prior 6 days; *at-risk MAU* = inactive past 7 days but active in one of prior 23 days; *dormant* = inactive 31+ days. Gives no reactivation/resurrection rates. [company research] — [Lenny's Newsletter, "How Duolingo reignited user growth"](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)
- Same source: improving CURR (current-user retention rate) had "5 times the impact of the second-best metric" on DAU in Duolingo's simulation; i.e., keeping currently-active users from lapsing mattered far more than winning back lapsed ones. CURR rose 21% over 4 years, cutting daily churn of best users by >40%. [company research] — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)
- Drink Less (self-monitoring/diary app, daily local reminder) trial defines disengagement as "the first day in a period of ≥7 consecutive days of no use." [peer-reviewed] — [Bell et al., JMIR mHealth uHealth 2023;11:e38342](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10337295/)

**Vendor benchmarks for day 7 / 14 / 30 (all unverified, no methodology)**
- PushPilot: three win-back pushes at 7, 14 and 30 days inactive; open-rate "benchmarks" 6–12% (7-day inactive), 4–8% (14-day), 2–5% (30-day); suggests a cap of 3 win-back pushes per user per quarter. [weak vendor/blog] — [PushPilot, Win back inactive users workflow](https://pushpilot.ai/workflows/win-back-inactive-users)
- Vmobify: claims recovery 8–15% at day 7 vs 3–8% at day 14; calls day 3–14 the highest-recovery window; 10–25% overall for a day 3/7/14 sequence. No data source named. [weak vendor/blog] — [Vmobify, Push notification strategy 2026](https://vmobify.com/blog/push-notification-strategy); [Vmobify, App win-back playbook](https://vmobify.com/blog/app-re-engagement-winback)
- MWM glossary: "Typical recovery rates: 20-30% for 7-day lapsed users, 5-15% for 30-day, under 5% for 90+ days lapsed." No source. [weak vendor/blog] — [mwm.ai, Re-engagement](https://mwm.ai/glossary/re-engagement)
- SemNexus: recommends ~3 messages per re-engagement window as a working maximum and says sequences with 7+ messages "accelerate unsubscribes"; argues a utility app opened about weekly shouldn't trigger reactivation logic before 21–30 days of silence (i.e., threshold should scale with the user's normal cadence). [weak vendor/blog] — [SemNexus, Re-engagement campaigns: when to fire them](https://semnexus.com/re-engagement-campaigns-when-to-fire-them-when-not-to); [SemNexus, App reactivation campaigns: timing](https://semnexus.com/app-reactivation-campaigns-timing-triggers-channel-mix)
- Countly (games): start re-engagement after 3–7 days of inactivity for casual games, 7–14 days for deeper session-based games. [vendor] — [Countly, push for lapsed players](https://countly.com/blog/how-to-use-push-notifications-to-bring-lapsed-players-back-to-your-game)
- CleverTap's built-in "Get back non-returning users" campaign uses **Day 20** as its example default threshold (configurable); its recommendations docs use a 14-day-inactive segment as the example. No outcome data. [policy/docs, vendor] — [CleverTap docs, Re-engage inactive users](https://docs.clevertap.com/v4.0/docs/re-engage-inactive-users); [CleverTap docs, Personalize engagements using recommendations](https://docs.clevertap.com/docs/personalize-engagements-using-recommendations)
- MoEngage case studies: Publishers Clearing House reactivated "over 30,000 dormant" app users at a 3.93% overall conversion rate (prediction-targeted campaigns); Airtel Wynk Music page claims a 44% win-back rate of dormant users (page ~3.5 years old, definition unclear). [vendor] — [MoEngage, PCH case study](https://www.moengage.com/casestudy/publishers-clearing-house-re-engages-dormant-customers-with-moengage-predictions/); [MoEngage, Reactivate](https://www.moengage.com/customer-lifecycle/reactivate/)
- Email analog (not push): a secondary blog attributes to Klaviyo's 2025 benchmark that one win-back email reactivates ~6% while a 4-email sequence reaches 14–18%; generic win-back programs often cited at 3–12%. [weak vendor/blog, secondhand] — [US Tech Automations blog](https://ustechautomations.com/resources/blog/ecommerce-win-back-campaign-automation); [Rework, Win-back campaigns](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/ecommerce-growth/win-back-campaigns)
- Lifecycle Architect (running apps): when lapsed users are lumped into one segment, reactivation is ~2–3% vs 8–12% when segmented; suggests measuring reactivation as a logged run within 14 days of contact. [weak vendor/blog] — [Lifecycle Architect](https://lifecyclearchitect.com/guides/win-back-campaigns-for-running-apps/)

**Reminders and retention in wellness/self-tracking apps (closest analogs)**
- Calm (2016, via Amplitude): users who set Daily Reminders had "almost 3x" retention (correlational at first); a prompt shown after the first meditation led 40% of prompted users to set a reminder, and those users got an equivalent retention boost, still visible "12+ weeks out." **Older than 2017 and vendor-published.** [vendor, 2016] — [Amplitude, How Calm increased retention 3X](https://amplitude.com/blog/calm-increased-retention-amplitude)
- Drink Less MRT (n=349 in the MRT arm, first 30 days after download, 8 PM randomized notification): probability of opening the app in the next hour was 3.6% with no notification vs 13.1% (standard message) and 12.2% (new message) — ~3.5-fold (RR 3.52, 95% CI 2.92–4.26). But median time to disengagement was 11 days (standard daily 11 AM reminder arm), 11 days (MRT arm) vs 7 days (no-notification arm), log-rank P=.42 — not significant (trial underpowered: 598 randomized vs 2,000 target). Authors: fixed/random reminder policies did not improve long-term engagement; recommend dynamic policies and "fewer notifications." [peer-reviewed, 2023] — [Bell et al. 2023, PMC10337295](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10337295/)
- Bidargaddi et al. MRT (1,255 users of a workplace well-being app, 89 days): sending a tailored push increased the chance of self-monitoring within 24h by 3.9% (RR 1.039, 95% CI 1.01–1.08); 8.7% on weekends vs 2.5% weekdays (difference n.s., P=.18); best slot 12:30 on weekends (+11.8%). Effect attenuated over the study but not significantly (P=.84). [peer-reviewed, 2018] — [Bidargaddi et al., JMIR mHealth uHealth 2018;6(11):e10123](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6293241/)

**Named consumer apps (Headspace, Fabulous, Finch, Daylio, Iterable/Braze/Airship/OneSignal case studies)**
- No published win-back timing or back-off numbers were found for Headspace, Fabulous, Finch or Daylio, and no Braze/Airship/OneSignal/Iterable report with day-7/14/30 reactivation rates was retrievable in this session (see Gaps). Linkrunner notes push-driven returns are often mis-attributed as "organic" in dashboards, which inflates/deflates vendor-reported win-back figures. [vendor] — [Linkrunner, Attribution for re-engagement campaigns](https://linkrunner.io/blog/attribution-for-re-engagement-campaigns-tracking-dormant-user-reactivation)

### Inferences
- The consistent shape across all (weak) sources is steep decay: if a day-7 message gets X, day-14 gets roughly X/2 and day-30 roughly X/3 to X/4. For a daily app with most churn after day 1, the window where a message still has leverage is the first ~1–2 weeks after the last open; a "final" message at ~day 10 after last open (3 daily reminders + ~7 days silence) sits inside that window.
- Cap of ~3 win-back messages per lapse episode is the recurring vendor recommendation; the owner's design (3 daily reminders + 1 final) is 4 total touches per lapse, within the range vendors treat as safe, and much gentler than Duolingo (7 daily reminders + a stop notice, see §2).
- Evidence from self-tracking apps (Drink Less, Bidargaddi) says daily reminders reliably cause *same-day/next-hour opens* (3.5x in Drink Less) but do not by themselves move *long-run* disengagement much. So reminders are worth having, but they will not fix a D1→D7 cliff alone; Duolingo's own finding that keeping current users active (CURR) dwarfs win-back reinforces that the reminder opt-in moment and the first week matter more than the day-30 message.
- Calm's (old, vendor) result suggests that *getting users to choose a reminder time* early (after first meaningful action) is a bigger lever than any win-back copy; with local notifications this is the only way to reach users at all.
- With ~200 accounts, no A/B test on timing will be statistically meaningful (Drink Less with 598 users was underpowered for retention outcomes); choices must come from priors like these, not from in-app experiments.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or first-party study comparing win-back at day 3 vs 7 vs 14 vs 30 for any consumer app was found; all day-specific numbers are vendor blogs without methodology.
- Braze, Airship, OneSignal, Iterable, CleverTap benchmark reports with lapse-day reactivation rates were not found/retrievable (several aggregator pages returned 403). A secondhand "Localytics: 65% of users return within 30 days with push enabled vs 20% without" figure appeared in search snippets but its primary report could not be verified.
- No public data from Headspace, Fabulous, Finch or Daylio on how many reminders they send to lapsed users or when they stop.

## 2. Duolingo's approach: stopping ignored reminders, the "sleeping, recovering bandit" (KDD 2020), published numbers

### Takeaway
Duolingo sends one practice reminder per day; if a user receives reminders for 7 consecutive days without opening the app, it stops them and tells the user ("Hey, these reminders don't seem to be working. We'll stop sending them for now."), which the CEO says brought people back (no numbers published). Its KDD 2020 bandit treats template freshness as a recovering resource (recency penalty with a 15-day half-life); the system lifted DAU +0.5% and new-user D7 retention +2.0%, and showed that reusing the same template every day costs ~0.5% of conversions.

### Cited Findings
**The "we'll stop sending them" rule**
- LinkedIn Talent Blog (Laura Hilgers, 2024-01-30) quoting CEO Luis von Ahn: Duolingo normally sends a daily reminder; after **seven straight days** of reminders without the user opening the app, it pauses them. Von Ahn: "Now, at some point, it occurred to us that if we're stopping sending people notifications, we should let them know." Message: "Hey, these reminders don't seem to be working. We'll stop sending them for now." Reported effect: users felt the owl had given up on them and came back. No figures. [anecdote / executive quote] — [LinkedIn, 4 Tips on How to Make Learning Addictive from Duolingo's CEO](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/learning-and-development/tips-on-how-to-make-learning-addictive)
- Salesflare's analysis describes this as one of Duolingo's most famous copy experiments (secondary). [blog] — [Salesflare, Duolingo: how they nailed their product](https://blog.salesflare.com/duolingo-iconic-product-e3df449017df)
- No official Duolingo help-center page describing the rule was found; exact current threshold (7 days) rests on the CEO interview.

**"Protect the channel"**
- Mazal (ex-CPO): the core notification rule was "protect the channel"; the team could optimize timing, templates, images, copy, localization, but could not increase notification quantity "without strong justification and CEO approval." Cautionary tale: Groupon tested from 1 to 5 emails/day and "the accumulation of Groupon's aggressive email tests had basically destroyed their channel." Late-night streak-saver notifications were the first big streak win. [company research] — [Lenny's Newsletter, 2023-02-28](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)

**KDD 2020 paper: Yancey & Settles, "A Sleeping, Recovering Bandit Algorithm for Optimizing Recurring Notifications" (KDD '20, Aug 23–27 2020, DOI 10.1145/3394486.3403351)** [peer-reviewed, company authors] — [PDF](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)
- Problem: (1) "'Fresh' templates (which have not been recently seen) tend to have higher impact on user behavior"; without variation the bandit "will converge to using the same template for a given user day after day, which will get repetitive, desensitize users, and fail to improve engagement in the long run." (2) "sleeping" arms: some templates only eligible in some conditions (e.g., streak wager).
- Novelty evidence: "Prior A/B tests at Duolingo that introduced new templates tended to boost notification conversion rates, even when those new templates did not outperform existing templates in the long run."
- Recency matters more than count: "reminders sent months or even years before should have little impact on their effectiveness today, regardless of their quantity."
- Recovery model: score penalised by a base recency penalty γ decaying with days since the template was last shown to that user; "Based upon previous A/B tests at Duolingo, we guessed that the recency penalty should mostly decay within the span of a few weeks to a month, and so we chose a **half-life of 15 days**." Grid search put γ around 0.017 (later production tests up to 3.4 showed no significant business-metric change).
- Reward = lesson completed **within 2 hours** of the reminder. Baseline (random template) reward ≈ 0.1295, i.e., ~13% of reminders convert within 2h (population = users still receiving reminders).
- Offline: template+UI-language arms +1.8%; with recency penalty +1.9%; "reusing the same template ... hurts reward by 0.5%." Data: 34 days of logs (15 train / 19 test; 88M / 114M rounds).
- Online A/B (production, millions of daily reminders): DAU **+0.5%**, total lessons **+0.4%**, existing-user D1 retention +0.5%, existing-user D7 +0.2% (n.s.), **new-user D1 retention +2.2%, new-user D7 retention +2.0%**. The 95% of rounds using the bandit had 2.5% higher reward than a 5% uniform-random holdout. Authors note new users "may be more sensitive" to notification content.
- Duolingo blog companion (Yancey & Settles, 2020-09-03): ~200 million practice reminders over 34 days; "brand-new notifications are especially persuasive, but the effect wears off"; repetition spacing is handled like the forgetting curve; helped "tens of thousands of new learners return." [company research] — [Duolingo blog, "Hi, it's Duo": the AI behind the meme](https://blog.duolingo.com/hi-its-duo-the-ai-behind-the-meme/)
- Replication dataset of 200M reminders over 35 days was released (template, 2-hour conversion). — [Papers with Code entry](https://paperswithcode.com/paper/a-sleeping-recovering-bandit-algorithm-for)

### Inferences
- Duolingo's stop rule (7 ignored days → explicit stop notice → silence) is the canonical precedent for the owner's design; the owner's 3-ignored threshold is more conservative, which fits a cozy, non-gamified tone and a much weaker "streak" pull. There is no public evidence on whether 3, 5 or 7 is optimal.
- Duolingo's stop notice *is itself* the "one last message" — sent at the moment of stopping, not 7 days later. The owner's variant (stop silently after 3, then one "no rush" message ~7 days later) adds a rest period before the last touch, which is consistent with the habituation-recovery logic in §3 (a pause restores responsiveness; a novel message dishabituates), but untested.
- The 15-day template half-life implies that a final message should use copy the user has never seen (or not seen in weeks), not a re-send of the daily reminder text; repetition of the same text measurably lowers conversion even at Duolingo's scale.
- Note the magnitude: even world-class optimization of reminder *content* moved DAU by only 0.5% and new-user retention by ~2%; content/timing tweaks are second-order compared with whether a reminder exists and whether the user opted in.

### Gaps
- Duolingo has not published the reactivation rate produced by the "these reminders don't seem to be working" message, nor whether it ever resumes reminders after the stop (anecdotal reports exist of re-engagement messages and emails continuing, unverified).
- The paper does not report how conversion of reminders decays with *days since last app use* (only with days since a template was last seen).

## 3. Habituation / alert fatigue, recovery after a pause, ignored notifications as a signal, back-off rules

### Takeaway
Repeated prompts habituate: in HeartSteps the effect of activity suggestions fell ~2–3% per day and was undetectable after ~4 weeks; Twitter and Duolingo report desensitization to repeated/high-volume pushes. Habituation theory predicts partial "spontaneous recovery" when the stimulus is withheld and "dishabituation" by a novel stimulus, which supports "pause, then one different message." Large platforms (Meta, Pinterest, Twitter) found that sending fewer notifications to users who don't respond costs little or nothing short-term and can increase long-term usage.

### Cited Findings
**Habituation in field trials**
- HeartSteps MRT (44 recruited/37 analyzed, 6 weeks, ~2.4 suggestions/day): walking suggestions raised 30-min step count overall by 24% (+59 steps); on **day 1 +107% (+271 steps)**, declining ~3%/day, **undetectable by day 29**; all suggestions: day 1 +66% (+167 steps), declining ~2%/day, null by day 28. Authors attribute decline "most likely" to habituation; exit interviews: suggestions became boring after 2–4 weeks. [peer-reviewed; Ann Behav Med, online 2018/print 2019] — [Klasnja et al., PMC6401341](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6401341)
- Drink Less: receiving a notification the previous day reduced the standard message's effect by ~11% (RR 0.889, n.s.), while rotating new messages showed no such reduction (RR 1.013, n.s.); no significant decay over 30 days overall (0.993/day). [peer-reviewed, 2023] — [Bell et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10337295/)
- Bidargaddi: attenuation over 89 days present but not significant (P=.84). [peer-reviewed, 2018] — [PMC6293241](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6293241/)
- Twitter (2022): users may respond to high push caps at first but "become desensitized over time"; long-term labels collected after a two-month delay; HMM-based per-cohort/per-user push caps gave DAU +0.62% (iOS) / +0.39% (Android) with insignificant reachability loss (reachability = users keeping notifications on). [company research] — [Twitter Eng blog 2022, via Eugene Yan summary](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/push/); [Twitter Engineering blog](https://blog.twitter.com/engineering/en_us/topics/insights/2022/a-hybrid-approach-to-personalize-notification-volume)

**Recovery after a pause / novelty (theory)**
- Revised habituation criteria (Rankin et al. 2009, Neurobiol Learn Mem 92(2):135–138): "If the stimulus is withheld after response decrement, the response recovers at least partially over the observation time" (spontaneous recovery); recovery is faster after high-frequency than low-frequency stimulation; a different, strong stimulus restores the response (dishabituation), but repeated dishabituating stimuli lose effect ("habituation of dishabituation"). **Basic-science source from 2009 (older than requested window), not about phones.** [peer-reviewed] — [Rankin et al. 2009, PMC2754195](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2754195)
- Duolingo operationalizes recovery for content: template effectiveness recovers with a ~15-day half-life after last exposure (see §2). [peer-reviewed] — [Yancey & Settles 2020](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)

**Volume reduction / suppression at scale**
- Meta (Analytics at Meta, "Notifications: why less is more", Dec 2022): in a long-running (~1 year+) experiment sending fewer notifications, there was an initial drop in visits that gradually recovered and turned into a long-term gain in usage and satisfaction. Exact numbers not retrievable (Medium page 403). [company research] — [Analytics at Meta, Medium](https://medium.com/@AnalyticsAtMeta/notifications-why-less-is-more-how-facebook-has-been-increasing-both-user-satisfaction-and-app-9463f7325e7d); summary repost [exde601e blog](https://exde601e.blogspot.com/2023/01/notifications-why-less-is-more.html)
- Pinterest (KDD 2018, notification volume optimization): randomized weekly caps to learn unsubscribe risk; the volume model cut volume by 6–24% while CTR rose 11–31% and site engagement +1–3%; core users received fewer emails with no significant DAU/WAU drop; **marginal users (active 1–3 of past 28 days) received more emails, which raised their clicks, DAU and WAU**. [peer-reviewed, company authors] — [Eugene Yan summary, 2023-12-24](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/push/); [ACM DL 10.1145/3219819.3219906](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3219819.3219906)
- LinkedIn (KDD 2018): filters low-signal, high-volume notifications via a volume-optimization system. [peer-reviewed, company authors] — [ACM DL 10.1145/3219819.3219880](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3219819.3219880)

**Ignored notifications as a signal (baseline behaviour)**
- Pielot, Vradi & Park (MobileHCI 2018; 794,525 notifications from 278 users, data from summer 2016): median 56 notifications/day per person; non-messaging notifications "rarely lead to a conversion (rates between ca. 15 and 25%)"; NonSocial notifications "tend to be dismissed quickly." **Data from 2016.** [peer-reviewed] — [Pielot et al. 2018 PDF](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Pielot-MobileHCI18.pdf)
- Android: dismissing a notification does **not** count as app usage for hibernation purposes ("If the user dismisses the notification and doesn't otherwise interact with the notification, this doesn't count as app usage"). [policy/docs] — [Android Developers, App hibernation](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/app-hibernation)
- Android (Pixel) has shown a system prompt asking whether the user wants to "keep seeing these notifications?" when notifications are repeatedly swiped away (forum report, Pixel 3, Nov 2018). [anecdote] — [Android Central forum](https://forums.androidcentral.com/threads/disable-the-do-you-want-to-keep-seeing-these-notifications-prompt.927111)
- Review of digital triggers in health interventions lists "alert fatigue and habituation" as key pitfalls when tailoring frequency/intensity. [peer-reviewed, narrative review] — [PMC5466696](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5466696/)
- Systematic review of 17 studies on push frequency (consumer apps, search window ~2017–2020): usage rises with frequency, highly active users tolerate more, too-high frequency is perceived as disturbing; recommends letting users set frequency. [peer-reviewed review] — [Consumer acceptance of app push notifications: systematic review](https://openaccess.library.uitm.edu.my/Record/doaj-0ea595b5b22745e8b37d8b968e0a688f/Description)

**Back-off rules observed in practice**
- Duolingo: 1 reminder/day → after 7 consecutive ignored days, stop + explicit notice (§2). [anecdote/executive quote] — [LinkedIn 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/learning-and-development/tips-on-how-to-make-learning-addictive)
- Twitter: per-cohort caps from a 6-state HMM; move users between caps when predicted state changes. [company research] — [Eugene Yan summary](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/push/)
- Pinterest: per-user weekly budget; add notifications only while marginal value exceeds a threshold that includes unsubscribe risk. [peer-reviewed] — [Eugene Yan summary](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/push/)
- Drink Less authors recommend dynamic policies that respond to engagement and habituation state, possibly RL-based, and sending fewer notifications. [peer-reviewed] — [Bell et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10337295/)

### Inferences
- Daily identical reminders lose potency within 2–4 weeks even for engaged users (HeartSteps); for users who stop opening the app, each extra ignored reminder adds annoyance/opt-out risk with sharply diminishing benefit. "Stop after N ignored" is the simplest form of the adaptive caps Twitter/Pinterest/Meta use.
- "Silence, then one novel message" matches two well-established habituation properties (spontaneous recovery after withholding + dishabituation by novelty) and Duolingo's novelty finding, so a single different "no rush" message after ~7 days of silence has a mechanistic rationale. Habituation-of-dishabituation implies it should be *one* message (or very rare), not a recurring weekly nudge.
- Pinterest's result for marginal users (more messages helped them) is a caution against going *too* quiet too early for users who are only intermittently active: the stop rule should key on "reminders ignored with no app open at all", not on lower-than-usual activity. A user who opens the app every few days should keep getting the daily reminder.
- With a daily app, "ignored" can be defined purely as "no app open since the reminder fired" — measurable locally — which is also how Duolingo phrases its rule (days without opening the app).
- Rotating a small bank of reminder texts (Drink Less: rotated new messages showed no next-day decay vs −11% for the repeated standard text; Duolingo: same template every day −0.5%) is a cheap local-only anti-habituation measure.

### Gaps
- No study directly tests "stop after 3 vs 5 vs 7 ignored reminders" or measures the reactivation yield of a "final" message after a silence period.
- Meta's exact effect sizes and time-to-recovery were not retrievable.
- No official iOS behaviour that auto-demotes/suggests disabling an app's ignored notifications was confirmed; the Android "keep seeing these notifications?" prompt is documented only by user reports (2018).

## 4. Risks: opt-out/uninstall spikes, guilt-tripping copy, platform policy limits

### Takeaway
Too many messages is the #1 stated reason consumers opt out of app notifications (51% globally, Airship/Sapio 2021), so every ignored reminder spends from a finite "channel" (Duolingo's "protect the channel" rule). Guilt/"I miss you" copy is classified in HCI research as a social dark pattern and is the root of the Duolingo-owl backlash memes; a gentle tone avoids this. Platform rules do not restrict non-promotional reminders the user opted into, but Apple 4.5.4 requires explicit opt-in + in-app opt-out for any promotional/marketing push, and Google Play limits system notifications to an app's "integral features."

### Cited Findings
**Opt-out / annoyance**
- Airship global survey (Sapio Research, Sept 2021, >9,000 consumers, 7 countries): top reason for opting out of brand smartphone messages = "receiving too many messages too often" (**51%**); second = information "not relevant/personalized" (**40%**); in the US only 34% cite frequency. [vendor survey] — [Airship press release, 2021-12-08](https://www.airship.com/newsroom/new-global-survey-finds-consumers-are-becoming-more-selective-about-where-when-and-how-they-share-information-with-brands/); [MarketingProfs chart 2022](https://marketingprofs.com/charts/2022/46372/why-people-opt-out-of-and-in-to-mobile-notifications-from-brands); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/369253/easy-to-say-yes-consumers-ignore-and-delete-the-e.html)
- Statista (June 2021, US smartphone users): 42% changed in-app settings when faced with too many push notifications (other reactions: turning off notifications, deleting the app). [survey, secondary] — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1242709/us-too-many-push-notifications-users-reaction)
- Pinterest explicitly models unsubscribe probability as a function of volume, and Twitter optimizes DAU against "reachability" loss (users disabling pushes), i.e., large platforms treat opt-out as the main cost of extra notifications. [peer-reviewed/company research] — [Eugene Yan summary](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/push/)
- Duolingo's "protect the channel" doctrine and Groupon's email-channel collapse after ramping 1→5 emails/day. [company research] — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)

**Guilt-tripping copy**
- Alberts, Lyngs & Van Kleek ("Computers as Bad Social Actors: Dark Patterns and Anti-Patterns in Interfaces that Act Socially", arXiv 2302.04720, 2023): describe interfaces that pressure users by eliciting guilt or empathy, using emotive language such as "I miss you", or appearing annoyed at users for "ignoring" them, as socially-acting dark patterns. [peer-reviewed/preprint] — [arXiv 2302.04720](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.04720v3)
- Confirmshaming (guilt framing) measurably shifts behaviour: in Strahilevitz-led experiments ~20% accepted a dubious offer under a confirmshaming condition vs <15% in control (secondary report). [secondary] — [Built In, Confirmshaming](https://builtin.com/design-ux/confirmshaming)

**Apple**
- App Store Review Guideline 4.5.4 (verbatim): "Push Notifications must not be required for the app to function, and should not be used to send sensitive personal or confidential information. Push Notifications should not be used for promotions or direct marketing purposes unless customers have explicitly opted in to receive them via consent language displayed in your app's UI, and you provide a method in your app for a user to opt out from receiving such messages. **Google Play**
- "Unauthorized Use or Imitation of System Functionality" policy: "System level notifications may only be used for an app's integral features"; "Use system notifications only for your app's integral features"; "Don't trigger notifications to promote a different app or a third-party service"; don't create notifications that look like OS alerts; "Clearly attribute notifications to your app"; example violation: a system notification used to serve an ad. [policy/docs] — [Google Play Console Help 9969861](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9969861?hl=en)

### Inferences
- A reminder to colour today's dot is an integral, user-requested feature, so neither Apple 4.5.4's marketing-consent clause nor Google's "integral features" rule is triggered — as long as the win-back/final message stays about the user's own grid. Adding a sale, Premium upsell or "new feature" pitch to the final message would turn it into promotional content (needs explicit consent language + in-app opt-out on iOS). Inference — Apple does not define the boundary precisely.
- Given 51% of opt-outs cite frequency, a user who has ignored 3 reminders is precisely the profile most likely to revoke notification permission; stopping early preserves the ability to reach them later (e.g., a seasonal/yearly-grid moment), which a revoked permission would destroy.
- Copy for the final message should avoid "we miss you", disappointment, streak-loss threats or anything implying the user is being watched/judged (classified as social dark patterns); "your grid is waiting, no rush" fits the recommended register. A Duolingo-style "these reminders don't seem to be working" line is effective for Duolingo partly because of its meme persona; in a cozy app it risks reading as passive-aggressive.

### Gaps
- No public data quantifies uninstall/opt-out spikes caused specifically by win-back messages (vs regular notification volume).
- No measured data on user reactions to Duolingo's stop notice or to guilt-framed reminders in a habit app.
- Apple's Human Interface Guidelines page on notifications could not be retrieved verbatim in this session.

## 5. What is feasible with local (on-device) notifications only vs server push

### Takeaway
The whole proposed policy (daily reminder → stop after 3 ignored days → one final message ~7 days later → silence) can be implemented locally with a "reschedule-on-open" pattern: on every app open, cancel all pending reminders and schedule the next 3 daily reminders plus one final message at last-open + ~10 days; if the user opens the app, everything resets. Platform constraints (iOS 64 pending-request limit, Android 13+ runtime permission, Android 14 exact-alarm default-denial, Android hibernation after "a few months") do not block this short horizon; server push would only add remote copy changes, per-message analytics and reach beyond the pre-scheduled horizon.

### Cited Findings
- iOS: an Apple engineer states a limit of **64** active/pending local notification requests per app, a system limit with no workaround (repeating triggers count as one request). [policy/docs, forum statement] — [Apple Developer Forums thread 811171](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/811171); [thread 682509](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/682509)
- Flutter `flutter_local_notifications` issue reports that scheduling >64 on iOS can result in none firing (behaviour inconsistent with docs that say the soonest are kept). [anecdote] — [GitHub issue 2312](https://www.github.com/MaikuB/flutter_local_notifications/issues/2312)
- Android app hibernation (target SDK 31+/Android 12+): if the user doesn't interact with the app "for a few months", runtime permissions are reset (incl. notification permission on 13+, as a runtime permission), the app "can't run jobs or alerts from the background" and can't receive FCM pushes; on leaving hibernation the system does **not** reschedule previously scheduled alarms/notifications. Scheduled alarms and jobs do not count as usage; dismissing a notification does not count as usage; tapping it does. [policy/docs] — [Android Developers, App hibernation](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/app-hibernation)
- Android 14: `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` is no longer pre-granted to most newly installed apps targeting Android 13+; `USE_EXACT_ALARM` is intended for calendar/alarm-clock apps; for reminders that don't need precise timing, use inexact alarms (`setWindow()`, minimum window 10 minutes for apps targeting 12+); denial also occurs after backup-restore to an Android 14 device. [policy/docs] — [Android 14 behavior change: schedule exact alarms](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms)
- Android 13+ requires the `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` runtime permission. [policy/docs] — [Android Developers, Notification runtime permission](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission)
- Drink Less itself used a *local* daily notification (11 AM, "Please complete your mood and drinks diary"), showing that the reminder mechanism studied in the trial is the same class as the one planned. [peer-reviewed] — [Bell et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10337295/)
- Server-side systems in the literature (Duolingo bandit, Twitter caps, Pinterest budgets) depend on server-logged per-notification outcomes (e.g., lesson within 2h) and daily batch retraining — capabilities that need server push or at least server analytics. [peer-reviewed] — [Yancey & Settles 2020](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf); [Eugene Yan summary](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/push/)

### Inferences
- Local implementation sketch (inference, not sourced practice): store `lastOpen` and today's logged state; on each app open/resume (and after the user logs today's dots, to suppress today's reminder) cancel all pending IDs and schedule: R1 = next day at the chosen time (or today if not yet logged and time not passed), R2 = +1 day, R3 = +2 day, and F = lastOpen + ~10 days ("no rush" copy, novel text). If R1–R3 fire with no open, the device naturally goes silent after R3 and only F remains; after F nothing is pending. Any open resets the cycle. This uses ≤4 pending requests (far below iOS's 64) and needs no knowledge of whether a notification was dismissed.
- Rotating copy (anti-habituation) can be pre-baked at scheduling time from a local bank, avoiding the same text two days in a row.
- What local-only cannot do: know that the user *saw/dismissed* a reminder (only that they did not open the app afterwards); change copy/timing without an app update; measure reactivation per message without client analytics on next open (a local "opened within X h of reminder F" event sent with normal sync could approximate it); reach users after Android hibernation (a few months) or after permission revocation; send anything when the app has been uninstalled.
- Use inexact scheduling on Android (window ≥10 min) to avoid exact-alarm permissions; time precision is irrelevant for a daily colouring reminder.
- Because day-10 is far inside Android's "few months" hibernation horizon, the final message will fire; a hypothetical day-30/day-60 message would also usually fire, but anything at multi-month horizons is unreliable on Android 12+.

### Gaps
- Not verified in this session: whether iOS `customDismissAction` callbacks can reliably record dismissals of local notifications in the background (would allow "ignored" = dismissed rather than "no open").
- Exact Android hibernation threshold ("a few months") is not specified numerically in the official docs.
- No source compares the effectiveness of locally pre-scheduled vs server-sent win-back messages.
