# Daily reminder notifications: frequency, fatigue, timing and wording (industry benchmarks and studies, as of Nov 2026)

Source-quality legend used below:
- **[VENDOR]**: data from a push or engagement vendor (Airship, CleverTap, Braze, Leanplum and others). It has a commercial bias toward "send more", and its samples are apps that already push a lot (selection bias). The figures are usually correlational.
- **[SURVEY]**: stated-intent consumer survey ("I would disable if..."), not observed behaviour.
- **[CAUSAL]**: randomized or quasi-experimental (A/B test, micro-randomized trial, natural experiment).
- **[DATED]**: data collected before 2019.
- **[SECONDARY]**: I only saw a secondary summary (search snippet, blog recap), not the primary document.

---

## Q1. What frequency thresholds lead users to disable notifications or uninstall?

### Takeaway
The often-quoted thresholds ("1 push a week leads 10% to disable and 6% to uninstall"; "2–5 a week leads 37–46% to disable") are stated-intent surveys from 2015–2017, mostly from Localytics, about generic brand and marketing pushes. Behavioural evidence from large platforms (Pinterest, Twitter) shows that cutting volume for users who are already active raises response without losing engagement. Surveys consistently rank "too frequent" together with "irrelevant" as the top two reasons people opt out. No reliable threshold exists for a user-requested daily reminder.

### Cited Findings

**Stated-intent surveys (Localytics/Upland, 2015–2017) [SURVEY][DATED]**
- In Localytics' survey with Research Now (reported January 2016, so fielded in 2015), **46%** of consumers said receiving **2–5 notifications in a week** would make them disable an app's push notifications, and **32%** said they would stop using the app altogether at **6–10 messages a week**. — [WAN-IFRA, Jan 2016](https://wan-ifra.org/2016/01/lets-say-goodbye-to-pointless-ill-timed-endless-push-notifications/) [SECONDARY]
- In the follow-up wave (about 1,000 US smartphone users, 2017), **37%** said they would disable notifications at **2–5/week** and **33%** at **6–10/week**. These look like a distribution of each person's tolerance threshold, not cumulative shares. — [MarketingProfs, 2018](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2018/33486/how-many-mobile-push-notifications-are-too-many) [SECONDARY]
- In a Localytics/Qualtrics poll of US app users (November 2017), **22%** said they would **stop using an app** after 2–5 pushes in a week, down from **31% in October 2015**. Tolerance appeared to be rising. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/app-users-are-more-open-to-push-notifications-than-they-were-two-years-ago); [eMarketer chart, Oct 2015–Nov 2017](https://www.emarketer.com/chart/215696/volume-of-push-notifications-that-prompt-us-smartphone-users-stop-using-app-oct-2015-nov-2017-of-respondents) [SECONDARY]
- The widely recycled line "**one push per week** leads **10%** to disable notifications and **6%** to uninstall" (sometimes qualified "if that push is irrelevant") appears in vendor and aggregator blogs, including a 2026 Business of Apps benchmark summary. A related variant says "13% would disable at even one weekly push". I could not find the primary report. It is most likely a recycled Localytics-era survey figure. — [MoEngage stats page](https://www.moengage.com/learn/push-notification-statistics/); [Helplama survey page](https://helplama.com/what-do-consumers-think-about-push-notifications/) [SECONDARY][SURVEY]

**Opt-out and uninstall reasons**
- CleverTap survey of **2,000+** app users (post dated 3 Jan 2019): **39%** uninstalled because they no longer used the app, **28%** because of "too many ads and notifications" (ads alone over 15%), **20%** for limited storage, and **11%** for technical issues or confusion. — [CleverTap blog](https://clevertap.com/blog/uninstall-apps/) [VENDOR][SURVEY][DATED]
- Another 2019 summary of a survey of more than 2,000 users gives: no longer use the app **39.9%**, limited storage **18.7%**, too much advertising **16.2%**, **excessive notifications 12.6%**. The attribution (CleverTap or GoodFirms) is unclear. Read alongside the line above, notifications alone seem to be roughly 10–13% of uninstall reasons, not the "71%" figure seen in some blogs, which has no traceable primary source. — [GoodFirms 2019](https://www.goodfirms.co/resources/app-download-usage-statistics-to-know) [SECONDARY]
- Airship's 2025 benchmark report states that a global consumer survey it fielded found the **top two reasons for opting out of brand communications were the same across seven countries**: messages were **too frequent** or **not relevant/personalized**. — [Airship 2025 Push Notification Benchmarks (PDF)](https://growth.airship.com/rs/313-QPJ-195/images/Airship-2025-Push-Notification-Benchmarks-EN.pdf) [VENDOR]
- Braze (2016), from a non-representative sample of 14 staff covering 584 app prompts: opt-out reasons were **30%** irrelevant or not personalized, **25%** too many pushes, **25%** default refusal, and **20%** no interest in the brand. The same article cites a 2015 Delvv survey in which **78%** said most pushes they receive are irrelevant. — [Braze article](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/opt-out-of-push-notifications-why-users-do-it) [VENDOR][DATED; tiny sample]

**Behavioural and causal evidence on volume (independent, large platforms)**
- **Pinterest** (Zhao et al., KDD 2018): an ML volume-control system that **cut notification volume by 6–24% raised CTR by 11–31% and site engagement metrics by 1–3%**. Volume moved away from core users, who got fewer messages with no significant DAU/WAU drop, toward marginal users, whose clicks, DAU and WAU rose. Unsubscribe effects were measured with a **4-week delay**. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3219819.3219906), summarized in [Eugene Yan, "Push notifications"](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/push/) [CAUSAL][SECONDARY summary]
- **Twitter** (2022): personalized per-user notification caps tuned by online A/B tests raised **DAU +0.62% on iOS and +0.39% on Android** with an **insignificant loss of "reachability"** (users keeping notifications on). Labels were collected after **two months** because users "may initially respond to high caps and then become desensitized". Cohort-level caps worked better for less-active users. — [Twitter Engineering blog, 2022](https://blog.twitter.com/engineering/en_us/topics/insights/2022/a-hybrid-approach-to-personalize-notification-volume); [arXiv 2202.08812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08812) [CAUSAL][SECONDARY summary via Eugene Yan]
- **Android** surfaces volume to users: "Users can see the number of daily notifications that your app sends, and they can revoke the permission at any time." — [Android Developers, notification permission](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission)
- **Apple HIG**: "Avoid sending multiple notifications for the same thing, even if someone hasn't responded… If you send multiple notifications for the same thing, you fill up Notification Center, and people may turn off all notifications from your app." — [Apple HIG, Notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications)

**What category apps actually send (Airship 2025 report, data Jan–Dec 2024) [VENDOR]**
- Methodology: apps with **≥1,000 active users** that sent **≥1,000 pushes in a month**; more than **9 billion app users**, thousands of apps, 13 verticals; monthly metrics averaged over 12 months. — [Airship 2025 benchmarks PDF](https://growth.airship.com/rs/313-QPJ-195/images/Airship-2025-Push-Notification-Benchmarks-EN.pdf)
- **90th-percentile** average monthly pushes per user: **Medical, Health & Fitness 9.3 (iOS) / 12.6 (Android)**; Utility & Productivity 38.7 (iOS) / 25.2 (Android); Media about 293 on both platforms. Even top-sending health and fitness apps average under one push per user per day across their audience. — same PDF (values read from chart labels; the label-to-vertical mapping was checked against the rendered iOS open-rate chart, which uses the same layout).
- The same report says the type of push ("promotional, transactional, educational or engagement-based reminders") "dramatically impacts the volume". It also says high-sending apps (90th percentile of sends per user) see "a 72% average increase in sessions per active user" versus their category average, which is correlational. — same PDF

### Inferences
- The 2–5/week "disable" thresholds describe unsolicited marketing pushes from brands, measured as intent. A reminder the user turned on and timed themselves is closer to a utility notification (Airship's 2019 report notes Utility & Productivity users "expect these apps to help them accomplish tasks… reminders, alerts"). One reminder a day is unlikely to be read as spam if it is clearly opt-in, easy to change or switch off in-app, and skipped when there is nothing left to do.
- The planned "only if some trackers are still empty" rule is in effect the Pinterest finding applied to a small app: the most engaged users, who fill their trackers before the reminder time, automatically get **fewer** notifications. That is the group the platform studies say gains nothing from more pushes.
- Twitter's "desensitization after initial response" and the decays in Q2 (HeartSteps, Bidargaddi, Duolingo novelty) suggest a back-off rule for long silences. For example, after N consecutive ignored reminders, drop to every other day or offer a one-tap "pause". This is a design inference, not a measured threshold.
- Because Apple's HIG warns against repeated notifications "for the same thing", one grouped notification is safer than one per tracker. Never send a follow-up "you still haven't…" nudge the same evening.

### Gaps
- I could not reach the primary Localytics/Upland survey PDFs; all Localytics numbers come via secondary reports. The "1/week leads 10% to disable, 6% to uninstall" figure has no verifiable primary source.
- I found no YouGov or other independent 2019–2026 survey isolating notification frequency as an uninstall trigger.
- I found no measured threshold for **self-scheduled daily reminders** specifically: all frequency/fatigue data concerns publisher-initiated pushes.
- MoEngage, OneSignal and Batch frequency reports were not retrieved, so their methodology is unverified.

## Q2. Retention lift for users who receive notifications vs not (and caveats)

### Takeaway
Vendor data shows large correlational multipliers. Airship found "nearly 3x" 90-day retention for new users who received at least one push, and 820% higher for daily-or-more recipients. These come from 2016 cohorts and are heavily confounded, since apps push engaged users more and users who opt in are more motivated. Causal studies show **small but real** effects: low-single-digit percentage lifts in daily activity or engagement, larger for new users. Removing daily reminders from a diary app reduced entries by about 11% and raised attrition by about 32% (relative).

### Cited Findings

**Vendor correlational data [VENDOR]**
- Airship retention benchmark: **63 million** new users across **1,500 apps** (apps with ≥5,000 downloads and ≥1,000 pushes/month). The cohort **first opened an app in Sept 2016** and was tracked **through Dec 2016**; the report is © 2019. Retention means at least one open in the 90 days after the first open, and churn includes uninstalls and "soft churn". — [Airship, "How Push Notifications Impact Mobile App Retention Rates" (PDF)](https://growth.airship.com/rs/313-QPJ-195/images/airship-how-push-notifications-impact-mobile-app-retention-rates.pdf) [DATED]
  - Users who received **any push in their first 90 days** had "nearly **3x** (190%) higher retention" than those who received none.
  - Versus zero pushes: **one push +120%**, **weekly +440%**, **Daily+ +820%** retention. By platform: weekly gives **6x on Android and 2x on iOS**; Daily+ gives **10x on Android and 3x on iOS**.
  - **95%** of opt-in users who received no push in 90 days churned. **27%** of opt-in users never received a push. **54%** of users churn within 90 days "even at optimal notification frequency levels".
  - **Utility & Productivity** (6.96M users, 167 apps): highest opt-in (**87% Android / 57% iOS**, 81% overall). Opt-in users were retained **49% longer** than opt-out users, the highest of any vertical. **33%** received zero pushes.
  - **Selection-bias warning from the same report**: in Retail, **opt-out users had better 90-day retention than opt-in users who received no messages** (+50% Android, +36% iOS). Opt-in status alone does not explain the gap; the messaging, and confounding with engagement, do.
- Airship's **2023** benchmark is cited in secondary summaries as finding about **one-third higher** retention for users who received at least one push in their first 90 days. That is far smaller than the 2016 "3x". — [Mobiloud summary](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/push-notification-statistics/) [SECONDARY, not verified; page returned 403]
- Airship 2025 report: "Customers who are opted-in to push notifications have **13% more purchases** compared to opted-out customers". Apps running onboarding campaigns see opt-in rates "as high as **40%** above their category average", and in-app messages drive a "**14%** average increase in opt-in rates among opted-out users". — [Airship 2025 benchmarks PDF](https://growth.airship.com/rs/313-QPJ-195/images/Airship-2025-Push-Notification-Benchmarks-EN.pdf)

**Opt-in and engagement benchmarks by vertical (who you can reach) [VENDOR]**
- Overall **median opt-in, 2023 → 2024**: **Android 71.3% → 59.5%** (falling after the Android 13 runtime permission), **iOS 49.1% → 49.4%** (stable). 90th percentile: Android 88.0% → 79.7%, iOS 73.9% → 74.1%. — [Airship 2025 benchmarks PDF](https://growth.airship.com/rs/313-QPJ-195/images/Airship-2025-Push-Notification-Benchmarks-EN.pdf) (read from chart labels)
- **Medical, Health & Fitness** opt-in (2024 data, my visual estimates from bar charts without labels): iOS about **71% high / 50% median / 23% low**; Android about **72% / 51% / 22%**. **Utility & Productivity**: iOS about **81% / 65% / 35%**, Android about **87% / 71% / 42%**. — same PDF (approximate, read visually)
- **Direct open rate, 90th percentile** (labelled values): **Medical, Health & Fitness 11.3% iOS / 15.7% Android**; Utility & Productivity 11.3% iOS / 19% Android. The Health & Fitness iOS median is about **3.3%** (visual estimate). — same PDF
- Airship's 2026 edition (2025 data) is reported secondhand as giving medians of **53.3% Android / 48.85% iOS**, and for Medical, Health & Fitness **54.98% iPhone / 58.48% Android** opt-in, with **4.25% iPhone / 6.6% Android** median direct open rate. — [Mobiloud, opt-in benchmarks](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/push-notification-opt-in-rate/) [SECONDARY, not verified]

**Causal evidence [CAUSAL]**
- **Duolingo** (Yancey & Settles, KDD 2020): reward = lesson completed **within 2 hours** of the daily practice reminder. Choosing templates with a "sleeping, recovering" bandit, against a "strong baseline" already optimized "through years of A/B tests", gave **+0.5% DAU, +0.4% lessons, and +2% new-user recurring retention** after two weeks. The authors note new users "may be more sensitive… while their daily interaction with the app is still establishing habitual routines." — [Duolingo research PDF](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)
- **Bidargaddi et al. 2018** (JMIR mHealth uHealth 6(11):e10123): a micro-randomized trial with **1,255 users** of a workplace well-being self-monitoring app over **89 days**. Sending a tailored push made users **3.9% more likely** to self-monitor in the app within 24 hours (RR 1.039, 95% CI 1.01–1.08). The **effect faded over the study** (trend not significant). — [JMIR article](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2018/11/e10123/); abstract via [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30497999)
- **HeartSteps** (Klasnja et al., Ann Behav Med 2019), a micro-randomized trial of activity suggestions: averaged over study days, a suggestion raised the 30-minute step count by **14% (35 steps, p=.06)**. The effect was largest at the start of the study and **diminished over time**. — [PubMed 30192907](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30192907/); [PMC6401341](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6401341)
- **DMKG headache-diary app** (Ruscheweyh et al., JMIR mHealth uHealth 2026;14:e73259, published Sept 2026): a natural experiment in which **daily reminders failed for 14 days** in early 2023, with **4,236** active users. During the failure, daily **entries fell 11.0%** (10.2 → 9.1 per 14 days), **entry delay rose 46.3%** (1.90 → 2.78 days) and **attrition rose 32.2%** (4.0% → 5.2%). All recovered after the reminders were restored. The largest effects were among users with **high prior adherence**. The design is observational. — [DOI 10.2196/73259](https://doi.org/10.2196/73259) (abstract via Europe PMC). A search snippet quoted a "5.0%" decline; the Europe PMC abstract says 11.0%, which I use here.
- **Counterpoint**, mood monitoring meta-analysis (Astill Wright et al., JMIR Mental Health 2026;13:e83765): **77 studies, 17,123 participants** with depression or bipolar disorder. Mood-monitoring **reminders were associated with higher attrition and lower adherence**, but the authors say this "may be confounded by longer follow-up in those studies". Heterogeneity was very high and there was publication bias. — [DOI 10.2196/83765](https://doi.org/10.2196/83765)

### Inferences
- For dian-dian (about 200 accounts, 19% D7 retention), a realistic expectation from the causal literature is a **modest** lift. That means a few percentage points more days logged and somewhat lower early churn, not a 3x. The DMKG natural experiment is the closest analogue (a daily self-logging diary). There, reminders accounted for about **10% of entries** and a **third of short-term attrition**.
- New users benefit most (Duolingo +2% new-user retention against +0.5% overall DAU). This supports asking for the opt-in early in the first week and making the reminder part of onboarding, rather than hiding it in settings.
- Effects decay over time (Bidargaddi, HeartSteps, Duolingo's novelty effect), so rotating the wording is worth building in from day one.
- The Airship retail caveat (opt-out users outperforming opt-in users who never receive messages) is a reminder that **asking for permission and then sending nothing** gives no benefit.

### Gaps
- No primary access to the Airship 2023 or 2026 reports, so the "one-third higher" figure and the 2025 vertical medians are unverified.
- No causal study specific to "year in pixels" or mood-tracker consumer apps outside clinical populations.
- No independent replication of the "3x retention" claim. All such multipliers are vendor-correlational.

## Q3. Best time of day for daily-habit reminders: user-chosen vs fixed vs learned; evening vs morning

### Takeaway
There is no strong evidence for a universal "best hour". The only randomized timing data (Bidargaddi 2018) found the largest effect at 12:30 on weekends, but the differences between time slots were not significant. Vendor "best time" posts for health and fitness give conflicting windows (2–5 pm and 8–9 pm, or 6–8 am and 5–7 pm). Logging at a consistent time is associated with higher adherence, and evening-consistent trackers do well. Habit research suggests reminders drive repetition but not automaticity, and tying the log to an existing routine works better. That argues for a user-chosen time anchored to a routine (e.g. "after dinner" or "before bed"), with an evening default.

### Cited Findings
- **Bidargaddi et al. 2018** (randomized slots **8:30, 12:30, 17:30, 18:30, 19:30, 20:30**): the effect on next-24h self-monitoring was larger on **weekends (about 8.7%)** than weekdays (**about 2.5%**). The largest effect was at **12:30 on weekends (+11.8%, 90% CI 1.02–1.13)**, but the weekday/weekend difference was **not statistically significant (P=.18)**. — [JMIR mHealth uHealth 2018;6(11):e10123](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2018/11/e10123/); [Europe PMC abstract](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/30497999) [CAUSAL]
- **CleverTap** (vendor blog, methodology not stated) lists **2–5 pm and 8–9 pm** as "prime hours" for health and fitness. Other blogs suggest **6–8 am and 5–7 pm**, or a morning workout nudge plus an **evening 7–9 pm "log your progress"** reminder. — [CleverTap, best time to send push](https://clevertap.com/blog/best-time-to-send-push-notifications/); [Sahha blog](https://sahha.ai/blog/health-app-push-notifications-timing/) [VENDOR/blog; SECONDARY]
- **Consistency of logging time**: a Mount Sinai (Icahn) research-lab blog reports that participants who checked in at a **regular time** completed **92%** of daily check-ins versus **63%** for those with variable timing. People who consistently tracked **in the evening** had **88–90% adherence**. This is not peer-reviewed and does not show that reminders caused the difference. — [Ensari Lab, Icahn School of Medicine](https://labs.icahn.mssm.edu/ensarilab/one-small-habit-big-data-impact/) [SECONDARY; site unreachable for direct fetch]
- **Habit formation** (Stawarz, Cox & Blandford, CHI 2015): in a 4-week study, which also reviewed **115 habit apps**, **reminders supported repetition but hindered habit development (automaticity)**. **Event-based cues** (implementation intentions such as "after X, I do Y") increased automaticity, and positive reinforcement had no effect. Limitations: an artificial habit and only 4 weeks. — [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1468224/) [CAUSAL, small study]
- **Learned timing at scale (Duolingo)**: the KDD 2020 paper optimizes **which template** to send each day. It does not describe send-time logic. Third-party guides describe a "Smart Scheduling" option in Duolingo's notification settings alongside a user-chosen reminder time. — [Duolingo KDD 2020](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf); [Lingoly guide](https://lingoly.io/turn-on-duolingo-practice-reminder/) [SECONDARY]
- **Diary reminders reduce recall delay**: in the DMKG natural experiment, entry delay went from **1.90 to 2.78 days** without reminders, i.e. reminders cut retrospective back-filling. — [DOI 10.2196/73259](https://doi.org/10.2196/73259)
- **iOS delivery controls that affect evening sends**: "Delivery scheduling lets people choose whether to receive notification alerts immediately or in a summary that's delivered at times they choose", and a Focus (e.g. sleeping) can delay alerts. "Even though a Focus might delay the delivery of a notification alert, the notification itself is available as soon as it arrives." — [Apple HIG, Managing notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-notifications)

### Inferences
- A **21:00 default** is defensible for "end-of-day" trackers (mood, habits done today). It matches vendor evening windows (8–9 pm) and the consistent-evening-logging adherence data. **Letting users choose the time** is better supported than any fixed hour: the gains come from a consistent time and from anchoring to a routine (Stawarz). Onboarding copy could frame the choice as a routine, e.g. "When do you wind down?".
- Some trackers are naturally filled at other times (e.g. **sleep** is known in the morning). An evening reminder that lists "Sleep" as empty may feel odd. Per-tracker inclusion, or a note that sleep can be logged in the morning, avoids that.
- Avoid defaults late enough to run into a Sleep Focus or bedtime scheduled summary (often set around 22:00–23:00; I have no data on the distribution of user settings). 20:00–21:00 is safer than 22:00+.
- A light "learned time" is cheap to build locally, e.g. defaulting the reminder to about 30 minutes after the user's median logging time over the last week. The evidence for learned send times in habit apps is mostly vendor-asserted; I found no published effect size.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study comparing **morning vs evening** reminders specifically for mood or daily journaling in consumer apps.
- No published A/B of **user-chosen vs fixed vs learned** reminder time for a habit app (Duolingo's paper covers content selection, not timing).
- The often-quoted Duolingo "reminder about 23.5 hours after your last lesson" could not be verified from any official source.
- No vendor send-time dataset with stated methodology (Batch, OneSignal, MoEngage) was retrieved.

## Q4. Wording: personalization, naming the pending item, tone, emoji, length, grouping

### Takeaway
Specific, personalized content outperforms generic content in every source that measured it. Airship claims +37% opens from "tailored messages", and Duolingo's working templates name the user's streak and language. On iOS about 4 lines are visible on the lock screen, but on Android the collapsed view is only about one line (about 40–50 characters), so the essential words must come first. Emoji lifts (+85% opens in a 2016-data Leanplum study) are dated and vendor-sourced. I found no controlled data on guilt or loss framing against positive framing for reminders.

### Cited Findings
- **Personalization**: "delivering tailored messages is the single most important driver… to boost notification open rates — resulting in a **37% increase on average**". Segmentation lifts direct open rates **18–65%** ("as high as 334%"), and rich push gives **+22%** average direct-open lift. — [Airship 2025 benchmarks PDF](https://growth.airship.com/rs/313-QPJ-195/images/Airship-2025-Push-Notification-Benchmarks-EN.pdf) [VENDOR; methodology not given]
- **Naming the specific item** (Duolingo production templates): "You're on fire — Continue your **109 day Spanish streak** on Duolingo." (eligible only with a 3+ day streak); "You're on day 2 of your 7-day streak wager! Now get to day 3!"; "Ready for your trip? Take 5 minutes to practice Italian now". Templates "may contain slots for user-specific information". — [Duolingo KDD 2020](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)
- **Novelty and rotation**: Duolingo found new templates beat old ones in A/B tests but "did not outperform existing templates in the long run", attributed to a "**novelty effect**". A **recency penalty** with a **15-day half-life** improved results. Treating each template × UI language as a separate arm gave **+1.8%** vs **+1.2%** reward, i.e. the **translation of a template changes its performance**. Per-language scores for the same template ranged from about −1.8% to +3.0%. — [Duolingo KDD 2020](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)
- **Generic vs personalized content in a self-monitoring app**: generic suggestions drove more interactions overall, while personalized insights worked better for highly active users. — [Bidargaddi et al., Prev Med Reports 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6080195/) (via [Eugene Yan summary](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/push/)) [SECONDARY]
- **Emoji**: Leanplum and App Annie analyzed **2.6 billion** pushes (published 2017) and found **+85%** open rate on average with emoji (**iOS +50%, Android +135%**). This is correlational across campaigns. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/mobile/emojis-boost-boost-push-notification-open-rates-85-study-says) [VENDOR][DATED]. CleverTap reports emoji pushes averaging **3.44% CTR** and **+38%** CTR, from 4 billion pushes. — [CleverTap blog](https://clevertap.com/blog/emojis-in-push-notifications/) [VENDOR; SECONDARY via search summary]
- **Length / truncation**:
  - Airship (2021, "600 billion+ pushes" benchmark context): iOS lock screen and Notification Center show **about 4 lines**; the Android lock screen shows **about 1 line of about 50 characters** (about 8 lines when expanded). It gives a general ~40-character guideline but **no measured length vs open-rate data**. — [Airship, "Brevity = Wit"](https://www.airship.com/blog/brevity-wit-the-ideal-push-notification-length/) [VENDOR]
  - Braze's docs table: iOS lock screen about **160 characters** text-only, **130** with rich media. A Pushwoosh test on stock Android 12 found a collapsed **body of 43 characters** and **title of 39 characters** (one line each). APNs and FCM payload cap: **4 KB**. — [Braze message format](https://www.braze.com/docs/help/best_practices/push/message_format/); [Pushwoosh help](https://help.pushwoosh.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000440366) [SECONDARY via search summary; figures vary by device and OS]
- **Apple HIG on wording**: "Provide concise, informative notifications." "Use complete sentences, sentence case, and proper punctuation, and don't truncate your message — the system does this automatically." Titles: "Prefer brief titles that people can read at a glance… Use title-style capitalization and no ending punctuation." "Avoid including your app name or icon." "Avoid including sensitive, personal, or confidential information… you can't predict what people will be doing when they receive a notification." With previews hidden, write generic text such as "Reminder". Also: "Avoid telling people what to do because it's hard for people to remember such instructions after they dismiss the notification" (in context, this refers to instructions to carry out later, not to reminders). — [Apple HIG, Notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications)
- **Grouping**: Apple: "Avoid sending multiple notifications for the same thing… people may turn off all notifications from your app." — [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications). Volume reductions at Pinterest raised CTR 11–31% (see Q1). — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3219819.3219906)

### Inferences
- **Naming the empty trackers** fits the strongest wording evidence (specific, user-specific slots), for example "Mood and Sleep are still waiting for today's dot". On Android only about 40–50 characters show collapsed, so put the tracker names first and collapse to "Mood, Sleep + 2 more" past two names. Use iOS's extra lines for a soft second sentence.
- **Privacy**: tracker names can be sensitive (e.g. "Anxiety", "Period", "Drinks"). The HIG warns against private information on the lock screen. Consider a per-tracker "show in reminders" flag, or showing user-renamed labels only when previews are allowed (iOS falls back to generic text when previews are hidden).
- **Rotate a small pool of cozy templates** (Duolingo's novelty effect), localized and tested **per language**, since translation changed performance in Duolingo's data. With 4 app locales, write templates natively per locale rather than translating literally.
- **Tone**: Duolingo's guilt-tinged mascot is famous, but I found no data showing guilt or loss framing outperforms warm framing for reminders. Stawarz found positive reinforcement ineffective for habit formation, which neither supports nor refutes tone. Given the owner's stated non-aggressive intent and the pixel-art tone, warm, low-pressure wording is the low-risk choice. A small emoji or pixel motif is plausible but its lift is unproven for this category.

### Gaps
- No controlled study found on **guilt/loss vs positive framing** in reminder notifications, or on **"streak at risk"** framing outside Duolingo marketing anecdotes.
- No A/B data on **one summary notification vs several per-item notifications**.
- No recent (2023–2026) independent emoji study. Emoji figures are vendor 2016–2017 data.
- Exact truncation lengths vary by OS version, device, font size and the presence of a title. They need device testing.

## Q5. Platform guidelines: Apple HIG and App Store rules, Android guidance

### Takeaway
Apple requires consent before any notification, explicit separate consent for marketing pushes, an in-app settings screen to change the choice, and no marketing at the Time Sensitive level. A daily reminder belongs at the default "Active" level. Android 13+ makes notifications off by default and recommends asking in context after users know the app (e.g. on the 3rd or 4th launch), and it shows users how many notifications an app sends per day.

### Cited Findings
- **App Store Review Guideline 4.5.4**: "Push Notifications must not be required for the app to function, and should not be used to send sensitive personal or confidential information. Push Notifications should not be used for promotions or direct marketing purposes unless customers have explicitly opted in to receive them via consent language displayed in your app's UI, and you provide a method in your app for a user to opt out from receiving such messages." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Guideline 5.1.2(i)**: an app "may not require users to enable system functionalities (such as push notifications…) in order to access functionality, content, use the app, or receive monetary or other compensation". **4.5.3**: do not use push "to spam, phish, or send unsolicited messages". — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **HIG, Managing notifications**: "You need to get permission before sending any notification." "Get people's permission if you want to send them promotional or marketing notifications… Create an alert, modal view, or other interface that describes the types of information you want to send and gives people a clear way to opt in or out." "Make sure people can manage their notification settings within your app… you must also provide an in-app settings screen that lets people change their choice." "Never use the Time Sensitive interruption level to send a marketing notification." — [Apple HIG, Managing notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-notifications)
- **Interruption levels (HIG)**: **Passive** = "Information people can view at their leisure"; **Active** (the default) = "Information people might appreciate knowing about when it arrives"; **Time Sensitive** = only for something "happening now or will happen within an hour" that "requires their immediate attention"; **Critical** = health and safety, requires an entitlement. "You need to specify a system-defined interruption level for each noncommunication notification you send." — [Apple HIG, Managing notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-notifications)
- **Android 13+ (API 33) `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`**: on new installs, "your app's notifications are off by default". Guidance: "Before you ask users to grant any permissions, let them familiarize themselves with your app… you might wait until the third or fourth time the user launches your app." "Request the permission in context, so that it's explicitly clear what the notifications are used for and why the user should opt in." "Use the permission responsibly… Users can see the number of daily notifications that your app sends, and they can revoke the permission at any time." If an app targets 12L or lower and the user taps "Don't allow" once, they are not prompted again until reinstall. — [Android Developers, Notification runtime permission](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission)
- **Vendor practice consistent with platform guidance**: Airship recommends a "soft prompt" before the system dialog and giving new users "space to explore". It reports onboarding campaigns raising opt-in up to 40% above the category average, and in-app messages adding +14% opt-in among opted-out users. — [Airship 2025 benchmarks PDF](https://growth.airship.com/rs/313-QPJ-195/images/Airship-2025-Push-Notification-Benchmarks-EN.pdf) [VENDOR]

### Inferences
- A user-configured daily tracker reminder is **informational, not marketing**, so it falls under the general consent rule (system prompt plus in-app toggle). Any future "come back"/re-engagement or promotional copy, such as a premium upsell, would need **separate explicit opt-in** under 4.5.4 and the HIG.
- Use the **Active** level (or **Passive** if users choose "quiet reminders"). Never Time Sensitive: a mood log is not urgent.
- The in-app settings screen (on/off, time, which trackers count) is effectively **required** by the HIG and also helps with the "relevance" opt-out reason from Q1.
- On Android, local notifications still need `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`. Asking right after the user creates their first tracker, or after the first coloured dot, is "in context" in Google's sense.

### Gaps
- Neither Apple nor Google gives a numeric frequency limit for reminders. The guidance is qualitative ("responsibly", "avoid multiple notifications for the same thing").
- I did not retrieve Android's general notification design page or Material guidance (channels, "don't use notifications for cross-promotion") in this pass. Exact-alarm restrictions for scheduled local reminders on Android 12–14 were not researched here.
