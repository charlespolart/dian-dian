# One-time / lifetime purchase vs subscription for small consumer apps (willingness to pay, lifetime pricing, paywall presentation)

Context for the report writer: Dian Dian (indie freemium mood/habit "year in pixels" tracker). Current US prices: yearly $8.99 (7-day trial), lifetime $22.99 (= 2.56x yearly), monthly (price unknown, 7-day trial). Paywall order: Monthly → Yearly (preselected, "save X%") → Lifetime. Observed: lifetime is the majority choice among direct buyers; nobody bought a subscription without a trial; lifetime buyers used the app 2 days to 1 month first. Sample is ~6 payers, which is far too small to be statistically meaningful by itself.

Evidence labels used below: [INDEP] = independent survey or academic work; [VENDOR] = data or advice from a monetization vendor (RevenueCat, Adapty, Superwall, Purchasely, Airbridge, Rocketship), large samples but commercial interest; [ANECDOTE] = single developer or blogger; [STORE] = App Store listing checked November 2026; [UNVERIFIED] = seen only in a secondary source.

## Q1. Subscription fatigue and willingness to pay: do users of small utility / self-tracking apps refuse subscriptions?

### Takeaway
In Western markets, a plurality of consumers do not want software subscriptions. The US shows the lowest support of 18 markets surveyed (16% in favour), and older app-specific polling found about half of US iOS users prefer a one-time payment. Hard, recent (2023–2026) Western survey data specifically on small apps is thin. The best large-sample behavioural data (RevenueCat, Adapty) shows freemium apps convert only ~2% of downloads, and that one-time purchases are gaining share in Lifestyle-type apps. That is consistent with the owner's hunch, though it does not prove it.

### Cited Findings
**Consumer surveys**
- [INDEP] YouGov Global Profiles, fieldwork January 2022, 18 markets, 528–2,002 adults per market: overall 30% prefer to pay a monthly subscription for software, 32% do not, 38% don't know or are ambivalent. Support was lowest in the **United States (16%)**, then Germany (18%) and Great Britain (20%), and highest in UAE (50%), India (43%) and Mexico (41%). By age it peaks at 25–34 (36%) and 35–44 (33%). Published 29 March 2022 — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/articles/41836-global-how-many-consumers-are-fine-monthly-softwar)
- [INDEP, old] Creative Strategies survey of 800 US smartphone users (July 2018): **54% of iOS users and 47% of Android users prefer a one-time payment** over subscriptions. 45% of iOS users have 5+ paid apps or subscriptions vs 19% of Android users. 46% of iOS / 38% of Android users would pay to remove ads — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2018/07/18/app-store-play-store-survey/)
- [INDEP, press release] CNET survey (2025): Americans spend nearly $1,100 a year on subscriptions, with about $200 going unused — [CNET via EIN Presswire](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/949563719/americans-spend-nearly-1-100-a-year-on-subscriptions-with-about-200-going-unused-cnet-survey-finds)
- [INDEP, academic] "Subscriptions versus one-off purchases: the impact of fee-charging models on consumer privacy concerns" reports seven experiments (four preregistered) and finds that heightened privacy concern increases preference for subscriptions over lifetime access. Its abstract notes that prior research explains subscription preference mainly by economic factors: uncertain future usage and flexible payments. I could see only the abstract snippet; the full text returned 403 — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369942940_Subscriptions_versus_one-off_purchases_the_impact_of_fee-charging_models_on_consumer_privacy_concerns)

**Behavioural / market data**
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026 (calendar-2025 data; 115,000+ apps, $16B+ revenue): median download-to-paid at day 35 is **2.1% for freemium** (top quartile >4.5%) vs **10.7% for hard paywall** (top quartile >20%). Overall median is 2.0%; North America 2.8%. Health & Fitness D35 download-to-paid is 2.9% (top quartile >6.2%) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- [VENDOR] Same report: 63.5% of apps are subscription-only and **23.2% combine subscriptions + lifetime**; Photo & Video has the highest combo share (33.0%) — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- [VENDOR] Adapty State of In-App Subscriptions 2026 (16K apps, ~$3B revenue, 2023–2025, published 13 March 2026): **one-time purchases rose to 26.3% of Lifestyle-category revenue in 2025, up from 5.9% in 2023** — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)
- [VENDOR, citing Adapty 2026] One-time/lifetime purchases rose from 6.4% to 10.3% of app monetization models between 2023 and 2025 — [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/should-you-offer-a-lifetime-subscription)
- [VENDOR] Adapty 2026: revenue is extremely concentrated, with the top 10% of apps capturing ~95% of subscription revenue — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/subscription-app-success-2026/)

**Category signals (mood / habit trackers)**
- [VENDOR, competitor-authored] Pay-once mood-tracker roundup (A Two Lab, maker of MoodKit; US prices checked August 2026, updated 30 October 2026). Pixels: core free, ~$4.99 one-time unlock. Daylio: $59.99 lifetime. MoodKit: $79.99 lifetime or $39.99/year. stoic.: lifetime raised from $199.99 (August) to $299 (October 2026). How We Feel and Apple State of Mind: free. eMoods: ~$9.99/year. The existence of this "no subscription" SEO genre is itself a signal of demand — [MoodKit blog](https://moodkit.co/blog/best-mood-trackers-no-subscription/)
- [ANECDOTE] Ulysses (writing app) switched from one-time to subscription in August 2017 ($4.99/month, $39.99/year). Its App Store rating reportedly fell to ~2.5 stars within days, with reviewers citing the price change — [Medium, Dan Merfeld](https://medium.com/@dan_merfeld/ulysses-fucked-up-311a5f6ea080); pricing context: [TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2017/08/10/ulysses-writing-app-moves-to-subscription-pricing/)
- [ANECDOTE] Tech blogger buying Gentler Streak: his personal threshold for questioning any subscription is **$10/year**, anchored on Overcast Premium's price. He took the lifetime because it was "just $20 more" than one year — [HeyDingus, June 2023](https://heydingus.net/blog/2023/6/why-i-happily-spent-70-on-an-app-i-almost-ignored)

### Inferences
- The US, the main Western market, is the market least favourable to software subscriptions in the YouGov data (16% for, with the rest split between against and undecided). Together with the 2018 app-specific 54% (iOS) one-time preference, a large minority to a majority of potential buyers would rather pay once. That fits Dian Dian's observation that no one subscribed without a trial while direct buyers chose lifetime.
- The rise of one-time revenue in Lifestyle (5.9% → 26.3%) is the strongest recent large-sample signal that lifetime options are working, not just tolerated, in consumer non-utility apps. Mood and habit trackers sit between Lifestyle and Health & Fitness.
- A freemium tracker should expect ~2–3% of downloads to ever pay (RevenueCat medians). With such low conversion, letting subscription-averse users pay at all (a lifetime option) likely matters more than optimising subscription mix.

### Gaps
- I found no 2023–2026 Western survey asking specifically about small utility or self-tracking apps (subscription vs one-time). The best data are YouGov 2022 (software in general) and Creative Strategies 2018 (apps, US only).
- Widely repeated statistics are found only on aggregator or vendor glossaries and were not traced to primary reports, so they are [UNVERIFIED]: "41% of consumers report subscription fatigue", "CivicScience: 42% cancelled ≥1 subscription in 6 months (late 2025, n=4,800)", C+R Research "estimate $86/month vs actual $219", West Monroe "89% underestimate", and Deloitte 2025 "60% would cancel favourite streaming service after a $5 increase".
- HabitKit's developer is quoted on third-party sites as saying that "a lot of people really, really despise subscriptions". I could not verify the primary quote.

## Q2. Lifetime plans: share of revenue, cannibalization, lifetime-to-annual multiple, sustainability, indie case studies

### Takeaway
Lifetime is a mainstream secondary option: ~23% of apps pair it with subscriptions. Vendor guidance says 5–12x the annual price for high-retention, well-funded apps, but **small indie trackers actually price lifetime at ~1.7–3x annual** (Daylio 1.67x, MoodKit 2x, HabitKit 2.5x, Gentler Streak ~2.8x, Itemlist 3x). The closest comparable, HabitKit, earned roughly half its revenue from lifetime at one snapshot and reached $602k in 2025 with subscription + lifetime side by side. Vendors warn that lifetime above ~20–30% of revenue compresses recurring growth, and that heavily lifetime-driven apps have low revenue per install. Dian Dian's 2.56x sits in the indie band, but its absolute prices are very low.

### Cited Findings
**Adoption and revenue share (large-sample, vendor)**
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat SOSA 2026: subscriptions + lifetime = 23.2% of apps. A lifetime/other plan appears on 4–18% of paywalls depending on category, with Gaming highest (18%). The fetched page says Health & Fitness is lowest, while a search snippet of the same page said Shopping is lowest (conflict, unresolved). **Lifetime-dominant apps have median revenue per install of $0.19 at D14 and $0.24 at D60.** Comparable medians: Health & Fitness $0.48/$0.66, freemium $0.27/$0.38, hard paywall $2.32/$3.09 — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat guide to lifetime subscriptions (Daphne Tideman, 12 November 2025): 18–24% of apps in most categories offer both subscription and lifetime, the second most common model; Photo & Video, Education and Travel lead. The guide gives **no figures on lifetime revenue share or cannibalization** — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/lifetime-subscriptions)
- [VENDOR] Airbridge (published June 2026, updated 5 November 2026): heuristic that if **lifetime exceeds 20–30% of total revenue, MRR growth is likely being compressed**. Its cannibalization example: a $79/yr subscriber staying 4 years is worth ~$316, so a $299 lifetime is a loss on that user — [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/should-you-offer-a-lifetime-subscription)
- [VENDOR] Adapty 2026: Lifestyle one-time purchases were 26.3% of revenue in 2025 (5.9% in 2023). Annual plans were 60.6% of Health & Fitness revenue in 2025 (51% in 2023) — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)

**Price multiples (lifetime ÷ annual)**
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat guide table: Calm $79 → $399 (5.0x); Jumpspeak $69 → $249 (3.6x); Moonly $28.98 → $59.98 (2.1x); Fiit £119.99 → £375 (3.1x; 2.5x on offer at £299); Placify $14.99 → $49.99 (3.3x); Waking Up $129.99 → $1,500 (11.5x). The text says lifetime is "often priced at 5–12x annual" and advises A/B testing 5x vs 8x vs 12x and pricing **higher rather than lower when in doubt** — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/lifetime-subscriptions)
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat "8 paywall test ideas" (David Barnard & Peter Meinertzhagen, 15 September 2023): "Lifetime subscription: start at **1.5–2x LTV** and track how it changes over time" — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-tests-grow-app-revenue)
- [VENDOR] Airbridge suggests testing 3.5x, 5x and 7x, and says most successful premium-tier offers land at ≥5x — [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/should-you-offer-a-lifetime-subscription)
- [VENDOR, low rigor] Other pricing blogs give rules of thumb of 3–5x, 2.5–4x, or "at least 2.5x" annual, with no disclosed methodology — [PricePush](https://pricepush.app/glossary/lifetime-purchase); [Funnelfox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/app-pricing-models-guide/)

**Retention inputs needed to price lifetime against annual LTV** (sources conflict)
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat SOSA 2025, as cited by Airbridge: 12-month retention yearly 44.1%, monthly 17.5%, weekly 3.4%. First renewal yearly 66.3%, monthly 55.5%. Renewal rates approach 80%+ by the third renewal — [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/should-you-offer-a-lifetime-subscription)
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat SOSA 2026, as summarised by Rocketship: first renewal of yearly plans is **37% for below-average-price apps vs 24% for above-average-price apps**. 35% of annual cancellations happen in month 1 — [Rocketship HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/subscription-app-conversion-rate-price-increase/)
- [VENDOR] Adapty 2026: annual-plan D380 retention is 21.3% in Europe and 20.0% in North America — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)
- Note: these three are not the same metric (cohort definitions, trial inclusion) and disagree widely, from ~20–24% to 66% first-year renewal.
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat 2023: users who renew a yearly plan after the first year are twice as likely to renew again — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-tests-grow-app-revenue)

**Indie / comparable case studies**
- [ANECDOTE, closest comparable] **HabitKit** (Sebastian Röhl, solo dev, habit tracker with a GitHub-style grid, launched November 2022; free for up to 4 habits, Pro by month, year or lifetime):
  - Snapshot from the developer's Indie Hackers post: MRR $15,100 = **$7,800 subscriptions + $7,300 lifetime (~48% lifetime)** — [Small Start case page citing IH](https://small-start.com/en/cases/global-habitkit-sebastian-roehl/)
  - Separate snapshot (24 September 2024): MRR ≈ $7,900 vs total monthly revenue ≈ $15,000 including lifetime — [High Signal](https://www.highsignal.io/15k-a-month-habit-tracking-app/)

Revenue "stabilized above $40k" a month over the year. 272k App Store + 290k Google Play downloads in 2025 — [Sebastian Röhl Substack](https://sebastianroehl.substack.com/p/2025-the-year-that-changed-everything)
  - [STORE] US App Store in-app purchases listed (November 2026): Pro 1 Month $0.99 / $1.99 / $4.99; 1 Year $5.99 / $11.99 / $24.99; **Lifetime $14.99 / $29.99**; "HabitKit Pro" $59.99. - [ANECDOTE] **Itemlist** (indie home-inventory app; dabo.dev, March 2024): $2.99/month, $19.99/year, **$59.99 lifetime (3.0x)**. "Most paid users choose yearly or lifetime option." - [VENDOR, competitor] **MoodKit** $39.99/year vs $79.99 lifetime (2.0x); **stoic.** lifetime raised $199.99 → $299 in 2026 — [MoodKit blog](https://moodkit.co/blog/best-mood-trackers-no-subscription/)

**Operational gotchas**
- [VENDOR community] On iOS, buying a lifetime product does **not** cancel an active subscription: users can end up paying for both, and developers cannot cancel iOS subscriptions programmatically — [RevenueCat Community](https://community.revenuecat.com/general-questions-7/setting-up-a-subscription-groups-and-a-lifetime-product-handling-users-who-switch-between-them-6445)
- [VENDOR] Airbridge recommends tracking refunds and double-charge complaints for 14 days after lifetime purchases — [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/should-you-offer-a-lifetime-subscription)

### Inferences
- **Two pricing worlds.** The 5–12x multiples come from high-retention, high-price, often funded apps (Calm, Waking Up), where an annual subscriber is expected to renew for years. Small indie trackers with $6–40/year subscriptions cluster at **~1.7–3x** (Daylio 1.67x, MoodKit 2.0x, HabitKit 2.5x on both of its visible price pairs, Itemlist 3.0x, Gentler Streak ~2.8x regular). Dian Dian's 2.56x ($22.99 / $8.99) is squarely in the indie band.
- **LTV arithmetic for the multiple.** Expected annual payments per annual subscriber ≈ 1 + r₁/(1 − r_later). With first renewal r₁ of 20–50% and later renewals of ~75%, that is ≈ 1.8–3.0 annual payments. RevenueCat's "1.5–2x LTV" rule therefore implies lifetime ≈ **2.7–6x annual**. Dian Dian at 2.56x is at or slightly below the bottom of that range. It is defensible if lifetime buyers are mostly people who would never subscribe (the observed pattern), less so if they would otherwise have renewed.
- **HabitKit is the strongest evidence that "both" works for exactly this category.** It is a solo-dev, grid-visual habit tracker with subscription + lifetime offered side by side, about half its revenue from lifetime at one point, and $602k/year. Rough inference: $28k end-of-year MRR × 12 ≈ $336k, an upper bound on 2025 subscription revenue, leaves ≥~$266k (≥~44%) of 2025 revenue from non-recurring (lifetime) sales. This is approximate because MRR grew during the year and the developer's exact split is not public.
- **Lifetime-heavy apps earn little per install** (RevenueCat median RPI $0.19–0.24 for lifetime-dominant apps). For a solo dev with no server costs, though, the low marginal cost of a lifetime user makes this less dangerous than for content apps. Vendors' cannibalization warnings mainly protect MRR and valuation, which matter less to a solo indie.
- **Lifetime revenue is lumpy and depends on new users.** Sustainability rests on continuous acquisition (HabitKit gets ~98% of users from store search) and on seasonal peaks (January).

### Gaps
- No independent or large-sample figure for **the share of revenue coming from lifetime** across apps. A claim attributed to Adapty 2026 ("14% of apps offer lifetime, it brings ~1% of subscription revenue, and lifetime next to annual lifted revenue 10–15% in winning tests") appeared only in a search summary and could not be traced to a primary Adapty page [UNVERIFIED].
- No controlled public test measuring **cannibalization** (with vs without lifetime). Superwall recommends running one but publishes no results — [Superwall](https://superwall.com/solutions/lifetime-or-no-lifetime)
- No public revenue numbers found for Streaks, Bear, Carrot, Things, Day One, Structured or Gentler Streak on lifetime vs subscription. Third-party "revenue estimate" sites (Starter Story etc.) gave implausible or conflicting figures and were excluded.
- HabitKit's current exact US price pair could not be confirmed. Several historical tiers remain listed; both visible pairs ($5.99/$14.99 and $11.99/$29.99) imply 2.5x.

## Q3. Paywall presentation: anchoring, decoys, default selection, monthly-equivalent price, "save X%", trials, intro discounts, limited-time lifetime offers

### Takeaway
The best-supported levers are a preselected annual plan with fewer visible options, annual shown as a per-month equivalent, explicit savings, and clear (not toggled) trials of about a week or more. Seasonal promotions, especially Black Friday and New Year, measurably lift new payers, and lifetime offers are about twice as common on holiday paywalls. Classic "decoy" effects are fragile outside lab settings. Vendors mostly advise keeping lifetime secondary on the paywall or using it in promotions and win-back, but some utility apps show it permanently.

### Cited Findings
**Anchoring and decoys**
- [INDEP] Ariely's Economist example: web-only $59, print-only $125 (decoy), print+web $125. Without the decoy, 68% chose web-only and 32% the bundle; with it, 16% and 84% — [Wikipedia: Decoy effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decoy_effect)
- [INDEP] Frederick, Lee & Baskin (2014, *Journal of Marketing Research*, "The Limits of Attraction"): attraction/decoy effects appear reliably only when attributes are clean numbers. With meaningful verbal descriptions or pictures, the effect fell to chance. Later work (Yang & Lynn 2014; Trendl, Stewart & Mullett 2021) also found no attraction effect in naturalistic choice — [arXiv 2006.14868 literature review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.14868); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decoy_effect)
- [INDEP] A Palgrave Communications paper argues the Economist decoy was "ill-positioned" and that larger effects come from moving the decoy to higher attribute values — [Nature / Palgrave Comms 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/palcomms201682)
- [VENDOR] Superwall: the anchor is the first price a user sees; it recommends testing whether a lifetime plan converts better than emphasising annual, without published results — [Superwall](https://superwall.com/blog/5-paywall-patterns-used-by-million-dollar-apps)

**Default selection and number of plans**
- [VENDOR] Mojo (RevenueCat 2023): making yearly **the default and only visible plan**, with monthly behind "view all plans", raised yearly take-up 15–20%, with a minor rise in cancellations. RevenueCat: "a single annual product usually performs best" on the main screen — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-tests-grow-app-revenue)
- [VENDOR] A party game app removed monthly from the main screen (under "View All Plans") and showed weekly + yearly with "SAVE 83%". Result: **+31% install-to-trial and +64% revenue** (also added a trial toggle, now banned on iOS; see below) — [RevenueCat, 27 March 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-redesigns-case-studies)
- [VENDOR] Adapty 2026: plan-count tests (e.g. 2 vs 3 plans) yield 63% more conversion uplift than price-change tests. Average LTV uplift by test type: locale 62.3%, trial structure 59.6%, plan duration 58.7%, plan count 57.1%, price 45.5%, visual/text 34.6% — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)

**Monthly-equivalent display and "save X%"**
- [VENDOR] Mojo: replacing "$59.99 per year" with **"$4.99 per month billed annually" significantly increased revenue per paywall impression**, especially in lower-income regions — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-tests-grow-app-revenue)
- [VENDOR] Food app: "SAVE 40%" on annual plus a $2.49/month effective yearly price (with other changes) gave +72% install-to-trial — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-redesigns-case-studies)
- [VENDOR] Adapty: the median annual plan costs ~3x the monthly price (~75% saving per month). Advice: "Show it explicitly on the paywall" — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/weekly-monthly-annual-subscription-plan/)

**Trials**
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat SOSA 2026 median trial-to-paid by trial length: **≤4 days 25.5%**, **5–9 days 37.4%**, **17–32 days 42.5%**. Despite this, 46.5% of apps now use ≤4-day trials (up from 42.1%). Health & Fitness trial-to-paid is 37.7% and North America 34.2%. In Health & Fitness, 82.1% of trial starts happen on day 0, and only 18.3% of apps use no trial — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- [VENDOR] For 3-day trials, 55.4% of cancellations happen on day 0 (RevenueCat 2026 via Rocketship) — [Rocketship HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/subscription-app-conversion-rate-price-increase/)
- [VENDOR] Adapty 2026: trial users' day-380 LTV premium over direct buyers is **Health & Fitness +63.6%**, Utilities +85.1%, Education +50.4%, but **Productivity −13.7% and Lifestyle −21.2%**. Annual+trial LTV goes from $42.08 at day 0 to $49.92 at day 380 — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat recommends testing 3/7/14/30-day trials and watching refunds, since higher price with a shorter trial can raise refunds — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-tests-grow-app-revenue)
- [VENDOR, policy] Since mid-January 2026, **Apple rejects iOS paywalls with a free-trial on/off toggle** under Guideline 3.1.2 ("confusing and misleading"). Recommended fix: present explicit plans that state whether a trial is included; Flo replaced its toggle with a distinct "14-day free trial" plan. Toggles reportedly remain allowed on Google Play and web — [RevenueCat "R.I.P. toggle paywall"](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/r-i-p-toggle-paywall-we-hardly-knew-ye/); [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/your-toggle-paywall-is-about-to-get-rejected/); [Superwall](https://superwall.com/blog/external-checkout-a-b-testing-and-trial-toggles-confirmed-apples-rules-for-ios)
- [VENDOR] Flo Health blackout test: turning off its trial toggle cost ~15% of new revenue (summary) — [Flo Health on Medium](https://medium.com/flo-health/zero-uplift-was-the-win-how-we-replaced-a-trial-toggle-without-losing-a-dollar-78122b2a69c6)

**Lifetime placement on the paywall**
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat: most lifetime offers are **not prominently featured** on the main paywall. They are sent by email or framed as one-off opportunities, though some utility apps (Placify, Moonly) include lifetime as a standard option. Recommended: run lifetime first as seasonal promotions (Black Friday, New Year) and to lapsed or low-retention segments via owned channels — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/lifetime-subscriptions)
- [VENDOR] Airbridge: avoid putting lifetime on the main paywall for all users, since it cannibalises normal conversion. Start with win-back emails and long-tenure subscribers, and consider a limited paywall placement only after 2–3 months of owned-channel results — [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/should-you-offer-a-lifetime-subscription)
- [VENDOR] Superwall frames lifetime as something that "can capture buyers who'll never start a subscription — or quietly cannibalize your recurring revenue and cap your LTV", and advises a with/without holdout test — [Superwall](https://superwall.com/solutions/lifetime-or-no-lifetime)

**Seasonal / limited-time offers (measured)**
- [VENDOR, most recent and largest] RevenueCat holiday study (Margarita Loktionova, 27 October 2026; 4,964 apps vs each app's own Sept–Oct 2025 baseline; 180-day revenue follow-up):
  - Black Friday week: new paying customers per day **+20% with promotions vs −16% without**. Peak day (Nov 28) +49%. Revenue per acquisition day +12% vs −15%.
  - **Health & Fitness +37%** and Utilities +46% in Black Friday week with promotions.
  - Median six-month revenue per Black Friday buyer was −8% (discount effect), but 60% of apps came out ahead on revenue per acquisition day.
  - New Year/January (Dec 26–Feb 1): **+14% with promotions vs −4% without** (Dec 26–Jan 4: +24%). Health & Fitness was +11% over baseline in New Year/January. January buyers in promotion apps generated 10% more six-month revenue per customer than Black Friday buyers.
  - Christmas is weak: −11% with promotions vs −22% without.
  - About **half of the Black Friday increase came from users who had installed more than 3 months earlier**. 55% of Black Friday-week payers installed at least a day before paying (vs 45% in Sept–Oct).
  - Annual plans rose to 49% of new payers in Black Friday week (from 41%), and the annual 30-day refund rate fell to 2.7% (from 4%).
  - **Lifetime access appeared on 35–38% of Black Friday / New Year paywalls vs 17% normally.** Percentage discounts appeared on 61% of Black Friday paywalls vs 8% normally, and nearly 3 in 4 of those showed ≥50% off. About 1/3 used limited-time wording and ~1 in 5 a countdown.
  - The authors caution: "The comparisons show associations, not direct proof that promotions cause growth."
  - Source: [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/holiday-offers-subscription-apps)
- [ANECDOTE] HabitKit: January 2025 = $112k of $602k annual revenue (~19% in one month) — [Sebastian Röhl Substack](https://sebastianroehl.substack.com/p/2025-the-year-that-changed-everything)
- [ANECDOTE] Itemlist: 50%-off lifetime at Black Friday 2023 produced a clear sales spike (>$1,400 that month) — [dabo.dev](https://dabo.dev/revealing-my-revenue-metrics)
- [ANECDOTE] Gentler Streak's 50%-off limited-time lifetime converted a buyer who said he did not want to wait for a better deal or a trial — [HeyDingus](https://heydingus.net/blog/2023/6/why-i-happily-spent-70-on-an-app-i-almost-ignored)

### Inferences
- For Dian Dian's paywall, the evidence points to keeping Yearly preselected with an explicit per-month equivalent and an explicit "save X%" figure (Mojo, food app). Monthly could move behind "view all plans", or at least lose the first/anchor slot (Mojo +15–20% yearly take-up; party app +64% revenue). Monthly's main remaining job is as the anchor that makes the yearly saving look large.
- Showing Monthly first as the anchor fits the "first price seen" anchoring logic. Decoy-style manipulation should not be relied on, because attraction effects shrink when options carry rich non-numeric descriptions (Frederick et al. 2014).
- Dian Dian's 7-day trial sits in the 5–9-day band (37.4% median trial-to-paid, vs 25.5% for ≤4 days). For Health & Fitness, trial buyers are worth +63.6% more than direct buyers (Adapty). Moving to a shorter trial is not supported. A longer one (14+ days) has higher median conversion, but it delays revenue and is unproven for this app.
- On iOS, any "enable free trial" toggle is now a rejection risk. Plans should state the trial explicitly (e.g. "Yearly — 7 days free, then $X/yr").
- Lifetime: vendor advice (hide it, use it only in promos) is tuned to maximise MRR for subscription-first apps. For an app where direct buyers overwhelmingly choose lifetime and nobody subscribes without a trial, the HabitKit / Daylio / Placify / Moonly pattern of a permanent lifetime option is the closer precedent. Seasonal lifetime discounts at New Year (habit-tracker peak) and Black Friday are well supported by the 2026 RevenueCat data. About half of the Black Friday lift comes from long-time free users, which matches Dian Dian's "test for weeks, then buy lifetime" behaviour.

### Gaps
- No published field experiment isolates the effect of adding or removing a lifetime option (decoy or anchor) on a real app paywall.
- No direct data compare a trial on annual only vs a trial on both monthly and annual.
- No isolated measurement of introductory discounts (pay-up-front or pay-as-you-go intro offers) for low-priced apps. RevenueCat only reports that 45% of Black Friday promo apps used first-time subscriber offers.
- The RevenueCat paywall-redesign case studies bundle several changes, so the effect of "save X%" alone is not isolated.
- The academic "pennies-a-day" temporal-reframing literature (Gourville, 1998), which underlies per-month or per-day equivalents, was not fetched in this pass, so it is uncited here.

## Q4. Psychological price points (.99 vs .49) and price increases for low-priced apps: is $8.99/year leaving money on the table?

### Takeaway
Dian Dian's $8.99/year is roughly a quarter of the category median (~$35–40/year in North America and Health & Fitness) and below every indie comparable found ($11.99–$39.99/year). Large-sample data show higher-priced apps convert downloads to paid at least as well and earn far more per payer, though they renew less. Price tests win on LTV more often than on conversion. The left-digit (.99) effect is real but small and fragile. Evidence therefore favours testing substantially higher yearly and lifetime prices rather than fine-tuning endings.

### Cited Findings
**Where $8.99/year sits**
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat SOSA 2026: North America median yearly price **$39.99** and monthly $9.99. Health & Fitness yearly median ~$39.94, monthly $9.99. Overall yearly median rose $31.60 → $34.80, and the most common yearly price point is $30 — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- [VENDOR] Adapty 2026: global medians weekly $7.48, monthly $12.99, annual $38.42 — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/weekly-monthly-annual-subscription-plan/). European apps charge 29–39% more than North American apps; the monthly gap reached 39% in 2025 ($15.25 vs $10.95) — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)
- [STORE] Indie tracker yearly prices: HabitKit $11.99 (older $5.99 still listed) with $29.99 lifetime; Daylio $35.99 with $59.99 lifetime; Itemlist $19.99 with $59.99 lifetime; MoodKit $39.99 with $79.99 lifetime; Gentler Streak $49.99 — [HabitKit App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6443918070); [Daylio App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/daylio-journal-mood-tracker/id1194023242); [dabo.dev](https://dabo.dev/revealing-my-revenue-metrics); [MoodKit blog](https://moodkit.co/blog/best-mood-trackers-no-subscription/); [HeyDingus](https://heydingus.net/blog/2023/6/why-i-happily-spent-70-on-an-app-i-almost-ignored)

**Price level vs conversion and revenue**
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat SOSA 2026, below- vs above-average-price apps (medians): download-to-trial 4.4% vs 8.9%; **download-to-paid (D35) 1.4% vs 2.8%**; **revenue per payer after one year $10.69 vs $62.19**; first yearly renewal 37% vs 24%; refund rate 2.7% vs 4.5% (via Rocketship) — [Rocketship HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/subscription-app-conversion-rate-price-increase/). The SOSA page itself gives D35 download-to-paid by price tier as high 2.8%, mid 2.0%, low 1.4% — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- Note: this is cross-sectional. Higher-priced apps may simply be better products or have better funnels, so it is not proof that raising price raises conversion.
- [VENDOR] Adapty 2026: price-change tests had an average LTV uplift of 45.5% and conversion uplift of 28.3%, the lowest conversion uplift of any test type — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/). Rocketship reads the same figures as "price changes lifted LTV in 45.5% of tests and conversion in 28.3%". It also reports that high-priced apps earned ~3x the LTV of low-priced apps, and that monthly plans were the most price-sensitive (conversion −53% from low to mid-range price) — [Rocketship HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/subscription-app-conversion-rate-price-increase/). The two readings of the 45.5%/28.3% figures conflict (average uplift vs win rate).
- [VENDOR] Rocketship break-even rule: a price rise of p breaks even at a relative conversion drop of p/(1+p). +20% needs a ≤16.7% conversion drop; +50% ≤33.3%; +100% ≤50% — [Rocketship HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/subscription-app-conversion-rate-price-increase/)
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat advice: test **large** price jumps (e.g. $30/year vs $90 or $140, not $40) — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-tests-grow-app-revenue)
- [VENDOR case] Travel app: +30% price, conversion only −5% — [Adapty case study](https://adapty.io/case-studies/travel-app/). Purchasely: a slightly higher price for Canadian iPhone users more than doubled paid subscriptions and nearly tripled revenue (counter-intuitive single test) — [Purchasely](https://www.purchasely.com/blog/in-app-subscription-price-testing)
- [ANECDOTE] Indie developer tested $3.99 / $9.99 / $29.99 / $99.99 tiers: "The cheapest one lost" (search snippet only) — [Medium](https://medium.com/@andy.a.g/3-99-vs-9-99-9584d3960c84)

**Charm pricing (.99 vs .49 / round)**
- [INDEP] Thomas & Morwitz (2005, *Journal of Consumer Research*, "Penny Wise and Pound Foolish"): the left-digit effect occurs **only when the leading digit changes** ($2.99 vs $3.00 feels different; $4.59 vs $4.60 does not) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/23547242_Penny_Wise_and_Pound_Foolish_The_Left-Digit_Effect_in_Price_Cognition)
- [INDEP] A large online experiment **failed to reproduce** either a left-digit or a perceptual-fluency effect on purchase decisions, and noted that earlier studies relied on moderate samples likely to be underpowered (title: "Psychological price perception may exert a weaker effect on purchasing decisions than previously suggested") — [PMC9387778](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9387778/)
- [VENDOR] Retail blog lore says charm prices outsell round prices by ~24% (from the book *Priceless*), but the effect "nearly vanishes" on an isolated product page where the buyer already knows the price — [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/psychological-pricing-dtc); [Learning Loop](https://learningloop.io/blog/charm-pricing-99-vs-95)

**Store rules for raising prices on existing subscribers**
- [VENDOR] Apple requires subscriber consent for some increases, including increases over 50% and over ~US$5 per period (US$50/year for annual). Notice is 30 days for plans longer than a month. Google Play keeps existing subscribers on legacy price by default; moving them needs opt-in with 37 days' notice. Price increases account for ≤0.5% of recorded cancellation reasons on both stores — [Rocketship HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/subscription-app-conversion-rate-price-increase/)

### Inferences
- $8.99/year is a deep outlier at the low end: ~1/4 of the North America and Health & Fitness median, and below every indie tracker comparable (HabitKit $11.99, Itemlist $19.99, Daylio $35.99, MoodKit $39.99). Cross-sectional data show low-price apps do not convert better (1.4% vs 2.8% D35) and earn ~6x less per payer in year one. That strongly suggests money is being left on the table, though only an A/B test on Dian Dian's own traffic can confirm it.
- By the break-even rule, doubling yearly to ~$17.99 only needs conversion to drop by less than 50% to win. Raising lifetime proportionally (e.g. ~$39.99–$49.99 at 2.2–2.8x) would stay inside the indie multiple band.
- Low prices renew somewhat better (37% vs 24% first yearly renewal), which partly offsets a lower price, but not a 6x gap in revenue per payer.
- Charm-ending choices ($X.99 vs $X.49) are second-order. The only defensible rule from the evidence is to avoid crossing a leading digit (e.g. prefer $19.99 over $20.49). Experiments on the level of price, plan count and default matter far more (Adapty test-type uplifts).
- Because existing subscribers are grandfathered by default on Google Play and need consent above +50% on Apple, price rises mostly affect new users. The risk to the current small base is low.

### Gaps
- No app-specific test of .99 vs .49 endings was found.
- The often-cited Mojo test (50% vs 100% increase on the US yearly price, "100% made more new revenue") appeared only in a secondary search summary without a traceable primary URL [UNVERIFIED].
- No public data on price elasticity specifically in the sub-$15/year band for habit or mood trackers.
- Cross-sectional price-tier statistics confound price with app quality and funnel design; no large-sample causal estimate for low-priced consumer apps was found.
