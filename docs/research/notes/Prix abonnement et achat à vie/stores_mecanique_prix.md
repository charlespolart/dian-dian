# Store pricing mechanics (App Store / Google Play), commissions, price changes and iOS vs Android pricing — Western markets, as of November 2026

Context reminder: Dian Dian = freemium, monthly + yearly auto-renewing subs (7-day trial each) + lifetime non-consumable, via RevenueCat. Current US: yearly $8.99, lifetime $22.99 (RevenueCat shows ~$27.41 for a French lifetime buyer). Solo developer in France, presumably in Apple's Small Business Program (SBP) and Google's reduced tier.

---

## 1. Apple App Store: price points, base storefront, equalization, taxes, overrides

### Takeaway
You pick one base storefront and price; Apple generates "equalized" prices in the other 174 storefronts (FX + tax, following local price conventions), and keeps re-equalizing **apps and one-time IAPs (so the lifetime product)** when FX/tax move, but **not auto-renewable subscriptions**. US and Canada prices are shown before tax, while everywhere else (EU, UK, AU) prices include tax. So an equalized EUR/GBP/AUD sticker price looks about 10–25% higher than the USD number, but proceeds are about the same. Any storefront you set by hand is no longer auto-adjusted.

### Cited Findings
- Base storefront: "You can set a price for the country or region you're familiar with as the basis for automatically generating prices across the other 174 storefronts and 43 currencies." You can change the base at any time. — [App Store Connect Help: Set a price for an In-App Purchase](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-in-app-purchases/set-a-price-for-an-in-app-purchase)
- Each IAP has its own base, separate from the app: "Once you edit the base country or region or update the pricing of an In-App Purchase, it'll no longer inherit changes you make to the app's base country or region." Changing the base country **deletes any scheduled price changes**, and the new pricing takes effect immediately. — [same](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-in-app-purchases/set-a-price-for-an-in-app-purchase)
- Products created before March 9, 2023 default to the US as base region. "Changing the base region will reset the existing price schedules." — [WWDC23 "What's new in App Store pricing"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10014/)
- Price points: "You can choose from up to 800 price points by default. You can also submit a request to access an additional 100 higher price points" (up to $10,000). In a US base the list starts at $0.29. The menu first shows "25 price points that follow the most common pricing convention for each country or region"; the rest are under "See Additional Prices". — [ASC Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-in-app-purchases/set-a-price-for-an-in-app-purchase); also 900 points in 44 currencies — [WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10014/)
- USD increments: "10 cents price steps are supported until $10 and 50 cents price steps are supported until $50." — [Apple Tech Talk 110364](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/110364); same wording: "every $0.10 up to $10, every $0.50 between $10 and $50, etc." — [Apple Developer News, Mar 9 2023](https://developer.apple.com/news?id=dbrszv62)
- Tax treatment: "Prices are globally equalized before tax, which means customers in each country pay approximately the same amount as your base price, but with local taxes added on top." and "**Prices advertised to customers in all countries except the U.S. and Canada include taxes**, so changes in tax may affect your earnings from App Store sales if prices aren't adjusted accordingly." — [Apple Tech Talk 110364](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/110364)
- Commission is taken after VAT: "For any value-added tax (VAT) rate changes, your proceeds and Apple's commission will be calculated after the deduction of VAT." — [ASC Help: Manage pricing for auto-renewable subscriptions (FAQ)](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/manage-pricing-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions)
- Worked Apple example: Germany as base at €7.99 gives proceeds of €4.70 in Germany, with proceeds in other storefronts ranging "from 4.45 euros in Japan to 4.88 euros in Canada" (so proceeds, not sticker prices, are what gets equalized). — [Tech Talk 110364](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/110364)
- Automatic re-equalization: "Periodically, Apple updates prices in certain regions based on changes in taxes and foreign exchange rates… Apple will never change the price in your base country or region, and will always notify you in advance of changes on other storefronts." Upcoming changes are shown in ASC "at least 14 days in advance". — [ASC Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-in-app-purchases/set-a-price-for-an-in-app-purchase); [WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10014/)
- **Subscriptions are excluded from automatic repricing:** "Retail price changes initiated by Apple due to tax changes and significant foreign exchange rate movements will exclude auto-renewable subscriptions… You can choose to pass any changes caused by taxation or foreign exchange onto your subscribers by adjusting the price for your subscription." — [ASC Help: Manage pricing for auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/manage-pricing-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions)
- Subscriptions: "You can choose from 800 price points in each currency"; ASC "provides comparable prices for all 175 App Store countries and regions, taking into account taxes and foreign exchange rates. If you want to set different prices for specific storefronts, make the changes and click Next." — [same](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/manage-pricing-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions)
- Manual overrides: "you can choose different prices for individual countries or regions. If you select your own prices, Apple won't adjust your pricing on those storefronts in the future." You then become "responsible for staying up to date with taxes and exchange rates in the storefronts you manually manage." — [ASC Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-in-app-purchases/set-a-price-for-an-in-app-purchase)
- Subscription price scheduling: "you can schedule one future price change at a time, per country or region, per billing plan type"; a second schedule overwrites the first; earliest effective date is "generally 1 to 2 days in advance". — [ASC Help subs pricing](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/manage-pricing-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions)
- Equalization ignores purchasing power: Apple suggests a UK price about 26% higher than the US, "while Jacob Rushfinn found most apps price about 10% lower there". — [RevenueCat, "The ultimate guide to price localization", Sep 4 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/price-localization-for-apps)
- Historical EUR tier moves (for context only, possibly outdated): in Oct 2022 Apple raised euro prices (minimum €0.99 → €1.19) because of a weak euro. — [MacRumors, Sep 19 2022](https://www.macrumors.com/2022/09/19/app-store-prices-to-increase-in-europe-next-month/). I found no dated 2025–2026 EUR-wide tier announcement. Since 2023, FX moves are handled by rolling re-equalization announced in ASC, not by tier tables.

### Inferences
- **How $22.99 ends up in EUR:** the equalized price ≈ (USD price ÷ EUR/USD rate) × (1 + local VAT), snapped to the nearest conventional local price point. Example: at EUR/USD ≈ 1.16 and 20% French VAT, $22.99 → €19.82 net → €23.78 → about **€23.99**. RevenueCat's "$27.41 for a French buyer" fits €23.99 at EUR/USD ≈ 1.143 (23.99 × 1.143 = 27.41) or €22.99 at ≈ 1.192. **Check the exact FR/DE/UK/CA/AU prices in ASC** (Pricing → comparable prices, or the App Store Connect API price-point equalizations).
- Rough expectations at plausible late-2026 FX rates (to verify, not facts): UK ≈ $22.99/1.33 × 1.20 ≈ £20.7 → £20.99–21.99. Canada (tax-exclusive) ≈ C$31.99. Australia (10% GST included) ≈ A$38.99. The yearly $8.99 → roughly €8.99–9.49, £7.99–8.49 (to verify).
- Because the French price includes 20% VAT and the US price excludes sales tax, an "€X.99 = $X.99" mapping actually gives lower net revenue in Europe. With the SBP 15% rate: US $22.99 → $19.54 proceeds (sales tax added on top for the buyer). FR €23.99 → €19.99 net of VAT → €16.99 proceeds (≈ $19.4 at 1.14). Equalization keeps proceeds roughly equal, which is why the EU sticker price is higher.
- **Mechanical asymmetry to keep in mind:** the lifetime IAP is re-equalized automatically when FX/tax move, but the subscriptions are not. Over time the yearly-vs-lifetime ratio can drift per storefront unless subscription prices are reviewed by hand.
- Price points in the target band: below $10 every $0.10 (e.g., $5.99, $6.99, $7.99, $8.99, $9.49, $9.99 are all available); from $10 to $50 every $0.50 (e.g., $14.99, $19.99, $22.49, $22.99, $24.99, $29.99, $39.99, $49.99). Above $50 the steps are larger (exact step size not verified).

### Gaps
- I could not get Apple's current price-point → EUR/GBP/CAD/AUD equivalence table (it is only inside ASC or via the API). Exact equalized prices for $8.99 / $22.99 must be read in ASC.
- I did not confirm whether eurozone storefronts all show the same € price despite different VAT rates (e.g., DE 19%, FR 20%, IE 23%), or whether prices vary by country.
- Step size above $50, and whether "round" endings (e.g., $10.00) are in the default list, were not verified.

## 2. Google Play: default price, local prices, templates, tax, price points

### Takeaway
On Google Play you type a default price and Play converts it once, at that day's FX rate, into local prices with "locally relevant pricing patterns". Unlike Apple, Play does **not** keep re-equalizing: you refresh exchange rates by hand. Pricing templates were retired on Oct 27, 2025. Subscriptions are priced per base plan per region. EU/UK/AU prices are tax-inclusive, while US/Canada tax is added on top.

### Cited Findings
- "Play uses the price you enter as the base for calculating market-specific prices." "Local prices use today's exchange rate and country-specific pricing patterns." Refresh is manual: "Update exchange rates" then "Apply prices". — [Play Console Help: Set up your app's prices (6334373)](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6334373?hl=en)
- "Starting October 27th, 2025, you'll no longer be able to manage prices across your products using pricing templates." Existing templates were unlinked and their prices copied onto each product. Prices are now managed per product, through Play Console or the `onetimeproducts` API. — [same](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6334373?hl=en)
- Subscription bulk pricing: "Update prices" → select countries → "Set price" → enter a **tax-exclusive** price and currency; "This price will be converted into the appropriate currency for each country/region." Tax is added in tax-inclusive jurisdictions and prices are "adjusted to comply with local customs". — [Play Console Help 140504](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/140504?hl=en)
- Tax: tax-inclusive pricing applies in the EU countries, UK and Australia. There, "prices shown to buyers on search and detail pages must equal the amount paid at the time of payment". "If you don't use auto-converted pricing, prices must include tax." The US and Canada are not on the tax-inclusive list, so sales tax/GST is added on top. Google is responsible for "determining, charging, and remitting VAT" for EU and UK purchases made through Play Billing. In Australia, Google handles GST if the developer is outside Australia. — [Play Console Help 138000](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138000?hl=en)
- A "Default price includes tax" checkbox existed (to keep the same tax-inclusive price across, e.g., the EU) but appeared in the pricing-template flow (now retired), so it may be outdated. — [6334373](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6334373?hl=en)
- New subscription prices take effect "within a few hours for all new purchases". Prices can be set programmatically via `monetization.subscriptions.patch` (`RegionalBasePlanConfig`). — [Android Developers: Change subscription prices](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/price-changes)
- Google Play has no fixed price-point grid like Apple's. You type any amount within per-currency min/max ranges ("currencies and price ranges allowed by country"). — [6334373](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6334373?hl=en)

### Inferences
- If you enter $22.99 as the default price on Play, the EU price is computed once (FX + VAT + rounding) and then stays fixed until you refresh it. Over time Play and App Store EU prices can diverge even if both started from the same USD price.
- Since you can type any price on Play, you can mirror the exact App Store local prices (e.g., €23.99, £21.99) for parity, or set Android-specific ones. Remember that EU/UK/AU prices typed manually must include VAT/GST.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the exact min/max price ranges per currency or Google's rounding rules ("locally relevant pricing patterns" are not defined in the docs).

## 3. Commissions: Apple SBP, Google Play tiers, EU DMA, US external links (Epic v. Apple), web checkout

### Takeaway
For a sub-$1M developer, the effective store cut on in-app purchases is **15% on both stores in Western markets**. Apple: SBP 15% on IAP, including the EU under the single terms in force since Oct 1, 2026. Google (US/UK/EEA since June 30, 2026): 10% service fee + 5% Play billing fee. Linking out to web checkout costs 0% on iOS in the US for now (contested in court), 10–15% in the EU, and 10% on Google, plus Stripe fees and VAT handling. For a small app, the net saving is a few points and usually doesn't justify a second billing stack yet.

### Cited Findings
**Apple Small Business Program**
- "a reduced commission rate of 15% on paid apps and Apple In-App Purchases". Eligible if you and your Associated Developer Accounts earned ≤ $1M in proceeds the prior calendar year and so far this year (new developers also qualify). If you go over $1M during the year, "the standard commission rate will apply to future sales". You can re-qualify the year after a year below $1M. Proceeds = "sales net of Apple's commission and certain taxes and adjustments". Enrollment is not automatic: the Account Holder applies. — [Apple: App Store Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)
- The same page: "For developers on the alternative terms in the EU in the App Store Small Business Program and for subscriptions after their first year, Apple will offer a further reduced commission of 10%." — [same](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/) (wording may predate the Oct 2026 EU single terms. Read it as the 10% alternative payment / link-out rate for program members, see below.)

**Apple EU (DMA) — new single business terms, announced Aug 18, 2026, effective Oct 1, 2026**
- App Store apps using Apple IAP: 26% standard. **15% "for the vast majority of developers, including those in the App Store Small Business Program… and for auto-renewing subscriptions after their first year"**. Alternative in-app payment processing: 20% (10% for program members). Link-out to complete purchases: 15% (10% for program members). Apps distributed outside the App Store: 5% "Core Technology Commission". Per-install Core Technology Fee, initial acquisition fee and store services fee are eliminated. Every EU developer moves to one set of terms, and you must keep your chosen payment options for 12 months. — [Apple Newsroom, Aug 2026](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/08/apple-announces-changes-for-apps-in-the-european-union/)
- The European Commission welcomed the changes and will monitor implementation. Epic called them "junk fees". — [MacRumors, Aug 19 2026](https://macrumors.com/2026/08/19/ec-epic-apple-app-store-changes); [TNW, Aug 18 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/apple-eu-app-store-fees-dma-core-technology-commission)
- Background: the EC fined Apple €500M in April 2025 over DMA anti-steering. — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/apple-eu-app-store-fees-dma-core-technology-commission)
- EU link-out commission applies to purchases made within 7 days of the tap, with monthly reporting. IAP must have equal or greater prominence. — [RevenueCat, App-to-web purchase guidelines, Dec 9 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/app-to-web-purchase-guidelines)

**Apple US — Epic v. Apple**
- April 30, 2025: Judge Gonzalez Rogers found Apple in contempt. Since then Apple has been under a **zero-commission regime on US external purchase links**. Dec 2025: the Ninth Circuit upheld the contempt finding but replaced the outright ban with a standard allowing only fees "genuinely and reasonably necessary" for coordinating external links, and remanded the rate to the district court. June 2026: SCOTUS agreed to hear one civil-contempt question (No. 25-1311). Briefing runs to Dec 14, 2026. Argument is not expected before Jan 2027 and a ruling likely not before June 2027. As of Sept 2026, the commission developers can be charged right now is "Zero." — [TechTimes, Sep 15 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/327527/20260915/app-store-commission-limbo-enters-new-phase-apples-epic-merits-brief-opens-scotus-fight.htm); [Fenwick: Ninth Circuit largely upholds](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/ninth-circuit-largely-upholds-ruling-in-epic-v-apple); [SCOTUS cert petition PDF](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-1311/409561/20260526163506450_2026-05-26%20Apple-Epic%20--%20Cert%20Petition%20and%20Appendix.pdf)
- Aug 13, 2026: Apple's proffer to the district court proposed link-out commissions of **15% standard, 10% for renewals/partner programs, 5% for Small Business Program apps**. Apple also said that under the Ninth Circuit's "necessary costs" standard it would charge 0%. SCOTUS denied Apple's request to pause the fee-setting proceedings. — [9to5Mac, Aug 13 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/13/apple-proposes-commissions-of-up-to-15-for-off-app-store-purchases-in-the-us/); [AppleInsider, Aug 13 2026](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/08/13/apples-latest-commission-rates-for-external-app-store-purchases-havent-satisfied-epic). **Conflict:** 9to5Mac describes Apple as "currently" charging 27% on off-store purchases. TechTimes and RevenueCat ("currently 0%, subject to the court process") say 0%. The 27% is the pre-April-2025 compliance rate that triggered the contempt finding.
- During the 27% period, 27% + Stripe 2.9% + $0.30 gave no meaningful saving vs IAP at 15–30%. — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/327527/20260915/app-store-commission-limbo-enters-new-phase-apples-epic-merits-brief-opens-scotus-fight.htm)
- In the US, web purchase links can be added without applying to Apple's external purchase program. Third-party checkout inside the app is still not generally allowed. RevenueCat's Web Purchase Button has been supported on the US App Store since May 2025 and on Play since Oct 2025. — [RevenueCat guidelines](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/app-to-web-purchase-guidelines); [RevenueCat search summary of web purchase button docs](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/tools/paywalls/creating-paywalls/web-purchase-button)

**Google Play — new fee structure (US, UK, EEA from June 30, 2026; Australia & Japan from Sept 30, 2026)**
- Service fee and billing fee are separated: "the service fee starts at 10% on your first $1M (USD) in annual earnings", whatever the payment method (Play Billing, alternative billing, external web links). "This 10% service fee also applies to all auto-renewing subscriptions." "In the United States, United Kingdom, and the European Economic Area, the billing fee is set at 5%". The billing fee does not apply to alternative billing or external web links. — [Android Developers Blog, June 2026 "play-expanded-billing"](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/play-expanded-billing.html)
- Help Center table for AU/EEA/JP/UK/US (read from a flattened table, so row/column alignment is approximate): auto-renewing subs 10% + 5% billing fee. "Other transactions" new installs 20% + 5%, existing installs 25% + 5% (20% for external web links). Program (Play Games Level Up / Apps Experience) rates are 15%/20% + 5%. A "First $1M (USD) of annual earnings" row exists. "New install" means first install or first update on or after the regional rollout date. — [Play Console Help: Service fees (112622)](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)
- All other markets (incl. **Canada**) until the global rollout: "15% for the first $1M (USD) revenue earned by the developer each year" (15% tier, enrollment required), 30% above, and "15% for automatically renewing subscription products". — [same](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)
- Google US External Content Links: subs 10%, other purchases 10% on the first $1M then 20% (15% with partner programs). "Fees and transaction reporting apply from October 1, 2026." EEA External Offers: same 10% rates, but you can't also offer Play Billing unless you use the newer billing-choice program. UK Billing Choice: 10%, and Play Billing must be offered alongside. — [RevenueCat guidelines, Dec 9 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/app-to-web-purchase-guidelines). Some secondary blogs claim US external purchases were at $0 pending court rulings. Not verified, treat as conflicting.

**Web checkout economics (RevenueCat / Stripe)**
- Stripe: 2.9% + 30¢ (US) + 0.7% Stripe Billing + optional Stripe Tax. On a $5 weekly sub this comes to "close to 9%". RevenueCat's own A/B test (IAP only vs IAP + web): conversion to paying 7.8% vs 7.4% (−5.1%), refunded customers 28 vs 38 (+35.7%), more subscribers set to renew (attributed to users forgetting to cancel). Recommendation: treat app-to-web as a "narrow, constrained experiment". It suits apps with a large US base, high revenue per payer and paid acquisition. Paddle / Stripe Managed Payments can act as merchant of record. — [RevenueCat, "…here's why you shouldn't use it", Apr 14 2026](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/why-you-shouldnt-use-app-to-web/)
- For alternative payments, the developer is responsible for "determining if a transaction is taxable; charging and collecting the taxes…; remitting the taxes". — [ASC Help: Commissions, fees and taxes](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/making-payments-to-apple/commissions-fees-and-taxes/)

### Inferences
- Dian Dian on IAP today: Apple 15% (SBP), Google 10% + 5% = 15% in US/UK/EEA/(AU), and 15% (15% tier) in Canada. **The store cut is the same on both platforms, so commission is not a reason to price Android differently.**
- Web checkout for a yearly $8.99: Stripe ≈ 2.9% + $0.30 ($0.56, about 6.2%) + 0.7% Billing ≈ 6.9%, vs Apple SBP 15%. That saves about 8 points ($0.73/sale) at 0% Apple link-out in the US, minus the ~5% conversion loss and extra refunds. In the EU, the Apple link-out is 10% for SBP members + Stripe, so roughly no saving. A French seller using Stripe as merchant would also have to handle EU VAT (OSS) itself unless it uses a merchant of record. For a small indie with mostly non-US users, the conclusion is to stay on IAP for now and revisit if/when (a) the US share is large and (b) the SCOTUS/district-court rate is settled (2027).
- Apple's proposed US link-out rate for SBP (5%) would leave about 3 points of margin after Stripe, which is not enough to matter at small scale.

### Gaps
- Whether Google's "10% on first $1M" in US/UK/EEA requires prior enrollment in the 15%-tier / account-group declaration was not confirmed. Check Play Console → "Service fee" enrollment status.
- Google's fees above $1M for "other transactions" (lifetime IAP) are read from a flattened table and may be misaligned. Irrelevant below $1M.
- Canada and Australia link-out rules on Apple were not found (presumably not allowed outside specific programs).
- Final US link-out rate on Apple: unknown until the district court sets it and SCOTUS rules (not before mid-2027).

## 4. Changing prices: existing subscribers, consent rules, grandfathering, A/B testing with low traffic

### Takeaway
Both stores let you **grandfather** existing subscribers (Apple: "keep current price"; Google: legacy price cohort, the default) or move them to the new price. Apple only asks for consent if the increase is **both** >50% and more than ~$5/period (non-annual) or more than ~$50/year (annual), or if it's the second increase in 12 months. On Google, opt-out (notice-only) increases are allowed if the increase is ≤ the greater of 50% or $0.17/day, once per 365 days. **On both stores, Germany, Austria and Poland (and Romania on Google) always require opt-in.** For a cheap annual plan like $8.99 → $14.99–$19.99, most Western subscribers would only be notified, not asked. Price decreases apply to everyone automatically. RevenueCat Experiments (offerings as variants) is the tool for A/B tests, but at indie traffic levels only large price differences can be detected within a reasonable time.

### Cited Findings
**Apple**
- Increase options: "A: Keep the current price for existing subscribers. Anyone who subscribed before the start date of this price change won't be affected. Subscribers whose subscription expires can resubscribe at the preserved price within 60 days of expiration." or "B: Apply the price change to existing subscribers…" ASC indicates per country whether consent is required. "Once a price increase goes into effect, it can't be reversed." Un-preserving prices later also "can't be reversed." — [ASC Help: Manage pricing for auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/manage-pricing-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions)
- Consent needed if any of these applies: the subscriber is in a region requiring consent for any price change; "The increase is more than 50% of the current price **and** the difference exceeds approximately US$5 per period (non-annual) or US$50 per year (annual)"; or "The subscriber had a price increase for that subscription within the past 12 months." — [same](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/manage-pricing-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions)
- Per-currency thresholds (non-annual / annual): USD 5/50, most EUR countries (FR, BE, ES, IT, NL, IE, PT, FI…) 5/50, GBP 5/50, CAD 7/70, AUD 8/80. **Consent always required: Austria, Germany, Poland, Republic of Korea.** "These thresholds are subject to change based on changes in tax or foreign exchange rates." — [ASC Help: Auto-renewable subscription price increase thresholds](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/auto-renewable-subscription-price-increase-thresholds)
- Consumer-facing wording (Apple Support, Dec 13 2023): increases without opt-in only if they don't exceed "approximately USD $5 and 50% of the subscription price, or USD $50 and 50% for annual subscriptions", "no more than once per year", "where permitted by law". Subscribers who don't opt in when required do not renew. — [Apple Support HT213252](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT213252). The rule dates from 2022. I found no 2024–2025 change to the thresholds.
- Notification timing. Consent needed: first email 60 days before renewal for 2-, 3-, 6-month and annual plans, 27 days for monthly. Consent not needed: email 27 days before renewal, in-app message at first launch in the notice window, push 7 days before if the in-app message wasn't seen. If the change falls inside the minimum notice period (27 days monthly, 30 days longer plans), subscribers renew once more at the old price. — [ASC Help subs pricing](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/manage-pricing-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions)
- Decreases: "existing subscriptions will automatically renew at the lower price. You don't have the option to preserve the higher price for existing subscribers." A decrease "can't be reversed" (only a later increase). — [same](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/manage-pricing-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions)

**Google Play**
- By default, existing subscribers are "placed into a legacy price cohort where they continue to pay their original base plan price when they renew". You can "end a legacy price cohort" per region (Play Console or `basePlans.migratePrices`). — [Android Developers: Change subscription prices](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/price-changes)
- Opt-in increases (default): effective after "an advance notification period of 37 days". Play notifies 30 days before the charge. There is a 7-day freeze in which you can cancel by reverting. Users who don't accept are "automatically canceled and expired on that renewal date". Opt-out increases: the charge applies after 30 or 60 days' notice (country-dependent). You "must show users an in-app notice". — [same](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/price-changes)
- Opt-out eligibility: max increase is the **greater of 50% of the current price or 17 USD cents per day**. "Each subscription base plan can only have a single opt-out price increase per country/region in the last 365 days." Your ToS must reserve the right to raise prices. You must show a prominent in-app notice ≥ 30 days before, with old/new price, date and how to cancel. — [Play Console Help 12154973](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12154973?hl=en)
- Opt-out countries (30-day notice): US, CA, GB, AU, FR, ES, IT, NL, BE, IE, SE and most of the EU. **Not available: Austria, Germany, Poland, Romania.** — [Play Console Help 13542779](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13542779)
- Increases that don't meet the region/frequency/amount criteria are automatically downgraded to opt-in. — [Play Console Help 140504](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/140504?hl=en)
- Decreases (ending a cohort with a lower price) apply at the next renewal, with an email to users. Don't change prices of products with active subscribers for testing; use Play Billing Lab + license testers. — [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/price-changes)

**A/B testing**
- RevenueCat Experiments: "1 Control (Variant A) and up to 3 Treatment variants". The docs recommend 2 variants. New customers are enrolled by default ("the moment they open the app for the first time"), with an option to include existing customers. Audience filters: country, platform, app version, etc. "Enrolled customers are split evenly between all variants". Minimum audience 10%. When stopped, enrolled customers get the default Offering at the next paywall view, and results keep refreshing for 400 days. For App Store, the docs "recommend setting up new products to test as a new Subscription Group in App Store Connect". The tool estimates required customers and duration from the 28-day baseline conversion and the Minimum Detectable Effect. — [RevenueCat docs: Configuring experiments](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/tools/experiments-v1/configuring-experiments-v1)
- Experiments "is available to Pro & Enterprise customers" (per the overview page). Check the current RevenueCat plan. — [RevenueCat docs: Experiments overview](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/tools/experiments-v1)
- Google Play native price experiments: **one-time products only** (so usable for the Android lifetime product, not the subs). Up to 2 price variants + control, maximum 6 months. Prices revert 14 days after significance is reached. Default confidence is 90% and default MDE 30%. Significance is computed across all countries combined. Only one experiment per country at a time. — [Play Console Help 13343030](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13343030?hl=en)
- Freemium median download→paid by day 35 is 2.1% (hard paywall 10.7%). — [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026 (via secondary summary)](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Practical room for Dian Dian:** raising the yearly plan from $8.99 to, say, $14.99 or $19.99 is +$6 to +$11/year: well under Apple's $50 annual threshold, and under Google's $0.17/day (about $62/yr) cap. Existing subscribers in US/CA/UK/AU/FR/ES/IT/NL… could be moved with notice only (once per 12 months). In DE/AT/PL (both stores) and RO (Google), they would have to opt in, and those who don't would churn. The cleaner path is to **grandfather existing subscribers** (Apple option A, Google legacy cohort) and apply new prices to new buyers only. That keeps goodwill and avoids consent-driven churn. The lifetime product has no "existing subscriber" issue.
- Apple's annual threshold test is "more than 50% **and** more than ~$50". For any annual plan below about $100, the dollar condition can't be met, so annual increases never need consent outside DE/AT/PL/KR, apart from the once-per-12-months rule.
- **Low-traffic testing math** (my calculation, standard two-proportion test at 95% confidence / 80% power): with a 2% baseline paywall conversion, detecting a 20% relative change (2.0% → 2.4%) needs about 21,000 users **per variant**. Detecting a 50% change needs about 3,800 per variant. Unless Dian Dian has tens of thousands of new users per month, run at most **2 variants with large price gaps** (e.g., yearly $8.99 vs $14.99). Use revenue per new user (not conversion rate) as the decision metric, and/or do sequential before/after changes by market. The Play native experiment (lifetime only, 30% default MDE) is a free extra signal on Android.
- On iOS, a new subscription group per tested offering avoids users seeing conflicting prices in their subscription settings. This adds product-ID sprawl. Plan RevenueCat entitlements so all groups map to the same "premium" entitlement.

### Gaps
- I did not find a 2024–2025 Apple rule change allowing increases without opt-in. The current mechanism is the 2022 one (support page dated Dec 2023). If the brief's "2024–2025 changes" refers to something specific, I could not confirm it.
- RevenueCat plan availability of Experiments in late 2026 (pricing tiers change often) was not verified.

## 5. iOS vs Android price differences

### Takeaway
Nothing in either store's pricing docs requires price parity across platforms. Charging less on Android is common (historical Adapty data: Android averages are lower), and Android users convert much less at the top of the funnel (0.9% vs 2.6% download→paid). Commissions are now equal (15%), so a lower Android price is purely a demand-side bet. The main risks are cross-platform users and entitlement arbitrage: since RevenueCat entitlements are account-wide, a user can buy on the cheaper platform and unlock both.

### Cited Findings
- Historical prices (Adapty data 2021–22, article Jan 31 2023, possibly outdated): average iOS vs Android: weekly $6 vs $5, monthly $6 vs $5, annual $27 vs $18. "developers tend to price Android subscriptions lower than the ones on iOS." Regional iOS annual: US $26.1, EU $31.0, UK $40.0. — [Adapty: How to price mobile in-app subscriptions](https://adapty.io/blog/how-to-price-mobile-in-app-subscriptions/)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026: median download→paid at day 35 is **0.9% on Android vs 2.6% on iOS**. Trial→paid is 32.5% vs 32.6%, so the gap is in stage one (fewer Android users start trials/purchases). "Google Play does not send an equivalent system notification" before a trial converts. [Corrected 2026-11-23: wrong — Google Play emails users when a trial is about to end (Play Help 2476088); see `docs/research/notes/Vérification des docs 2026-11-23/verification.md`] The article recommends checking that the Android default offer includes the trial, and building your own trial-end reminder. It recommends **no Android-specific price cut**. — [RevenueCat blog, "The Android paywall conversion gap", Mar 25 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/android-paywall-gap/)
- Nearly a third of Google Play cancellations are involuntary billing failures, vs 14% on iOS. The App Store accounts for about 77% of new subscription app launches. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 (via search summary)](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)
- RevenueCat Experiments can filter enrollment by platform, which allows platform-specific price tests. — [RevenueCat docs](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/tools/experiments-v1/configuring-experiments-v1)
- Apple 3.1.3(b) Multiplatform Services: apps may unlock content bought on other platforms if those items are also available as IAP in the iOS app. — [Apple Developer News (guideline updates)](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=xqk627qu) (via search summary; the guideline doesn't address price parity).

### Inferences
- **Allowed:** each store only governs its own products. Different product IDs/prices per store are normal in RevenueCat (one entitlement, platform-specific products in the same Offering).
- **Is lower Android pricing justified?** Equal 15% commissions remove the cost argument. The 0.9% vs 2.6% conversion gap looks like a funnel/trial issue (RevenueCat), not proven price elasticity. Higher involuntary churn on Play lowers Android LTV regardless of price. The evidence that Western Android users are more price-sensitive is circumstantial (lower averages chosen by developers), not experimental. A platform-filtered RevenueCat experiment (or Play's native lifetime experiment) is the way to check this for Dian Dian rather than assuming.
- **Risks:** (1) a user with an account on both platforms sees two prices, which can hurt trust if noticed; (2) arbitrage: buy the cheaper Android lifetime and get premium on iOS through the shared account entitlement, which drains revenue from the higher-price platform; (3) outside the US, Apple's anti-steering rules mean you can't tell iOS users in the app that it's cheaper elsewhere. Linking/mentioning is allowed in the US since May 2025, and in the EU under the link-out terms with commission. A modest gap (e.g., 10–20%) limits arbitrage incentives. Keeping the lifetime price at parity while testing only subscription prices also limits the risk.

### Gaps
- I found no recent (2024–2026) controlled study or public A/B result comparing a lower Android price vs iOS in Western markets. Adapty's platform price split is 2021–22 data, and its 2026 report keeps platform breakdowns behind the full report.
- I found no explicit Apple or Google rule text stating that cross-platform price parity is not required (inferred from absence in pricing docs).

## 6. Psychological price endings and per-country norms

### Takeaway
Store auto-conversion already snaps prices to the local convention (.99 in USD/GBP/CAD/AUD/EUR, round numbers in Japan, etc.). The bigger question is the price level per country, not the ending. Europe and especially the UK end up priced well above the US in sticker terms, while many apps deliberately price the UK about 10% below the US.

### Cited Findings
- "the US, the UK and Canada follow a pricing convention ending in .99, while China has price points ending in 8. In Japan, we see even, rounded price points, and in Korea, we see a price like 9,901 that starts with a repeating digit". The ASC price menu first shows 25 "most common convention" points per storefront. — [Apple Tech Talk 110364](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/110364); [ASC Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-in-app-purchases/set-a-price-for-an-in-app-purchase)
- Google applies "country-specific pricing patterns" to auto-converted prices. — [Play Console Help 6334373](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6334373?hl=en)
- Adapty 2026 (2025 pricing): average weekly subscription price UK $13.43, France $11.72, Germany $11.72, Italy $11.71, Spain $11.62, US $9.99, Canada $9.46, Australia $10.05. "European apps charge 29–39% more than North American ones". 2025 global medians: $7.48/week, $12.99/month, $38.42/year. — [Adapty: State of in-app subscriptions 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions/)
- Apple's suggested UK price is +26% vs the US, while most apps price about 10% lower in the UK. Duolingo charges more in Spain and France than in the US (perceived value). About 40% of brands still "set it and forget it". — [RevenueCat, price localization guide, Sep 4 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/price-localization-for-apps)
- Evidence on .99 is mixed: a 2015 report found no correlation between price endings and sales in other currencies. — [TechCrunch, Jul 26 2015](https://techcrunch.com/2015/07/26/when-selling-virtual-products-abroad-dont-put-prices-on-autopilot) (via search summary, old)

### Inferences
- For EUR, GBP, CAD and AUD, keep x.99 endings (store defaults, and what users expect). Germany/France display "9,99 €" automatically; the store handles the formatting.
- Because EU/UK/AU stickers include VAT/GST, "$8.99 ↔ €8.99 ↔ £8.99" is **not** equalization. It is a deliberate net discount in Europe of about 15–20% (VAT) on top of the FX difference. That can be a valid positioning choice (simple, "same number" prices), but it should be decided explicitly per storefront in ASC (manual override, which stops auto re-equalization) and typed explicitly on Play.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous 2024–2026 data on .99 vs .49 vs round endings for app subscriptions in specific Western markets.
