# How consumer tracking apps monetise statistics and insights (free/premium split, teasers, contextual paywalls) and what year-in-review recaps do for engagement, sharing and conversion

Context for the report writer: Dian Dian is an indie iOS/Android "year in pixels" tracker with about 200 accounts. It is freemium: 3 trackers are free, and premium adds unlimited trackers, stats, themes, image export and no ads. From December 2026 premium costs $2.99/month, $14.99/year or $29.99 lifetime, with a 7-day trial. The stats wall is the most-seen paywall (curiosity taps), and every trial since late October came within minutes of a stats wall. The stats themselves are thin (filling counts only). The plan is to show one real stat free as a teaser, also inside an occasional premium popup after the 7th and 30th filled day, and keep richer stats premium. A year-end recap tied to the image export is under consideration.

Evidence labels used below:
- [INDEP]: independent or academic measured work.
- [COMPANY]: an app maker's own measured numbers about its own feature, which is first-party but not audited.
- [VENDOR]: monetisation vendor data or case study (RevenueCat, Adapty, Superwall). Samples are large, but vendors have a commercial interest, and case studies usually omit baselines and sample sizes.
- [OFFICIAL]: the developer's own help page or changelog.
- [STORE]: App Store listing.
- [PRESS]: journalism reporting facts or reactions.
- [ANECDOTE]: a single developer, blogger or reviewer.
- [UNVERIFIED]: seen only in a search snippet or a secondary source that I could not open.

All pages were consulted on 2026-11-23 unless a date is given.

## Q1. Free vs premium split for stats and insights in comparable apps, and whether they use locked or blurred previews or one free insight

### Takeaway
Four patterns dominate, and none of them is "one free insight":
- **Short window free, long window paid**: Bearable gives 30 days free; 60, 90 and 365 days are premium.
- **Headline free, interpretation paid**: Daylio's basic charts are free and its advanced stats are paid. Oura's scores are free and the detail is paid. Strava shows the top 10 of a leaderboard free and the full ranking paid.
- **All stats free, money made elsewhere**: Pixels, How We Feel, Apple Fitness, Spotify Wrapped and Goodreads.
- **Paid upfront**: Streaks.

None of the official feature pages I could read describes a blurred preview or "one free insight" mechanic for stats. Dian Dian's teaser plan is therefore not a copy of a documented competitor pattern. The closest analogues are Bearable's time window and Oura's free headline scores.

### Inferences
- In this set, the market splits into three groups by what is free. All the "stats are free" apps (Pixels, How We Feel, Apple Fitness, Spotify, Goodreads) earn their money elsewhere. Every premium-stats app keeps *some* view of the user's own data free:
  - basic charts and CSV (Daylio)
  - 30-day trends and comparison (Bearable)
  - headline scores and data download (Oura)
  - top 10 (Strava)
- Today Dian Dian's free tier shows almost nothing statistical, so it sits at the stingy end. A free teaser stat would bring it in line with category norms rather than being generous.
- Bearable's time window (30 days free, 60/90/365 premium) maps naturally onto a year-in-pixels app. "This month" stats could be free, with "all year", "compare months" and correlations premium. It is the most documented split close to Dian Dian's use case.
- The interpretation layer is what gets charged for: correlations, "what affects your mood", long-range trends and best/worst weekday (Daylio's advanced stats, Bearable's Impacts, Oura's detail). Raw counts are free nearly everywhere. Dian Dian's current premium stats (filling counts) are exactly the kind of number competitors give away, which supports the plan to enrich premium stats before leaning on them.
- No source showed a stats tracker using a blurred stats screen or a single free insight. That is not proof that none do: the in-app paywall UI is rarely documented on help pages.

### Gaps
- No in-app screenshots or teardown of Daylio, Bearable, Habitify or Finch paywall screens were available, so I cannot confirm whether any of them blur stats or show locked cards in-app.
- Whether Daylio's Year in Pixels and monthly report are free is unresolved; sources conflict.
- The exact free vs paid stats split for Habitify, HabitKit and Finch comes only from third-party reviews.
- Duolingo's in-app stats split (free vs Super/Max) was not researched.
- Whoop's US price for One was not verified.

## Q2. Evidence on "preview then paywall", teaser paywalls, and contextual paywalls on a feature the user just touched vs generic paywalls

### Takeaway
Large vendor datasets show that onboarding paywalls convert best per view. Paywalls seen later still bring 38% of revenue overall and 48% in Lifestyle/Utilities, and freemium apps have a long tail of late conversions. So a contextual stats wall is a real revenue surface for a freemium tracker.

Evidence that a *preview* or *contextual* paywall beats a generic one is limited to vendor case studies, each claiming about +50% with no baseline or sample size. The only independent study (news paywalls) found that showing more teaser content *reduced* subscriptions, and that blurred previews had no significant effect. The teaser should provoke curiosity, not satisfy it.

### Cited Findings
**Large-sample benchmarks (paywall timing and freemium)**
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat, *State of Subscription Apps 2026* (2025 data; 115k+ apps, $16B+ revenue):
  - Download-to-paid by day 35 is **2.1% median for freemium** (top quartile >4.5%) vs **10.7% for hard paywalls**.
  - Health & Fitness: download-to-trial (D30) 6.9%, download-to-paid (D35) 2.9%, trial-to-paid 37.7%. **82.1% of H&F trial starts happen on day 0.**
  - About 50.6% of all conversions happen on day 0, but **freemium apps have a longer tail: 23% of conversions happen six or more weeks after download**.
  - A consultant quoted in the report cites a reverse-trial test that raised a freemium app from 0.4% to 4.5% conversion (single anecdote).
  - Source: [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- [VENDOR] Adapty, *State of Paywalls 2026* (78,000+ paywalls, 30,000+ App Store apps; article dated 1 November 2026):
  - 93% of first purchases happen on install day.
  - 60% of buyers subscribe on their first paywall view.
  - **Onboarding paywalls convert about 4× better per view (0.85% vs 0.23%), i.e. $170 vs $40 per 1,000 views.**
  - About **7 in 10 paywall views happen after the first session, and those later paywalls bring 38% of revenue**. In **Lifestyle and Utilities, later paywalls bring 48% of revenue**, slightly more than onboarding.
  - Only **40% of Health & Fitness buyers purchase on their first paywall view**; they need several views.
  - Only 4% of apps show a second paywall after the first is closed, and it converts at **0.88× the main paywall**.
  - Hard paywalls: 4.7% of viewers start a trial vs 3.2% on soft paywalls.
  - The report does **not** separately quantify feature-gated or contextual placements, or "show value first" previews.
  - Source: [Adapty, State of Paywalls 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/paywall-optimization-tests/)
- [VENDOR] RevenueCat, "Context wins: the hidden third dimension of paywall targeting" (Felix Krause, 4 December 2025):
  - Median apps convert 1.9% of downloads to paid within 35 days, vs 4.6% for top performers; the post attributes much of the gap to *when* the paywall appears.
  - Case studies linked to a context-detection vendor: Blackbox (puzzle game) gained +50% revenue in peak periods by changing *when* paywalls appeared. Wizz cut prompt volume by about 50% and timed offers to receptive moments, giving **+81% on the primary offer flow and about +20% LTV**.
  - No methodology or control groups are given, and the post does not study paywalls triggered by touching a locked feature.
  - Source: [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/contextual-paywall-targeting)

**Preview and contextual paywalls (case studies)**
- [VENDOR] Superwall, **Unchaind** (faith-based recovery app): an A/B test of a text paywall (headline and bullets) vs a **visual app-preview paywall showing premium features**. The preview gave **conversion >+50% and estimated ARPU +64%**. No sample size, duration or significance given. Dated 11 December 2025 — [Superwall, Unchaind](https://superwall.com/case-studies/unchaind)
- [VENDOR] Superwall, **JourneyStamp** (camera app): replaced one generic paywall with context-specific ones. It showed discounts at high-engagement moments "such as after a user used a premium feature" and wrote copy tailored to the features the user had already used. Conversion rose **+50% to 7.3%**, with no baseline given. Dated 15 February 2026 — [Superwall, JourneyStamp](https://superwall.com/case-studies/journeystamp)
- [VENDOR] Superwall, **Linearity Curve**: an opt-in trial paywall produced fewer trial starts but **+10% trial-to-paid**, a reminder to measure paid conversion, not just trial starts — [Superwall, Linearity Curve](https://superwall.com/case-studies/linearity-curve)
- [ANECDOTE] **GainFrame** (fitness app): a new paywall with partially unlocked, **blurred** feature previews (per-muscle breakdown blurred behind the upgrade button). It converted 30.1% of paywall views to trial starts vs 24.1% before, which the author says is "not statistically significant yet" — [GainFrame blog](https://gainframe.app/blog/trial-conversion-21-to-44/)
- [UNVERIFIED, third-party] **Clear**: swapping a feature carousel for a preview of the user's first-week programme took paywall conversion from 20% to 30%. The same post argues upfront paywalls without a "taste" of the product convert badly — [dev.to / paywallpro](https://dev.to/paywallpro/when-upfront-paywalls-work-and-when-they-hurt-conversion-54k1)

**Independent evidence on teasers**
- [INDEP, academic] Xu, Thurman, Berhami, Strasser Ceballos & Fehling, *Journalism Studies*. Data: behavioural records from 21 German/Austrian news sites in 2022, covering 3.5M click cases and 28.5k subscription cases.
  - Paywall teasers that gave away content **reduced subscribing**: a deck cut the odds by **86.3%** and an intro by **72.2%**.
  - **Images and blurred article previews had no statistically significant effect.**
  - **Discounts multiplied subscription odds by 3.35**, and trial length had no significant effect.
  - The authors suggest visitors who get "enough information" from the teaser feel no need to pay.
  - Covered 10 February 2025 — [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/02/less-is-more-and-discounts-work-a-new-study-looks-at-the-minutiae-of-paywall-strategy/)
- [VENDOR] Adapty warns that a conversion-only A/B winner can mislead because the revenue effect lands weeks later, and that a cheaper price almost always wins on conversion. In its data, 31% of visual/copy tests improved conversion and 35% increased LTV — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/paywall-optimization-tests/)

### Inferences
- Dian Dian's own pattern matches the vendor data: trials come within minutes of a stats wall, Health & Fitness buyers need several views, and freemium has a late tail. Later, contextual paywalls carry a large share of revenue in Lifestyle/Utilities-type apps, and the stats wall is the app's highest-intent surface. Improving what that wall shows is likely worth more than adding new paywall placements.
- The two "+50%" case studies point the same way: show the actual premium thing (Unchaind) at the moment the user wants it (JourneyStamp). Both are vendor-published and lack baselines, so treat them as directional only.
- The news-paywall study is the only independent evidence. Its lesson for a teaser stat is to **pose a question rather than answer it**. A free stat that fully satisfies curiosity ("Your average mood is 3.8") may lower conversion. A free stat that opens a thread ("Your best days are Saturdays", followed by locked "what makes them better") keeps the reason to pay.
- Blurring is neutral at best in the only measured case (news). The GainFrame lift is not significant. A visible locked list of named premium insights ("Best weekday", "Streak record", "Mood vs sleep", ...) may work as well as blur and is cheaper to build. This is not tested.
- The popup at the 7th and 30th filled day fits the freemium long tail (23% of conversions after six weeks). Adapty's 0.88× second-paywall figure suggests repeat exposures keep most of their efficiency, provided frequency stays low (Wizz: fewer, better-timed prompts did better).
- At about 200 accounts, Dian Dian cannot A/B test with significance. Decisions will rest on these external directional results plus funnel logging (stats wall view → trial → paid).

### Gaps
- No rigorous or independent study of *blurred stats* or a "one free insight" teaser in a tracking app was found.
- No source compares a feature-triggered (contextual) paywall against an onboarding or generic one with numbers for the same app.
- Neither RevenueCat nor Adapty splits Health & Fitness or Lifestyle conversion by freemium vs hard paywall in the excerpts available.
- Purchasely sources were not reached in this pass.

## Q3. Year-in-review and monthly recap features: effects on engagement, sharing, installs, reactivation and conversion; ship dates; free or paid

### Takeaway
Recaps reliably produce **sharing** and short-term **activity spikes** at large scale:
- Spotify: 200M users in 24 h, 500M shares, double-digit download and DAU jumps.
- Duolingo: millions of shares, more lessons, a spike in new users.
- Wikipedia app: the best-instrumented case. Year in Review drove 43–58% of new accounts in its window, return rate rose from 77% to 86%, and iOS donations went from 216 to 3,000. A partial-personalisation A/B test lifted donations +327%.

The big players ship in the **first days of December**. Almost all keep the recap **free**. Strava is the counter-example: it paywalled Year in Sport in December 2025, gave free users nothing, and drew public backlash. No source measured a recap's effect on *subscription* conversion.

### Cited Findings
**Spotify Wrapped (free for all users)**
- [COMPANY via PRESS] Wrapped 2025 launched **3 December 2025**:
  - **200M+ engaged users in the first 24 h** (viewed at least one story), +19% YoY. The previous year took 62 hours to reach 200M.
  - **Shared 500M+ times (+41% YoY)**, counting in-app shares, screenshots and downloads.
  - No download or premium-conversion figures given.
  - Source: [TechCrunch, 4 Dec 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day)
- [INDEP-ish, market-intelligence estimates] Sensor Tower measurements, which conflict across its own posts:
  - 2019: about **2.3M downloads in the 3 days after launch, +23%** vs a 1.9M average — [Fortune, 11 Dec 2019](https://fortune.com/2019/12/11/spotify-wrapped-playlist-app-download/)
  - A September 2024 post gives downloads **+90% day-over-day at the 2022 launch vs +60% in 2023**, and DAU +14% / +13% DoD — [Sensor Tower, "Spotify Wrapped is on a Roll"](https://sensortower.com/blog/spotify-wrapped-is-on-a-roll)
  - Another Sensor Tower post gives downloads +35% DoD in 2023 vs +23% in 2022, and DAU +14% DoD on 29 November 2023 — [Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/mmm-spotify-wrapped-drives-app-usage-ski-resort-apps-propelled-by-winter). **These figures conflict.**
- [VENDOR] MoEngage: Spotify downloads rose 21% in the Wrapped month of 2020 — [MoEngage](https://www.moengage.com/blog/spotify-wrapped-2020-app-downloads-engagement/)

**Duolingo Year in Review (free, in-app since 2020)**
- [COMPANY] Facts from [Duolingo blog, 8 Dec 2022](https://blog.duolingo.com/year-in-review-behind-the-scenes):
  - History: started **December 2019 as an email** (active days, time learning, words, lessons), moved in-app in 2020 with shareable stats.
  - Sharing: "millions" of users share their cards, and #Duolingo365 trends yearly.
  - In 2020, **top-10% XP earners made more than half of all shares**. Adding **XP percentile rankings** to the share card (2021) increased overall share rates.
  - Adding **"learner style" personality labels** "significantly boosted share rates", so that non-top learners also had something worth sharing. In 2022, sharing earned a badge.
  - Effects: "**lessons completed and time spent learning increased** after learners went through Year in Review", and in 2021 "a significant spike in **new users**" was attributed to shared stats. No figures published.
  - Localisation into 25 UI languages is the team's biggest yearly project.
- [COMPANY, older] A 2022 Duolingo shareholder letter reported a record number of shares for the 2021 edition (search summary; letter not opened) — [Duolingo investor materials](https://investors.duolingo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/duolingo-announces-record-results-fourth-quarter-and-full-year)
- [UNVERIFIED] The 2025 edition shows total XP, minutes and lessons, with a bonus for sharing (page returned 403; snippet only) — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/duolingo-year-in-review-2025-3621782). Also from a search summary of an investor document: the 2023 edition required **at least 10 lessons in the year**.

**Wikipedia app Year in Review (free; best-instrumented case, with A/B tests)**
- [COMPANY] Facts from [MediaWiki, Wikipedia Year in Review 2025](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Apps/Team/Wikipedia_Year_in_Review/2025_Year_in_Review):
  - Timing: the 2024 edition shipped on iOS in January 2025. The 2025 edition was planned for **1 December 2025** and was **removed from the app in February 2026**.
  - Accounts: **43% (iOS) and 58% (Android) of all new accounts in the period were created through Year in Review.** December account creation rose +166% (iOS) and +116% (Android) YoY.
  - Return rate: **86% vs a 77% baseline**. iOS 2-day retention went from 37% to 46%. The Android retention change (+2%) was explicitly *not* attributable to the feature.
  - A/B tests: personalising for logged-out users gave **+2% overall retention and +327% donation lift**. A reading-list follow-up on Android gave +0.1% retention, not significant.
  - Donations: iOS **3,000 vs 216 the previous year**, a donation rate of 1.1% vs 0.2%. 26% of donation-related feedback comments were negative.
  - Satisfaction: 95–96% satisfied or neutral, from about 180k survey responses.
  - Lesson the team drew: offer **partial personalisation to everyone and require an account for deeper insights**.
  - Inconsistency: one logged-in-share figure on the page (12% control vs 8% variant described as a "50% increase") reads as contradictory. Verify it before reuse.

**Strava Year in Sport (paywalled from December 2025)**

  - Source: [road.cc, 23 Dec 2025](https://road.cc/content/news/strava-year-sport-now-only-subscribers-317425)
- [PRESS] Free users get no teaser. - [PRESS] French coverage framed it as Strava "ose l'impensable : vous facturer pour consulter vos propres kilomètres parcourus" ("dares the unthinkable: charging you to see your own kilometres") — [Clubic](https://clubic.com//actualite-592360-strava-ose-l-impensable-vous-facturer-pour-consulter-vos-propres-kilometres-parcourus.html); also [T3, "Dear Strava, we have a paywall problem that's gone a step too far"](https://t3.com/tech/dear-strava-we-have-a-paywall-problem-thats-gone-a-step-too-far) (headline only, not opened)
- No figures on subscriptions gained or free-user churn from the change were found.

### Inferences
- **Free plus shareable is the norm, and it is what produces the measured effects.** Spotify, Duolingo, Wikipedia and Goodreads keep the recap free. Their gains (shares, new users, accounts, returns, donations) all come from free users seeing it. Strava's paywalled recap gives free users nothing to share and generated negative press. It is the only paywalled case found, and no upside was published.
- **The Wikipedia model is the closest template for a freemium app:** a partial recap for everyone and a deeper layer gated (there by an account, for Dian Dian by premium). The gate drove large account creation without hurting satisfaction (95–96%).
- **For Dian Dian:**
  - A free, watermarked year-in-pixels share card for all users, with the full recap and high-resolution or themed image export as premium, follows this template.
  - The image export is already premium, so the recap becomes a natural seasonal paywall moment (a contextual paywall per Q2) rather than a withheld gift.
- **Timing:** majors ship 1–3 December (Spotify 3 Dec 2025, Apple 2 Dec 2025, Wikipedia 1 Dec 2025, Goodreads mid-December). For a year-in-pixels app the year is incomplete until 31 December. Two options:
  - an early-December "your year so far" recap that rides the Wrapped wave of social sharing, then a complete version on 1 January;
  - only the 1 January version, which avoids the crowd but misses the trend.
  - This is a design choice; no data compares the two.
- **Eligibility thresholds are common:** Goodreads requires ≥3 books, Duolingo reportedly ≥10 lessons. Dian Dian should require a minimum of filled days, so that recaps are not empty and embarrassing.
- **Duolingo's share findings transfer directly:** comparison stats and identity labels ("learner style") make even average users want to share. A Dian Dian analogue could be a "colour of your year" or a mood-profile label.
- **Scale caveat:** the download and share effects were measured on apps whose recaps reach very large audiences (Spotify: 200M+ engaged users in 24 h; Wikipedia's survey alone drew about 180k responses). With about 200 accounts, a recap's realistic value is reactivating lapsed users, deepening habit and giving engaged free users a premium moment, not install growth.
- Wikipedia removed its recap in February. Recaps are seasonal, and a short window (about 2 months) is standard.

### Gaps
- No source measured the effect of a recap on **subscription conversion** or trial starts, for Spotify Premium, Strava or any indie app.
- No data was found on Daylio, Pixels, Bearable or Finch shipping a year-in-review or monthly recap, nor on any effect if they do.
- No numbers exist on what Strava's paywalled Year in Sport did to subscriptions or free-user churn.
- The 2026 editions (Wrapped 2026, Replay 2026) had not shipped or been checked as of 23 November 2026.
- Spotify's Wrapped data window (when counting stops) was not sourced in this pass.

## Q4. Pitfalls: user resentment of paywalled personal data, store-review complaints about locked stats, and rules on withholding user data

### Takeaway
The main documented risk is reputational. Paywalling *summaries of users' own data* reads as "charging me to see my own data": Strava drew French and UK press with exactly that framing, Oura is challenged by bypass apps, and indie competitors market "no paywall on your stats". Store rules do not forbid premium analytics. Apple only bars taking away functionality users already paid for and bait-and-switch. EU portability covers data the user *provided*, not derived analytics.

The safe line is to keep the user's raw entries viewable and exportable for free, then charge for interpretation and presentation. That is how Daylio (CSV), Bearable (CSV) and Oura (data download) operate.

### Cited Findings
**User and press reactions**
- [PRESS] Strava's paywalled recap drew reactions such as "pathetic" and "a step too far", and the French framing "vous facturer pour consulter vos propres kilomètres" ("charging you to see your own kilometres") — [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/news/strava-year-sport-now-only-subscribers-317425); [T3](https://t3.com/tech/dear-strava-we-have-a-paywall-problem-thats-gone-a-step-too-far); [Clubic](https://clubic.com//actualite-592360-strava-ose-l-impensable-vous-facturer-pour-consulter-vos-propres-kilometres-parcourus.html)
- [PRESS] Users built or recommended free third-party recap tools from Strava API or .FIT exports — [Gadgets & Wearables](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2025/12/20/strava-year-in-sport/)
- [PRESS] An open-source "Cracked Oura" app promises to skip the Oura membership. BGR flags a catch, and its reliability and legality are unverified — [BGR](https://bgr.com/2192032/access-oura-ring-data-without-subscription/). Headline only, not opened: "Oura defends subscription paywall of the Oura Ring 4" — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Oura-defends-subscription-paywall-of-the-Oura-Ring-4.1218222.0.html)
- [ANECDOTE, competitor-authored] A developer of a free Daylio alternative argues that paywalling "your own patterns" is backwards, because "showing you a chart of your own data doesn't cost the company anything per month". The argument is interested but echoes a common user sentiment — [dev.to](https://dev.to/astraedus/mood-trackers-without-a-subscription-free-no-paywall-3291)
- [ANECDOTE] Daylio's paid advanced stats drew complaints that they are **confusing**: terms such as "confidence" and "next day influence" go unexplained — [Justuseapp reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1194023242/daylio-journal/reviews). Choosing Therapy says most needs are met by the free version and that premium value is mainly PDF export — [Choosing Therapy](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/daylio-app-review/)
- [STORE] Pixels markets the absence of upsell pressure ("nor does it push you to buy a premium feature") as a selling point — [App Store, Pixels](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pixels-journal/id1668460700)
- [PRESS] Free Strava users who missed the recap were left with workarounds (profile totals, third-party dashboards). Paywalling something that was free before is what drew the anger ("fair to be annoyed when something previously free goes behind a paywall") — [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/news/strava-year-sport-now-only-subscribers-317425)

**Apple App Review Guidelines** — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- [OFFICIAL] 3.1.2(a): "you must provide ongoing value to the customer".
- [OFFICIAL] 3.1.2(a): "If you are changing your existing app to a subscription-based business model, you should not take away the primary functionality existing users have already paid for."
- [OFFICIAL] Apps that "engage in bait-and-switch" will be removed.
- [OFFICIAL] 5.1.1(i) and 5.1.1(v): require a privacy policy explaining retention and deletion, and in-app account deletion when accounts exist.
- [OFFICIAL] I found no guideline that forbids charging for analytics of a user's own data.

**EU data portability (GDPR Art. 20)**
- [OFFICIAL/INDEP] The right covers personal data "provided by" the data subject, which the final WP29 guidelines (WP242 rev.01, endorsed by the EDPB) read as including *observed* data. **Inferred or derived data** created by the controller's analysis falls *outside* the right. Credit-score profiles and an algorithmically derived heating schedule are the examples given — [EDPB guidelines page](https://edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-right-data-portability-under-regulation-2016679_en); [Bird & Bird](https://twobirds.com/en/insights/2017/global/article-29-working-party-issues-final-guidelines-on-the-right-to-data-portability); [arXiv, IoT portability paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.07189). The primary guideline text was not opened; these are secondary summaries.

**Common practice: raw data stays free**
- [OFFICIAL] Daylio exports CSV free (PDF is premium) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylio); [Daylio FAQ](https://daylio.net/faq/?p=5856)
- [OFFICIAL] Bearable exports CSV free — [Bearable](https://bearable.app/support/common-questions/bearable-free-vs-premium-features/)
- [OFFICIAL] Oura lets users download their data without a membership — [Oura support](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409086524819)

### Inferences
- **The distinction that matters for users is the one that matters for law and press:** the user's entries (the pixels themselves) vs the app's interpretation (correlations, trends, recaps). Dian Dian already shows all entries free. Keeping a raw export free (CSV or JSON) would put it with Daylio, Bearable and Oura and pre-empt the "my data is held hostage" complaint. Image export can stay premium: it is presentation, not data access.
- **Taking something away is worse than never giving it.** Strava's backlash came from removing a free recap. If Dian Dian launches a free teaser stat or a free recap card, it should plan to keep them free for good, or users will read later changes as bait-and-switch (in the spirit of Apple 3.1.2(a), even though that rule targets paid features).
- **Thin or confusing premium stats create their own complaints** (Daylio "confusing", Strava "slideshow of already known stats"). Selling Dian Dian's current filling counts as premium risks the same reaction once trial users see what they bought. Richer, well-explained stats should land before or alongside a more prominent teaser.
- Health-type mood data is sensitive. A "no upsell pressure" competitor (Pixels) and a free nonprofit option (How We Feel) make aggressive stats gating easy to compare against. Keep the popup infrequent, consistent with Wizz's fewer-but-better-timed result in Q2.

### Gaps
- No structured analysis of App Store or Google Play review text (counts of "stats locked" complaints) was found for the named apps. Evidence is anecdotal.
- GDPR Art. 15 (right of access, first copy free of charge under Art. 12(5) and 15(3)) was not fetched in this pass. My understanding is that it guarantees access to the personal data itself, not to premium analytics; verify against the GDPR text or a lawyer if it matters.
- Google Play policy on feature gating was not checked. I am not aware of a relevant rule, but this was not verified.
- No consumer-law rulings were found (EU Omnibus/DSA or French DGCCRF) about paywalling summaries of user-generated data.

## Q5. Recommendation-ready summary for Dian Dian (synthesis of Q1–Q4)

### Takeaway
1. Show one real but question-raising stat free, plus a visible list of the named premium insights it belongs to.
2. Keep the user's raw data and a basic current-month view free, and charge for interpretation over long windows (Bearable's 30-day vs 60/90/365 split is the closest precedent).
3. Make the year-end recap free and shareable at a basic level, with the full recap and high-quality export as premium (the Wikipedia "partial for all, deeper gated" model). Ship it in early December or on 1 January.
4. Do not paywall anything that was free before.

The evidence for each piece is directional (vendor or company data); none of it is controlled evidence on tracking apps.

### Cited Findings
- Free-teaser analogues: time-window gating at Bearable (30 days free, 60/90/365 premium) — [Bearable](https://bearable.app/support/common-questions/bearable-free-vs-premium-features/); headline scores free and detail paid at Oura — [Oura support](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409086524819); top 10 free and full ranking paid at Strava — [Cycling Weekly](https://www.cyclingweekly.com/news/product-news/strava-overhaul-better-routes-more-training-analysis-leaderboard-for-paying-customers-only-455995)
- Showing too much in a teaser lowered subscription odds (deck −86.3%, intro −72.2%), and blurred previews had no significant effect [INDEP] — [Nieman Lab / Journalism Studies](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/02/less-is-more-and-discounts-work-a-new-study-looks-at-the-minutiae-of-paywall-strategy/)
- A visual preview of premium features beat a text paywall (+50% conversion, +64% ARPU) [VENDOR] — [Superwall, Unchaind](https://superwall.com/case-studies/unchaind)
- Paywalls shown after use of a premium feature, with tailored copy, gave +50% to a 7.3% conversion [VENDOR] — [Superwall, JourneyStamp](https://superwall.com/case-studies/journeystamp)
- Later (non-onboarding) paywalls bring 38% of revenue overall and 48% in Lifestyle/Utilities. Only 40% of Health & Fitness buyers buy on the first view [VENDOR] — [Adapty 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/paywall-optimization-tests/)
- Freemium medians: 2.1% D35 download-to-paid, with 23% of conversions coming after six weeks [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- Fewer, better-timed prompts: about −50% volume gave +81% on the primary offer flow (Wizz) [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/contextual-paywall-targeting)
- The recap drove 43–58% of new accounts, +9 points of return rate, and +327% donations from partial personalisation; the deeper layer was gated behind an account [COMPANY] — [Wikipedia Year in Review 2025](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Apps/Team/Wikipedia_Year_in_Review/2025_Year_in_Review)
- Percentile and identity labels on share cards increased share rates; lessons and new users rose after Year in Review [COMPANY] — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/year-in-review-behind-the-scenes)
- Wrapped 2025: 200M users in 24 h and 500M shares, launched 3 December [COMPANY] — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/spotify-says-wrapped-2025-is-its-biggest-yet-with-200m-users-in-its-first-day)
- Paywalling a previously free recap with no teaser drew a backlash framed as "charging to see your own kilometres" [PRESS] — [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/news/strava-year-sport-now-only-subscribers-317425); [Clubic](https://clubic.com//actualite-592360-strava-ose-l-impensable-vous-facturer-pour-consulter-vos-propres-kilometres-parcourus.html)
- Raw data export stays free at Daylio, Bearable and Oura; EU portability excludes derived analytics — [Daylio/Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylio); [Bearable](https://bearable.app/support/common-questions/bearable-free-vs-premium-features/); [Oura](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/4409086524819); [Bird & Bird](https://twobirds.com/en/insights/2017/global/article-29-working-party-issues-final-guidelines-on-the-right-to-data-portability)

### Inferences
**Teaser stat**
- Pick a stat that is personal, true and incomplete: for example "Your best weekday: Saturday" or "Longest run: 12 days". Under it, show the locked follow-ups by name ("why Saturdays are better", "how this month compares", "all-year trends").
- Avoid a free stat that closes the question, such as an average of everything.
- Rotate the free stat across trackers, or keep it the same, but never make it richer than the premium set.

**Stats wall**
- Replace a generic or empty wall with a preview of the user's *own* premium stats: real numbers for one card, and named locked cards for the rest. This combines the two vendor-supported levers (visual preview, context).

**Time window**
- Consider "this month free, all year premium". It fits the year-in-pixels mental model and has a documented precedent (Bearable).

**Popups at the 7th and 30th filled day**
- Consistent with the freemium long tail and with H&F buyers needing several views.
- Keep them rare and tied to a real new stat ("you've unlocked enough data for X"), in line with the Wizz finding.
- Track view → trial → paid per placement, since Dian Dian's sample is too small for A/B tests.

**Recap**
- A free basic share card (the year grid plus 2–3 headline numbers, with the app name or watermark) for anyone above a minimum of filled days.
- Premium: the full recap story, extra stats, themes and high-resolution export.
- Add an identity label to raise sharing (Duolingo).
- Ship either around 1–5 December as "your year so far", riding the Wrapped and Replay wave, or on 1 January for the complete year. Possibly both, with the January version as the premium full-year export.

**Guard rails**
- Never move an existing free view behind the paywall.
- Keep raw export free.
- Make premium stats clearly richer than counts before promoting them harder: thin premium stats risk "slideshow of known stats" and "confusing" complaints.

### Gaps
- No controlled evidence specific to small tracking apps exists for any of these levers. Expected effect sizes for Dian Dian are unknown, and the vendor +50% figures should not be used as forecasts.
- No data was found on whether a free recap card converts free users to premium in a freemium tracker. The recap's conversion value is an untested hypothesis; its sharing and retention value is better supported.
