# Using the app without an account: effect on trial starts and paid conversion, where to put the paywall and the account, and the current App Store / Google Play rules (as of November 25, 2026)

Scope: (A) the evidence on whether letting people into a subscription app without an account raises or lowers trial starts and paid conversion, where practitioners put the paywall and the account in onboarding, and what an account actually adds to monetisation; (B) the current wording of the Apple and Google rules on requiring an account, Sign in with Apple, account deletion, and purchases/restores. All pages were read on 2026-11-25 unless marked [SEARCH]. Labels: [PRIMARY] original data or a company's own experiment, [VENDOR] a vendor with a commercial interest, [DOCS] official platform/SDK documentation, [REGULATOR] courts/law, [COMMENTARY] blogs/forums/practitioners without data, [SEARCH] seen only in a search snippet, [INFERENCE] my own reasoning. Each finding ends with "Transfer:", which says how far it applies to a small tracker app with a subscription and a lifetime purchase.

---

## 1. Does entering without an account raise or lower trial starts and paid conversion?

### Takeaway
I found no published controlled A/B test that compares "no account at all" with "mandatory account first" on trial starts or paid conversion in a subscription app. The evidence that does exist (company reports, small samples, vendor write-ups) points the same way. A login screen as the very first screen loses people before they ever see the paywall: Coconote reported about 10% drop-off and called moving login to after the paywall its biggest win. Duolingo reported +20% DAU from moving sign-up back a few steps. One counter-example matters for design: Tiimo got more trial starts by asking for the account *early* (right after the welcome screen) instead of *right before the paywall*. So the worst spots seem to be the first screen and the screen next to the payment sheet, not "having an account" as such. I found nothing that supports the claim that an account at the start raises later premium conversion.

### Cited Findings
- **Coconote (AI lecture-notes app, hard paywall, bootstrapped to $6.7M ARR, acquired by Quizlet).** Co-founder on RevenueCat's Sub Club podcast, episode of Mar 18, 2026: "one of the nice things is you actually don't really need to create an account to make a purchase because you're already signed into your Apple or Google account. So I think the biggest win that we had was moving login to after the paywall, basically at the end of onboarding." Also: "I think we were seeing 10% drop off by having login being the very first screen". Asked whether they captured contact details before that: "No, this is just like you download the app and open it and now you're forced to create an account." In the same interview, lengthening onboarding "increased our trial start by 16%". — [PRIMARY] (the company's own figures, given verbally; no test design published) — [transcript](https://share.transistor.fm/s/07d90e73/transcript.txt), [episode page](https://subclub.com/episode/bootstrapped-to-6-7m-arr-and-an-exit-to-quizlet-in-2-years-brett-bauman-zack-hargett-coconote). Transfer: **high for the mechanism.** That app had exactly today's setup (login as first screen, data on the server), and the loss happens before onboarding, whatever the category. The 10% figure comes from one app.
- The RevenueCat-written show notes summarise this as "Move login to after the paywall. Forcing account creation before users have experienced any value is a silent conversion killer. Removing it from the front of onboarding can cut drop-off by 10% or more." — [VENDOR] — [Sub Club feed / episode page](https://subclub.com/episode/bootstrapped-to-6-7m-arr-and-an-exit-to-quizlet-in-2-years-brett-bauman-zack-hargett-coconote). Transfer: this is the vendor's editorial gloss on the same single data point. "10% or more" generalises beyond what was said.
- RevenueCat's CEO, in the same episode, on the cost of doing this: "Being able to take a payment before you have an identity marker, being able to associate those, there's a bunch of plumbing we do to make that possible… the Apple purchase state and the account state are not the same thing". — [VENDOR] — [transcript](https://share.transistor.fm/s/07d90e73/transcript.txt). Transfer: high. Buying anonymously and linking later is engineering work (see §3).
- **Duolingo (2017).** "Simply moving the sign-up screen back a few steps led to about a 20% increase in DAUs." Later design: "soft walls" ("optional pages that ask users to sign up, but allow them to keep going by hitting 'Later'"), then "a hard wall, after several lessons, that basically says if you want to move forward, you have to sign up." Swapping a "Discard my progress" button for "a subtle button that simply read 'Later' moved the needle". The later refinements "have yielded an 8.2% increase in DAUs." — [PRIMARY] (the company's own A/B tests, reported by its VP of Growth in an interview, July 17, 2017) — [First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker). Transfer: medium. It measures activation/DAU, not purchases. It is old, and Duolingo was a free app then. Still, it is the best-documented "defer sign-up" test.
- **Tiimo (visual planner/routine app, close to the tracker category).** "In 2024 it sat right before the paywall. Now it comes right after the welcome screen." "According to their team, account creation and trial starts both went up." No figures. The author argues that intent is highest early, that someone who won't create an account then is "unlikely to pay later", and that moving the ask avoids "an email form and a payment screen back to back". His advice is to test it, not copy it: ask early, or skip the ask and request the account only after the paywall. "SSO usually wins, but not always." — [VENDOR] (Jacob Rushfinn, who runs Botsi, a paywall/offer vendor; second-hand report, Oct 1, 2026) — [Botsi blog](https://www.botsi.com/blog/13-onboarding-principles-high-converting-apps). Transfer: medium-high on category, but both arms had an account. This compares *where* the account goes, not account vs no account. It is the one data point for "account early can be fine".
- **What apps actually do (prevalence, not performance).** Of 129 onboarding flows tracked by Lazyweb, "Only 38 of 129 onboarding flows include a detected auth screen" (so about 91 had none). Of the 17 flows with both a paywall and an auth screen, 16 show the paywall *after* account creation and 1 before. The authors call it a "small-sample, directional finding", and auth detection "may under-count". There is no conversion data. Published Jul 7, 2026. — [PRIMARY] (original count) — [Lazyweb Research](https://design.lazyweb.com/blog/do-apps-paywall-before-or-after-account-creation). Transfer: low-medium. It shows that most tracked onboardings have no auth step at all. Among those that do, account-then-paywall is common, but the count says nothing about what converts better.
- RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2025 lists "Streamlining registration through SSO options like Apple, Google, and Facebook" as a lever for raising trial start rates (Phil Carter's commentary). — [VENDOR] — [SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025). Transfer: low-medium. This assumes a registration step exists and says to make it lighter. It is not evidence for or against having one.
- Web e-commerce analogue: about 19% of checkout abandonment is attributed to forced account creation (Baymard), and NN/g recommends guest checkout. — [SEARCH] (seen only in snippets, original not read) — e.g. [ecomhint summary](https://ecomhint.com/blog/guest-checkout-vs-account-creation). Transfer: low. On the web the account form sits on the payment path, while in an app the Apple/Google sheet replaces it.
- Vendor and agency claims that a sign-up wall "routinely loses 20–40%" or "67%" of users carry no underlying study. — [SEARCH] — e.g. [Affective](https://weareaffective.com/learning-centre/should-my-app-require-sign-up-before-users-can-explore-features), [Chop Dawg](https://www.chopdawg.com/guest-mode-vs-forced-sign-up-what-converts-better/). Transfer: do not use these numbers.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The strongest transferable mechanism is simple. Most trials start on day 0 (§4). A first-screen login removes a share of installers (one app: ~10%) before they reach onboarding or the paywall. Unless the account *raises* conversion among the users who pass it, which no source shows, an up-front login lowers trial starts in proportion.
- [INFERENCE] Today's numbers (8 of 9 purchases within 51 hours of sign-up) cannot show that the account helps. They only cover people who already passed the wall, so they are self-selected. They are consistent with the general "buy in the first sessions" pattern.
- [INFERENCE] Tiimo and Coconote together suggest a design rule, not an absolute: don't put the account on the first screen, and don't put it right before the payment sheet. Account *after* purchase (Coconote) and account *early as an optional value step* (Tiimo, Duolingo's soft wall with "Later") have both been reported to work.

## 2. Where do practitioners put the paywall and the account in onboarding, and why?

### Takeaway
The consensus, from Apple's own HIG and from practitioners, is: don't gate the app or the paywall behind sign-up. Let people see value, show the paywall at the end of onboarding or at a value/limit moment, and ask for an account after the purchase or as an optional "save/sync" step. Apple's App Review prescribes this exact pattern in its rejection text: purchase without registration, then optionally register to use the purchase on other devices. Most of the advice comes from vendors who sell paywall or onboarding tooling. They have an interest in more paywall exposure, though not in removing accounts as such, and their advice is backed by anecdotes more than by published tests.

### Cited Findings
- Apple HIG, Managing accounts: "Ask people to create an account only if your core functionality requires it; otherwise, let people enjoy your app or game without one." "Delay sign-in for as long as possible. People often abandon apps when they're forced to sign in before they can do anything useful. To help avoid this situation, give people a chance to get a sense of what your app or game does before asking them to make a commitment to it. For example, a shopping app might let people browse as much as they want, requiring sign-in only when they're ready to make a purchase." — [DOCS] — [HIG: Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts). Transfer: high. This is the platform's stated design guidance. Note that its shopping example puts sign-in *at* purchase; that example is about physical goods paid outside IAP.
- Apple HIG, Apple In-App Purchase (change log: rebranded and "refined guidance" September 17, 2026): "Let people experience your app before making a purchase." "Consider letting people try your content for free before signing up… you might offer a freemium app, a metered paywall, or a free trial." "Simplify initial signup by asking only for necessary information. A lengthy sign-up process may lower your subscription conversion rate. Defer asking for additional information until after people have signed up." The sign-up (subscribe) screen must include "A way for existing subscribers to sign in or restore purchases". "Call attention to subscription benefits during onboarding." — [DOCS] — [HIG: Apple In-App Purchase](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-in-app-purchase). Transfer: high. "Signup" here means subscribing. Apple treats the purchase as the sign-up and puts "sign in" only as a secondary path for existing subscribers.
- Apple HIG, Onboarding: "Prefer letting people experience your app or game before prompting them for ratings or purchases." "Postpone nonessential setup flows or customization steps." — [DOCS] — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding). Transfer: high, but this is a "prefer", not a rule, and hard paywalls at first open are common and allowed.
- Apple's App Review prescribes the order in its rejection template, reposted verbatim by developers in Jun 2023 and Mar 2026: "please revise your app to not require users to register before purchasing in-app purchase products that are not account based. You may explain to the user that registering will enable them to access the purchased content from any of their [iOS / supported] devices and provide them a way to register at any time, if they wish to later extend access to additional devices." — [COMMENTARY] (Apple's verbatim review text as posted on Apple's forums) — [thread 731471, Jun 2023](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/731471), [thread 819232, Mar 2026](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/819232). Transfer: high. This is the platform's own picture of "buy first, account optional later".
- Coconote moved login "to after the paywall, basically at the end of onboarding" (see §1). — [PRIMARY] — [transcript](https://share.transistor.fm/s/07d90e73/transcript.txt). Transfer: high for the order.
- Superwall's founder (via RevenueCat): place paywalls before and after onboarding and on app open, before any locked feature. Start with more placements, then test them down. His 2021 post: "Show the paywall in all these places first and keep removing until conversions drop". — [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat, "Optimizing paywall placement", Nov 20, 2023](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-placement); post seen as [SEARCH] on [X](https://x.com/jakemor/status/1458169112031551492). Transfer: medium. A paywall vendor's interest is maximum paywall exposure. This advice is about the paywall, not the account, but it implies nothing should block the paywall.
- Adapty, on asking for an e-mail address (updated Jun 22, 2026). Its recommended patterns are quiz-gated before the paywall (highest opt-in, "40%+"), in-app after value delivery, at the beginning with clear utility, and after the paywall ("the rarest pattern and the hardest to execute"). FAQ answer: "Before, in most cases." Placements it says hurt: the first screen of onboarding, the same screen as the paywall, and right after a declined purchase ("One user, two decisions at once. The weaker ask loses."). — [VENDOR] (Adapty sells subscription tooling and markets e-mail capture/checkout links) — [Adapty blog](https://adapty.io/blog/how-to-collect-emails-in-your-mobile-app/). Transfer: medium. It agrees with Tiimo that an identity ask *before* the paywall can work if it is not the first screen and not glued to the paywall. It concerns an e-mail opt-in, not a mandatory account.
- Botsi / Rushfinn: ask for the account early or after the paywall, never back-to-back with the payment screen; test it (see §1). — [VENDOR] — [Botsi](https://www.botsi.com/blog/13-onboarding-principles-high-converting-apps). Transfer: medium-high.
- A RevenueCat staff answer to a developer asking whether login is needed: "It's not required for you to have a log in system for users"; "the Purchases SDK will automatically generate an anonymous app user id on configuration"; keep "a 'Restore Purchases' button easily accessible"; set restore behaviour to "Transfer to new App User ID" "to ensure your users are not never locked out of their content" (Apr 3, 2024). — [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat Community](https://community.revenuecat.com/general-questions-7/no-log-in-required-4358). Transfer: high. Same SDK as today.
- RevenueCat docs: anonymous App User IDs are "suitable for many apps that don't require authentication and only support a single platform". `logIn()` from an anonymous ID can "alias the anonymous App User ID to the provided custom App User ID", and when the custom ID is new, or exists without an anonymous alias, "Anonymous ID and Provided ID have CustomerInfo merged". If the custom ID already has an anonymous alias, or the current ID is not anonymous, "No merge or purchase transfer occurs." — [DOCS] — [RevenueCat: Identifying customers](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/customers/identifying-customers). Transfer: high. This is the plumbing that "buy anonymously, create the account after" relies on, and the edge case where the merge does not happen.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The ordering that fits the evidence and the rules for this app: open straight into the product (create a tracker, colour a day). Then show a short onboarding and the paywall at the end of onboarding and at the free-tier limit. Ask for the account as an optional, explained step ("keep your trackers safe and on all your devices"), with Sign in with Apple / Google one tap. The best moments are right after a purchase (Coconote) or at a value moment (Tiimo, Duolingo soft wall). Every source warns against an account form on the first screen and right next to the paywall.
- [INFERENCE] Vendor interest: paywall vendors (Superwall, Adapty, Botsi, RevenueCat) gain from more paywall views and more tests, so "never block the paywall" suits them. None of them gains from users *not* having accounts. RevenueCat even sells the anonymous-to-identified plumbing. The bias is toward more paywall exposure, not against accounts.

## 3. Does an account make buying simpler? What does an app account add to monetisation?

### Takeaway
No. On both stores the purchase runs on the user's Apple ID / Google account, and on iOS a reinstall or a new device gets current entitlements automatically. Apple even says that requiring registration before a non-account purchase is not acceptable. What an account adds comes *after* the purchase. It lets one purchase work on both iOS and Android (anonymous RevenueCat IDs cannot do this). It lets the developer's server key RevenueCat webhooks to a known user, and it gives a stable identity across reinstalls without relying on "Restore". It provides an e-mail address for win-back and billing-recovery messages; vendor data says this is worth a few percent of revenue at the median. It also makes a web checkout simpler, though RevenueCat also supports anonymous web purchases. So "an account at the start makes going premium simpler later" is not supported for the purchase itself. It is partly supported for cross-platform access, the server-side link, and e-mail reach. All of those can be obtained by asking for the account *after* the purchase.

### Cited Findings
- StoreKit `sync()`: "When users reinstall your app or download it on a new device, the app automatically has all transactions available to it upon initial launch. There's no need for users to ask your app to restore transactions — your app can immediately get the current entitlements using currentEntitlements". Calling `sync()` "displays a system prompt that asks users to authenticate with their App Store credentials. Call this function only in response to an explicit user action". "Include some mechanism in your app, such as a Restore Purchases button". — [DOCS] — [Apple: AppStore.sync()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/appstore/sync()). Transfer: high. On iOS the Apple ID already carries the purchase across devices and reinstalls.
- `Transaction.currentEntitlements` emits "A transaction for each non-consumable Apple In-App Purchase" and "The latest transaction for each auto-renewable subscription that has a RenewalState state of subscribed or inGracePeriod". — [DOCS] — [Apple: currentEntitlements](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction/currententitlements). Transfer: high. It covers both the lifetime product and the subscription.
- StoreKit `appAccountToken`: "A UUID that associates the transaction with a user on your own service". The App Store returns the same value in the transaction. — [DOCS] — [Apple: appAccountToken](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction/appaccounttoken). Transfer: medium. An app-generated UUID can tie a purchase to a server record without any e-mail address.
- Apple App Review, in its rejection text: "although App Store Review Guideline 3.1.2 [5.1.1 in the 2026 wording] requires an app to make subscription content available to all the iOS devices owned by a single user, it is not appropriate to force user registration to meet this requirement; such user registration must be optional." — [COMMENTARY] (verbatim Apple text reposted on Apple's forums) — [thread 731471](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/731471), [thread 819232](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/819232). Transfer: high.
- Coconote: "you actually don't really need to create an account to make a purchase because you're already signed into your Apple or Google account… They've kind of already done the double tap to pay at that point, so they'll double tap to sign in." — [PRIMARY] — [transcript](https://share.transistor.fm/s/07d90e73/transcript.txt). Transfer: high. Asking for the account right after payment rides on the momentum of the purchase.
- Google Play Billing: call `queryPurchasesAsync()` when the billing connection is established. This covers, among other cases, "Multiple devices: A user may buy an item on one device and then expect to see the item when they switch devices." `obfuscatedAccountId` / `obfuscatedProfileId` are optional ("An example identifier could be an obfuscated version of the user's login in your system… it can help you ensure that purchases are attributed to the right user"). Page last updated 2026-11-13. — [DOCS] — [Android: Integrate the Google Play Billing Library](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate). Transfer: high. On Android the Google account carries the purchase.
- **Cross-platform needs an account.** RevenueCat: anonymous IDs cannot share subscription status across apps and platforms, and custom IDs used on multiple platforms are treated as the same user. "A user can only manage their subscription on the platform it was purchased from." After a delete and reinstall, "a new random anonymous App User ID will be generated." — [DOCS] — [RevenueCat: Identifying customers](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/customers/identifying-customers). Transfer: high. An iOS lifetime buyer who later moves to Android needs an account (or a manual grant) to keep premium. Lifetime is about 73% of revenue, so this case matters.
- RevenueCat restore docs: "It is recommended that all apps have some way for users to trigger the restorePurchases method", even if you require accounts. Consumables and non-renewing subscriptions "can only be restored by using an account system with custom App User IDs". On Google Billing Client 8+, apps relying on anonymous IDs may not recover *consumed* one-time products. — [DOCS] — [RevenueCat: Restoring purchases](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/getting-started/restoring-purchases). Transfer: medium. The app sells no consumables, so the consumable caveat does not apply. The restore button is needed either way.
- **E-mail for win-back (vendor data).** "The top 5% of subscription apps that collect emails in their mobile app generate 13.2% of their revenue from email. The median sits at 4.3%." "Apps running all four [never purchased / renewal cancelled / billing issue / expired playbooks] see a 7% MRR lift on average, based on Adapty data." "Apps like Zeely, Cal AI, and Flo collect emails at 40%+ opt-in rates with no measurable paywall impact." — [VENDOR] (Adapty, which markets e-mail tooling; method not published) — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/how-to-collect-emails-in-your-mobile-app/). Transfer: low-medium. These are percentages of large revenues with an unknown method. They also show that e-mail can be collected by opt-in, without a mandatory account.
- Lapsed-subscriber reactivation within a year (any channel): monthly 20% overall, weekly 9%, annual 5%; Productivity monthly 36.1%. — [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps). Transfer: low. Not tied to e-mail. With a lifetime-heavy mix there are few lapsing subscribers to win back.
- Phiture (via Sub Club): "Email is good for two things: drip campaigns — offering a staggered, increasing discount to entice signups — and long-form content to keep premium users engaged". — [COMMENTARY] — [Sub Club, Apr 5, 2023](https://subclub.com/episode/how-to-boost-retention-with-subscription-lifestyle-messaging-alice-muir). Transfer: medium (qualitative).
- **Web checkout and identity.** App Review Guideline 3.1.1(a): the link-out entitlements "are not required for developers to include buttons, external links, or other calls to action in their United States storefront apps". 3.1.3 keeps the anti-steering rule "except for apps on the United States storefront". — [DOCS] — [App Review Guidelines, last updated Oct 16, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). RevenueCat Web Purchase Links: "For identified users, append the customer's App User ID to the provided URL". Without it, users see a 404. Anonymous buyers use Redemption Links, which let customers "buy subscriptions without an App User ID" and attach the purchase in the app later. — [DOCS] — [RevenueCat: Web Purchase Links](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/web-billing/web-purchase-links). Transfer: medium. A web purchase needs *an* identifier to attach to. It can be an account, or a redemption link opened on the device.
- Web revenue is "just 3.2% of total revenue globally" (North America 4.9%). — [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps). Transfer: medium. The upside from web checkout is small for most apps.
- US link-out commission status. The Ninth Circuit (Dec 2025) allowed Apple a commission limited to costs "genuinely and reasonably necessary" for coordinating link-outs. A stay was reversed in April 2026. Apple proposed up to 15% on Aug 13, 2026. As of the latest reports found, no rate has been set and the Supreme Court agreed (Jun 30, 2026) to review the contempt finding. — [SEARCH] (news snippets, not read) — [MacRumors, Dec 11, 2025](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/12/11/apple-app-store-fees-external-payment-links/), [MacRumors, Apr 29, 2026](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/29/epic-games-wins-reversal-app-store-fee-battle/), [MacRumors, Aug 13, 2026](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/13/app-store-fees-apple-link-outs/). Transfer: low for now. The economics are unsettled.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] What the owner's belief gets right: today the server learns premium status from RevenueCat webhooks keyed by the account id, and the free tier's data lives on the server. Without an account at first launch, a purchase would land on an anonymous RevenueCat ID (or an app-generated device ID). It then has to be merged into the account when one is created (`logIn()` does this in the common case, with the documented exception above). The account is also the only way to carry a lifetime purchase from iOS to Android. These are reasons to *get* an account eventually, not reasons to require it before the paywall.
- [INFERENCE] Attribution: store-ads attribution (Apple Ads / AdServices, SKAdNetwork, Play install referrer) works per install, not per account, so an account adds nothing there. An account matters for attribution only when joining web funnels to app users. I did not find a source that quantifies this (see Gaps).
- [INFERENCE] Data loss is a retention and trust risk, and it also hits revenue. With anonymous use, a user who deletes the app before creating an account loses their trackers (if they were stored only locally or under an anonymous identity). Their *purchase* survives through the store. That is an argument for asking early for an account (soft wall), not before the paywall.

## 4. Hard paywall versus freemium, day-0 trials, and the effect of an account step

### Takeaway
Benchmarks agree that trials start almost entirely on day 0, about 78–90% depending on category. Hard paywalls (shown at first open) convert downloads to payers about five times better than freemium at the median (RevenueCat 2026: 10.7% vs 2.1%), though apps that pick a hard paywall are not a random sample. No benchmark splits results by whether an account step sits before or after the paywall. The only figure is Coconote's ~10% first-screen loss. Because the day-0 window is where trials happen, anything that removes users before the first paywall view costs trials one for one.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat SOSA 2026 (over 115,000 apps, more than $16B revenue, 2025 data): "Nearly all trial starts happen on Day 0 across categories". Business 89.9%, Health & Fitness 82.1%, Gaming 81.5%, Productivity 78% (lowest). D35 download-to-paid: "Hard paywall median: 10.7% (top quartile above 20.0%)", "Freemium median: 2.1% (top quartile above 4.5%)". D35 download-to-paid by store: 2.6% App Store vs 0.9% Google Play. Trial-to-paid by trial length: 5–9 days 37.4%. — [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps). Transfer: medium-high. Health & Fitness and Productivity are the closest categories. The iOS/Android gap matches all 9 of the app's purchases being on iOS.
- SOSA 2025: "82% of trial starts occur the same day a user installs an app" (Phil Carter); "80-90% of all trials happen on Day 0"; hard paywall "12.11% median conversion versus just 2.18% for freemium" (Hannah Parvaz). — [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025). Transfer: medium-high. It is consistent across years.
- SOSA 2026 commentary: hard paywalls at first open convert downloads to payers at "roughly 5×" freemium (Sven Jürgens). Freemium apps keep converting into week 6 and beyond. A weekly paywall shown to free users after onboarding drove 15% of new revenue (Michal Parizek). — [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps). Transfer: medium. A freemium tracker gets a day-0 burst plus a long tail at the limit.
- RevenueCat case studies on paywall *placement* (not accounts): Greg moved from a paywall after the fifth plant to a paywall for all users during onboarding, giving trial sign-ups +"approximately 400%" and sign-up-to-trial 3% → 15%. The article notes the team changed "two things at once". Rootd: paywall earlier in onboarding gave 5× revenue. — [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat blog, Nov 20, 2023](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/paywall-placement). Transfer: medium. It shows that exposure to the paywall during onboarding drives trials. A first-screen account wall reduces that exposure.
- No source found reports conversion split by account-before vs account-after paywall. The closest are Coconote (~10% loss at a first-screen login; login moved to after the paywall was "the biggest win") and Tiimo (account after welcome beat account right before paywall). — [PRIMARY] / [VENDOR] — see §1. Transfer: see §1.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] For a freemium app (3 trackers + ads), the onboarding paywall is the day-0 moment where most trials will start. An account screen in front of it removes users from that window. The free-tier limit then catches part of the rest later. A hard paywall is a separate decision, not needed to drop the account wall.
- [INFERENCE] Hard-paywall vs freemium medians are confounded by app type (apps choosing hard paywalls are often paid-acquisition, high-intent). They should not be read as "switch to a hard paywall = 5×".

## 5. Apple: requiring an account (5.1.1(v)), data collection (5.1.1(i)–(iii)) and account deletion

### Takeaway
The current guideline (page "Last Updated: October 16, 2026") says: "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login." It also says apps "may not require users to enter personal information to function" unless that is core or legally required. Any app that supports account creation must offer deletion inside the app, including automatically created "guest" accounts. Apple does not define "significant account-based features". Cross-device sync of personal data plausibly qualifies. Still, review practice (rejection templates in 2023 and 2026) rejects registration required before using non-account features or before buying non-account IAPs.

### Cited Findings
- 5.1.1(v) Account Sign-In (full first part): "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login. If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app. Apps may not require users to enter personal information to function, except when directly relevant to the core functionality of the app or required by law. If your core app functionality is not related to a specific social network (such as Facebook, WeChat, Weibo, X, etc.), you must provide access without a login or via another mechanism. Pulling basic profile information, sharing to the social network, or inviting friends to use the app are not considered core app functionality." — [DOCS] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (page shows "Last Updated: October 16, 2026"). Transfer: high.
- 5.1.1(i) Privacy Policies: the policy must "Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion of the user's data." 5.1.1(ii) Permission: "Apps that collect user or usage data must secure user consent for the collection, even if such data is considered to be anonymous… Paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data." 5.1.1(iii) Data Minimization: "Apps should only request access to data relevant to the core functionality of the app and should only collect and use data that is required to accomplish the relevant task." — [DOCS] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: high. Requiring an e-mail address just to colour cells is hard to square with (iii).
- 2.1 (App Completeness): "If your app includes account-based features, provide either an active demo account or fully-featured demo mode". — [DOCS] — [same](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: medium (review logistics).
- Apple support page "5.1.1(v) – Offering account deletion in your app": "Apps submitted to the App Store that support account creation must also let people initiate deletion of their account within the app." "only offering to temporarily deactivate or disable an account is insufficient". FAQ: "My app automatically creates an account for the user. Do I need to include an option to initiate account deletion? Yes. People should have the option to delete automatically generated accounts (sometimes called "guest" accounts) and the data associated with those accounts." "Apps not operating in highly regulated industries should not require people to make a phone call, send an email…". "Everyone should be able to delete their accounts, regardless of where they're located." Sign in with Apple apps "should use the Sign in with Apple REST API to revoke user tokens". For subscribers: "notify them that billing will continue through Apple and ask them to cancel their subscription before continuing". Deletion may be scheduled at expiry "as long as there's also an option to delete the account immediately". — [DOCS] — [Apple Developer: Offering account deletion in your app](https://developer.apple.com/support/offering-account-deletion-in-your-app/). Transfer: high. An anonymous server-side account (device/guest ID) also needs an in-app delete.
- HIG, Deleting accounts: "Provide a clear way to initiate account deletion within your app or game. If people can't perform account deletion within your app, you must provide a direct link to the webpage on which people can do so… don't bury it in your Privacy Policy or Terms of Service pages." "Billing for an auto-renewable subscription continues through Apple until people cancel the subscription, regardless of whether they delete their account." "Even if people didn't use your app to purchase the subscription, you still need to support account deletion." — [DOCS] — [HIG: Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts). Transfer: high.
- The in-app deletion requirement took effect for submissions from June 30, 2022, after an extension from January 31, 2022. — [SEARCH] (Apple news pages seen in results, not opened) — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=12m75xbj), [extension](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=i71db0mv). Transfer: context only.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Whether "data sync between devices" counts as "significant account-based features" is a reviewer judgement. The app has passed review with a mandatory login so far. That is evidence of tolerance, not a safe harbour: the 2023/2026 templates show reviewers reject login walls in front of non-account features. Letting people in without an account removes that risk.
- [INFERENCE] If an anonymous mode creates a server-side record (e.g. an anonymous account to hold synced data or RevenueCat aliases), Apple's FAQ says it must also be deletable in the app. Keeping anonymous data local-only until the user creates an account avoids creating an "account" in the first place.

## 6. Apple: 4.8 Login Services (Sign in with Apple or equivalent)

### Takeaway
Since January 25, 2024, 4.8 no longer names Sign in with Apple. An app that uses a third-party or social login (Google here) for the *primary account* must also offer an equivalent login service that limits data to name and e-mail, lets users hide their e-mail, and does not track for ads without consent. Sign in with Apple meets this. An app that uses only its own e-mail/password system is exempt. 4.8 says nothing about *when* login happens. It applies the same way whether login is mandatory at first launch or optional later.

### Cited Findings
- 4.8 Login Services (current text): "Apps that use a third-party or social login service (such as Facebook Login, Google Sign-In, Log in with X, Sign In with LinkedIn, Login with Amazon, or WeChat Login) to set up or authenticate the user's primary account with the app must also offer as an equivalent option another login service with the following features: the login service limits data collection to the user's name and email address; the login service allows users to keep their email address private as part of setting up their account; and the login service does not collect interactions with your app for advertising purposes without consent. A user's primary account is the account they establish with your app for the purposes of identifying themselves, signing in, and accessing your features and associated services." Exceptions: "Your app exclusively uses your company's own account setup and sign-in systems"; alternative app marketplaces with a marketplace login; education/enterprise/business apps with an existing education or enterprise account; government or industry-backed citizen ID; "a client for a specific third-party service and users are required to sign in to their mail, social media, or other third-party account directly to access their content." — [DOCS] — [App Review Guidelines, Oct 16, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: high. With Google sign-in offered, Sign in with Apple (or another qualifying service) must stay.
- Change history: Jan 25, 2024, "4.8: Edited to require an additional login service with certain privacy features if you use a third-party or social login service to set up or authenticate a user's primary account." — [DOCS] — [Apple Developer News, Jan 25, 2024](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=7j1f99yf). A further edit (around March 2024) clarified that the login service "cannot collect interactions with your app for advertising purposes without consent". — [SEARCH] — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=xqk627qu). Transfer: context.
- HIG: "If you require an account, consider using Sign in with Apple". "If you don't use Sign in with Apple… prefer using a passkey." When people used Sign in with Apple, "you revoke the associated tokens when they delete their account." — [DOCS] — [HIG: Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts). Transfer: high.

## 7. Apple: purchases and restores (3.1.1, 3.1.2, StoreKit) and the "register before purchasing" wording

### Takeaway
3.1.1 asks for "a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases". 3.1.2(a) requires subscriptions to work on all of the user's devices. The sentence "apps may not require users to register before purchasing non-account-based IAP" is **not** in the current guideline text: a full-text search of the October 16, 2026 page finds no "register"/"registration" outside a Siri clause. It survives in App Review's rejection template, now filed under 5.1.1, seen verbatim in 2023 and March 2026, together with the note that registration must not be forced to meet the "all devices" rule. In practice: no registration before purchase, a Restore button, and an optional account offered for multi-device access.

### Cited Findings
- 3.1.1: "If you want to unlock features or functionality within your app, (by way of example: subscriptions, in-game currencies, game levels, access to premium content, or unlocking a full version), you must use in-app purchase." "Any credits or in-game currencies purchased via in-app purchase may not expire, and you should make sure you have a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases." — [DOCS] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: high. Lifetime (non-consumable) and subscriptions are both restorable.
- 3.1.2(a): "the subscription period must last at least seven days and be available across all of the user's devices". "Subscriptions must work on all of the user's devices where the app is available." "those offering subscriptions should allow a user to get what they've paid for without performing additional tasks, such as posting on social media, uploading contacts, checking in to the app a certain number of times, etc." "Auto-renewable subscription apps may offer a free trial period to customers by providing the relevant information set forth in App Store Connect." 3.1.2(c): "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price." — [DOCS] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: high.
- I searched the full text of the current page (downloaded 2026-11-25) for "regist" and found only the Siri vocabulary clause. No guideline sentence forbids registration before purchase. The closest current wording is 5.1.1(v) above, plus 5.1.2(i) ("may not require users to enable system functionalities… in order to access functionality") and 3.2.2(x) (no forcing store-related actions). — [DOCS] (my own check of the primary page) — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: high.
- Rejection template (verbatim, posted Mar 2026): "We noticed that the app requires users to register with personal information to purchase In-App Purchase products that are not account based. Apps cannot require user registration prior to allowing access to app content and features that are not associated specifically to the user. User registration that requires the sharing of personal information must be optional or tied to account-specific functionality." The template continues: "although guideline 5.1.1 requires an app to make subscription content available to all the supported devices owned by a single user, it is not appropriate to force user registration to meet this requirement; such user registration must be optional." The Jun 2023 version cites 3.1.2 instead of 5.1.1 for the "all devices" rule. — [COMMENTARY] (Apple text reposted by developers on Apple's forum) — [thread 819232, Mar 2026](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/819232), [thread 731471, Jun 2023](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/731471). Transfer: high.
- StoreKit "Restoring purchased products": "Customers sometimes need to restore purchased content, such as when they upgrade to a new phone. Include some mechanism in your app, such as a Restore Purchases button". "Don't automatically restore purchases, especially when your app launches. Restoring purchases prompts for the user's App Store credentials". — [DOCS] — [Apple: Restoring purchased products](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/restoring-purchased-products). With StoreKit 2, entitlements are available at launch with no prompt (`currentEntitlements`), and `AppStore.sync()` is for an explicit button only (§3). — [DOCS] — [sync()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/appstore/sync()). Transfer: high.
- RevenueCat staff (Mar 6, 2025), on a no-login subscription app: "it's mandatory to have a restore purchase system to pass app review so you should totally add it to your app." — [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat Community](https://community.revenuecat.com/general-questions-7/do-i-need-a-restore-purchases-function-if-the-only-product-i-m-offering-is-a-monthly-subscription-for-app-with-no-login-5996). Transfer: high.
- HIG: the subscription sign-up screen must include "A way for existing subscribers to sign in or restore purchases". — [DOCS] — [HIG: Apple In-App Purchase](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-in-app-purchase). Transfer: high. It goes on the paywall itself.
- 3.1.1(a) / 3.1.3: US-storefront apps may include "buttons, external links, or other calls to action" to other purchase methods without an entitlement. 3.1.3(b) Multiplatform Services: apps "may allow users to access content, subscriptions, or features they have acquired in your app on other platforms or your web site… provided those items are also available as in-app purchases within the app." — [DOCS] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: medium. 3.1.3(b) is what allows an Android/web purchase to unlock iOS through an account.

## 8. Apple: reported rejections for forcing a login

### Takeaway
Developers keep reporting the same 5.1.1 rejection, from 2023 to March 2026, for apps that require registration before use or before buying non-account IAPs. Apple's answers push a guest/anonymous path and optional registration. Apple publishes no frequency, and the forum sample is self-selected, so how common these rejections are cannot be quantified.

### Cited Findings
- Thread "Clarification on Apple Guideline 5.1.1(v)" (Feb 2023): the rejection says the app "requires users to register with personal information to access the app's content and purchase in-app purchase products that are not account based". Users should be allowed to access none account-based features before registration and login. You may also choose to alert users that accessing game without binding an account may result in losing game progress or in-app purchase items… Once the user decides to use account-based features, the app may present the registration or login feature at that time." A Dec 2025 reply from a SaaS developer reports the same rejection even though the service is already web/Android with login, and plans a "Guest login" button. — [COMMENTARY] (forum; Apple text quoted by the poster, no Apple staff reply) — [Apple Developer Forums 724336](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/724336). Transfer: high. "Warn that progress may be lost without an account" is explicitly allowed.

Mar 2026: same template, thread locked by Apple DTS and redirected to another thread. — [COMMENTARY] — [forum 819232](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/819232). Transfer: high. - Other reports seen only in search results: a game rejected for requiring e-mail/password before IAP (Microsoft Q&A); a RevenueCat-community developer asked by App Review why login was required; SEO "how to fix 5.1.1" guides (2026) giving the same fix. — [SEARCH] — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-au/answers/questions/5896293/apple-app-store-my-game-was-rejected-because-i-don), [appsubmitter.io](https://www.appsubmitter.io/app-rejection-guides/app-store-guideline-5-1-1-data-collection-and-storage/). ## 9. Google Play: account deletion, user data, forced login, billing restores, sign-in UX

### Takeaway
Play's User Data policy requires apps that allow account creation to offer deletion both in the app and through a web link entered in Play Console, and deactivation does not count. "Accounts that are created and operated offline are not app accounts". I found no Play policy that forbids requiring a login before use; Play's only lever is the general rule to limit data collection to what users reasonably expect. Play Billing ties purchases to the Google account and tells apps to query purchases at every connection. Google's explicit "delay sign-in, offer features without it" guidance that I found is for Wear OS only.

### Cited Findings
- User Data policy, Account Deletion Requirement (full policy): "If your app allows users to create an account from within your app, then it must also allow users to request for their account to be deleted. Users must have a readily discoverable option to initiate app account deletion from within your app and outside of your app (for example, by visiting your website). A link to this web resource must be entered in the designated URL form field within Play Console. When you delete an app account based on a user's request, you must also delete the user data associated with that app account. Temporary account deactivation, disabling, or "freezing" the app account does not qualify as account deletion. If you need to retain certain data for legitimate reasons such as security, fraud prevention, or regulatory compliance, you must clearly inform users about your data retention practices (for example, within your privacy policy)." — [DOCS] — [Play Console Help: User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en). Transfer: high.
- Help article FAQ: "An app account is a unique user identity that developers provide as a user-facing feature to serve the user across applications and/or devices". "If your app offers account creation in any part of the app experience, then you still need to offer app account deletion even if some features can be accessed without an account." "accounts that are created and operated offline are not app accounts and do not fall within policy scope." Web resource: it "must be functional… relevant in scope… and reference the app or developer name", and should let users request deletion "without sending the user back to the app and requiring them to re-download it". Timeline: Data deletion questions due Dec 7, 2023 (extension to May 31, 2024). — [DOCS] — [Play Console Help 13327111](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en). Transfer: high. A purely local anonymous mode is out of scope, while a server-side anonymous account is arguably in scope.
- Personal and sensitive user data: "Limit the access, collection, use and sharing of personal and sensitive user data acquired through the app to app and service functionality and policy-conforming purposes reasonably expected by the user". Key considerations: "Only collect and use data that is necessary for your app's functionality." — [DOCS] — [User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en). Transfer: medium. It is the only Play wording that touches "requiring personal data", and it is weaker than Apple's 5.1.1(v).
- Play Billing: call `queryPurchasesAsync()` "when your app successfully establishes a connection with the Google Play Billing Library (which is recommended when your app is launched or comes to the foreground)". Cases include "Multiple devices: A user may buy an item on one device and then expect to see the item when they switch devices." `obfuscatedAccountId` is optional. Last updated 2026-11-13. — [DOCS] — [Android: Integrate the Google Play Billing Library](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate). Transfer: high. I found no Play rule that requires a visible Restore button (see Gaps).
- Wear OS sign-in guidance: "For apps offering functionality without sign-in: Delay presenting Credential Manager until necessary. Provide as many features as possible without requiring sign-in. When authentication becomes essential, display a 'sign in' button". Do: "Provide context by asking for sign-in after the user initiates an action." Don't: "Display sign-in without explaining why sign-in is beneficial." — [DOCS] — [Android Developers: Wear OS sign-in](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/behaviors-and-patterns/sign-in). Transfer: low. It is Wear OS only, but it is the one Google design page found that says "explore first".
- Android Developers Blog (Sep 2025): Economic Times removed passkey-creation prompts from "business-critical journeys such as subscription purchases or unlocking premium features", which "was leading to abandoned carts". — [SEARCH] — [Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/09/best-practices-migrating-users-passkeys-credential-manager.html). Transfer: medium. Identity prompts placed inside the purchase path cost purchases.
