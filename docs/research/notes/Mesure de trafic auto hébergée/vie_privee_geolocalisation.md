# Measuring a website's audience without consent in France/EU: CNIL exemption, ePrivacy scope, GDPR basics, country geolocation databases and cookieless unique visitors (state as of 23 Nov 2026)

Context for the report writer: Dian Dian is a small French indie app. The developer is based in France and users are worldwide, EU included. Its marketing landing page will measure visits, daily unique visitors, sources (UTM), referring site, device, in-app browser, country only (city ruled out), store-button clicks through `/go/apple` and `/go/play` redirects, and bots. There will be no consent banner. Two options are compared. One is a home-made counter in the Express/Postgres backend: server-side, no cookie, IP discarded after a country lookup, and a daily-salted hash of IP + User-Agent for unique visitors (Plausible-style). The other is a self-hosted open-source tool (Umami, Plausible CE, Matomo, GoatCounter). The existing spec is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-23-landing-visits-design.md`. It already says "localisation pas plus précise que la ville, IP non conservée, une ligne dans la politique de confidentialité". This note checks that against the sources and adds what it misses.

## Q1. CNIL audience-measurement consent exemption: exact conditions, 2025 self-assessment tool, end of the list, retention, IP, geolocation, information, objection, cross-referencing

### Takeaway
The exemption rests on CNIL deliberation 2020-091 (§50–52). Since July 2025 it has been applied through a CNIL self-assessment grid, which is much more specific than the 2020 text. The grid allows IP-based geolocation down to city level. It requires minimised User-Agent data (major versions only), at most three event types, a referrer limited to its host, and hashed fingerprints that include a site component and a time component. Reports must be anonymous, rounded to the nearest ten unless anonymity is otherwise justified. An opt-out link must sit in the privacy policy. The grid also **excludes marketing measurement: acquisition channels, conversion channels and campaign performance. Its recommended measure is to disable all collection of UTM or campaign identifiers in URLs.** That clashes directly with Dian Dian's plan to count visits by UTM source, by pin and through the visit → store funnel. The CNIL no longer publishes a list of exempted tools and has replaced it with self-assessment. The editor is responsible for its own compliance.

### Cited Findings

**The legal basis and the 2020 guidelines (still in force)**
- [LAW] Consent for reading or writing on a user's terminal is required by art. 82 of the loi Informatique et Libertés. That article transposes ePrivacy art. 5(3), and its exemption covers operations "strictly necessary" to a service the user expressly requested — [CNIL, art. 82](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/la-loi-informatique-et-libertes#article82)
- [REGULATOR, Sept 2020] Deliberation n° 2020-091 of 17 Sept 2020 (lignes directrices cookies et autres traceurs), §50: the Commission considers that trackers "dont la finalité se limite à la mesure de l'audience du site ou de l'application, pour répondre à différents besoins (mesure des performances, détection de problèmes de navigation, optimisation des performances techniques ou de l'ergonomie, estimation de la puissance des serveurs nécessaires, analyse des contenus consultés, etc.) sont strictement nécessaires … et ne sont donc pas soumis … à l'obligation légale de recueil préalable du consentement" — [CNIL guidelines PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/cnil/files/atoms/files/lignes_directrices_de_la_cnil_sur_les_cookies_et_autres_traceurs.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Sept 2020] §51: these trackers must have "une finalité strictement limitée à la seule mesure de l'audience sur le site ou l'application pour le compte exclusif de l'éditeur". They "ne doivent notamment pas permettre le suivi global de la navigation de la personne utilisant différentes applications ou naviguant sur différents sites web". They "doivent uniquement servir à produire des données statistiques anonymes, et les données à caractère personnel collectées ne peuvent être recoupées avec d'autres traitements ni transmises à des tiers" — [CNIL guidelines PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/cnil/files/atoms/files/lignes_directrices_de_la_cnil_sur_les_cookies_et_autres_traceurs.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Sept 2020] §52: "les traitements de mesure d'audience sont des traitements de données à caractère personnel qui sont soumis à l'ensemble des dispositions pertinentes du RGPD." In other words, the exemption lifts only the consent requirement, not the GDPR — [CNIL guidelines PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/cnil/files/atoms/files/lignes_directrices_de_la_cnil_sur_les_cookies_et_autres_traceurs.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Sept 2020] The same guidelines (§49 list) also exempt "les traceurs conservant le choix exprimé par les utilisateurs sur le dépôt de traceurs". An opt-out cookie that only stores a refusal is therefore exempt itself — [CNIL guidelines PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/cnil/files/atoms/files/lignes_directrices_de_la_cnil_sur_les_cookies_et_autres_traceurs.pdf)

**The CNIL page of 4 July 2025 ("Cookies : solutions pour les outils de mesure d'audience")**
- [REGULATOR, 4 Jul 2025] The trackers must serve "une finalité strictement limitée à la seule mesure de l'audience", "pour le compte exclusif de l'éditeur", and "produire des données statistiques anonymes uniquement". They must not lead to "un recoupement des données avec d'autres traitements" or to non-anonymous data being "transmises à des tiers". They must not track a person across sites or apps. A shared cross-site identifier used for unified "reach" is excluded — [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-solutions-pour-les-outils-de-mesure-daudience)
- [REGULATOR, 4 Jul 2025] The CNIL gives these as recommendations: users informed, "par exemple via la politique de confidentialité"; tracker lifetime "limitée à une durée de treize mois", not extended automatically on new visits; collected data kept "une durée maximale de vingt-cinq mois"; and lifetimes and retention reviewed periodically — [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-solutions-pour-les-outils-de-mesure-daudience)
- [REGULATOR, 4 Jul 2025] Vendors may self-assess with the CNIL tool. They must not present their solution as "certifiée" or "validée par la CNIL". The self-assessment does not bind the CNIL, which will check in an audit whether art. 82 is really met. Liability can fall on both controllers and processors. The page also warns about transfers outside the EU — [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-solutions-pour-les-outils-de-mesure-daudience)

**The self-assessment tool, "Outil d'auto-évaluation relatif à la mise en œuvre d'une solution de mesure d'audience exemptée de consentement" (dated "Juillet 2025", PDF metadata 3 Jul 2025, 8 pages). Read in full.**
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] Scope: the tool is for a measurement *provider* to check whether its solution "peut être configurée de manière à entrer dans le cadre de l'exemption". Where the table names a precise technical measure, "il s'agit d'une recommandation de la CNIL". A provider may show that "une mesure alternative remplit effectivement l'objectif", provided it says and justifies that it departs from the CNIL's measures — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] Allowed purposes: performance measurement, detection of navigation problems, technical or ergonomic optimisation, server capacity estimation, analysis of the content viewed. Other purposes "doivent être désactivées par défaut, notamment : toute mesure à visée marketing, y compris mais non limitée à la mesure de performance des canaux de conversion, de performance de campagnes publicitaires, de mesure des canaux d'acquisition, de lutte contre la fraude publicitaire" and "toute création de cohorte d'utilisateurs". When in doubt, the test is whether the site could still answer the user's express request without the measure. "la nécessité économique ne rentre pas dans le cadre de ce qui est considéré comme « strictement nécessaire »" — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] Reusing data that has been "effectivement" anonymised needs no consent. The GDPR still applies to the anonymisation step itself — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] Data minimisation: if HTTP header data is collected (browser version, OS, hardware, screen size), it is minimised, for example to the "version majeure système d'exploitation/navigateur". The solution collects "au plus trois type d'évènements": (1) a person's presence on a page and the page's information (name, type); (2) use of a feature ("clic bouton, clic lien") and its information ("destination, label"); (3) load time, scroll or time-on-page statistics — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] Criterion "Aucun import de données externes n'est possible". Recommended measure: "Désactivation de toute collecte ou import d'identifiant client (ou « CRM »), d'UTM ou d'identifiant de campagne dans les URLs". "Le référent (« referrer »), s'il est collecté, se limite au domaine (« host »)." "Toute intégration avec des outils tiers est exclue" — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] Criterion "Aucun identifiant permettant un suivi à travers plusieurs domaines n'est utilisé". A cookie, if used, must be first-party. "L'IP si elle est utilisée, permet la localisation à l'échelle de la ville puis est pseudonymisée en enlevant au moins le dernier octet." Any identifier built from device characteristics ("empreinte numérique ou « fingerprinting »") "intègre une composante spécifique au site lors du calcul du hash (par exemple le domaine en cours de visite)" and "une composante temporelle (permettant de s'assurer que l'emprunte numérique … ait une durée de vie limitée)". Tagging that collects personal information (for example forms) is excluded — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] Unified "reach" or deduplication features are excluded — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] Anonymous statistics: "Autant pour la visualisation … que lors d'export, l'ensemble des rapports … comportent uniquement des statistiques anonymes". Recommended measure: "Agrégation et la présentation à la dizaine la plus proche. A défaut, une analyse est menée pour justifier du caractère anonyme des données (voir l'avis du G29)". Anonymisation must hold "quels que soient les critères de sélection … (une combinaison de critère ne doit pas permettre d'isoler un utilisateur)". "Aucun suivi de la navigation d'un utilisateur unique n'est possible", with session replay disabled — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] Right to object: "Une modalité d'opposition est mise en œuvre dans la mesure ou un traitement de données à caractère personnel au sens du RGPD existe". Recommended form: "un bouton ou lien cliquable au sein de la politique de confidentialité". The refusal must last over time, by "dépôt d'un cookie d'opposition" or by adding the fingerprint to "une liste repoussoir" — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] Provider-side criteria apply when a third party runs the tool: a standard art. 28 DPA, no pooling of raw data across clients, no reuse for the provider's own account (including "amélioration de son service, lutte contre la fraude"), and a contact point — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The main finding for Dian Dian.** As the CNIL's July 2025 grid reads, these planned measures fall outside the consent exemption: counting by UTM source, by pin campaign, and the visit → store-tap → download funnel by source. That is "mesure des canaux d'acquisition" or "performance des canaux de conversion", and UTM collection is explicitly listed for disabling. The existing spec checked the city-level, IP and information conditions but not this one. The report should flag it and offer options:
  1. Keep the exemption and drop UTM values, keeping at most "tagged vs not tagged" plus the referrer host. This is what Matomo does: it keeps "campaign vs organic" but not the campaign values. Note that this already leans on Matomo's own reading, because the grid excludes acquisition measurement as a purpose.
  2. Keep UTMs and argue an "alternative measure". The grid allows this for *technical measures*. But the marketing exclusion sits in the *purpose* section, not the table, so this argument is weak.
  3. Measure acquisition from the store side instead, through App Store Connect campaign tokens and the Play install referrer, both already in the roadmap. The landing then only counts anonymous page and button statistics.
  4. Ask for consent for the campaign part only.
  The CNIL's real enforcement focus on a tiny indie site without third-party trackers is unknown. Low enforcement risk is a judgement call, not a sourced fact.
- Store-button clicks as plain events are explicitly allowed (event type 2: "clic bouton, clic lien … destination, label"). Counting `/go/apple` taps by store is fine. Counting them *by campaign* to build a funnel is the marketing part.
- Referring site must be stored as host only. User-Agent-derived fields must be coarse: device class, OS and browser family or major version, in-app browser name. Country only is stricter than the CNIL's city ceiling and therefore fine. Discarding the IP after lookup is stricter than "remove at least the last octet".
- A Plausible-style hash fits the CNIL wording if it includes the site component (the domain) and a time component (the daily salt). The CNIL itself names "le domaine en cours de visite" as the example.
- Dian Dian's volumes are small. "Présentation à la dizaine la plus proche" (or a written anonymity analysis) matters: a country × device × day cell with 1 or 2 visitors can single someone out. The `prod-landing.sql` read script should round or apply a minimum cell size, or the analysis should be documented.
- An opt-out is required "dans la mesure où" personal data is processed. Under §52 the CNIL treats audience measurement as personal-data processing, so plan for one: a link in the privacy policy that sets a first-party opt-out flag (exempt as a "choix exprimé" tracker), which the counter honours. Honouring browser Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signals would be an extra courtesy; no source here requires it.
- The provider criteria (DPA, no pooling, no reuse) are satisfied by construction for a home-made counter or a self-hosted tool. Dian Dian is the sole controller, and the VPS host is only an infrastructure processor.

### Gaps
- Exact CNIL date for ending the public list: the CNIL FAQ (29 Apr 2026) confirms the replacement but gives no date. "1 January 2026" comes from one agency.
- I did not find a CNIL deliberation *number* for the July 2025 update. It looks like a page plus a tool, not a new deliberation amending 2020-091. Not confirmed.
- I found no CNIL text addressing an editor that *builds its own* measurement tool. The grid is written for providers. Applying it to a home-made counter is an inference, though a reasonable one.
- No CNIL source on whether *campaign-wide* labels (the same `utm_campaign` for every visitor, no per-user ID) count as "identifiant de campagne" in the CNIL's sense. The grid does not distinguish, and Matomo strips them all.

## Q2. Does a server-side counter with no cookie and no stored IP fall under ePrivacy art. 5(3) / French art. 82? Is a daily-salted hash of IP + UA personal data or a tracker? What do Plausible, Umami, GoatCounter and Matomo publish about their compliance?

### Takeaway
Under the EDPB's final Guidelines 2/2023 (v2.0, 7 Oct 2024), using the IP address and HTTP headers such as the User-Agent to track navigation can fall within art. 5(3). So can tracking links that append identifiers to URLs to learn the origin of traffic. A "no cookie" server-side counter is therefore not automatically outside the consent rules. It still needs an exemption, and in France that is the CNIL audience-measurement exemption from Q1. The daily-salted hash is best treated as pseudonymous personal data while the salt exists. The CNIL says pseudonymisation is not anonymisation, and its grid treats such hashes as identifiers ("fingerprinting") that need a site component and a time component. Vendor claims vary. Plausible says no banner is needed. GoatCounter says "probably". Umami says it "depends". Matomo ships a dedicated CNIL mode. None is certified by the CNIL.

### Cited Findings

**ePrivacy scope (EDPB Guidelines 2/2023, final v2.0)**
- [REGULATOR, 7 Oct 2024] The guidelines "do not address the circumstances under which a processing operation may fall within the exemptions from the consent requirement". Those are left case by case to member-state transposition and national authorities' guidance (§4, §40, §56) — [EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 v2.0](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, 7 Oct 2024] §42–43: routing identifiers such as the IP address, and application-protocol context data "such as HTTP header including 'accept' field or user agent", can lead to art. 5(3) applying when relied on "to collect information (for example in the context of fingerprinting …)" — [EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 v2.0](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, 7 Oct 2024] §3.3 "Tracking based on IP only", §54: some providers "only rely on the collection of one component, namely the IP address, in order to track the navigation of the user … Article 5(3) ePD could apply". §55: it applies where the IP "originates from the terminal equipment". That is not systematic (for example with CGNAT), but "the static outbound IPv4 originating from a user's router would fall within that case, as well as IPV6 addresses". "Unless the entity can ensure that the IP address does not originate from the terminal equipment … it has to take all the steps pursuant to the Article 5(3) ePD." §56: applicability "does not systematically mean that consent needs to be collected", so exemptions must be assessed — [EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 v2.0](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, 7 Oct 2024] §3.1 URL and pixel tracking, §49–51: tracking links append an identifier to the URL, are "very commonly used … to identify the origin of their inbound source of traffic", and "the collection of identifiers provided through such tracking mechanisms constitutes a 'gaining of access'", so art. 5(3) applies — [EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 v2.0](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)

**Personal data or not**
- [REGULATOR, page dated 29 Apr 2026] CNIL FAQ, Q11: only trackers used solely to produce anonymous statistics are exempt. "La pseudonymisation (simple suppression du nom ou d'autres données identifiantes) n'est pas une anonymisation au sens de la réglementation." Q9: audience-measurement data remains personal data subject to the GDPR — [CNIL FAQ](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs/regles/cookies/FAQ)
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] The CNIL grid treats any identifier "en utilisant les caractéristiques du terminal (empreinte numérique ou « fingerprinting »)" as allowed only with a site component and a time component in the hash. A fingerprint can also be put on an opt-out "liste repoussoir", which implies it is recognisable for its lifetime — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)
- [LAW, not re-read this session] CJEU C-582/14 *Breyer* (19 Oct 2016): a dynamic IP address can be personal data for a website operator that has legal means to identify the person with the ISP's help — [EUR-Lex C-582/14](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62014CJ0582)
- [SEARCH, COMMENTARY] CJEU C-413/23 P *EDPS v SRB* (4 Sept 2025, ECLI:EU:C:2025:645): commentators read it as holding that pseudonymised data is not personal data "in all cases and for every person". For a *recipient* with no reasonable means of re-identification it may fall outside the GDPR, but it stays personal data for the party able to re-identify — [EUR-Lex case page](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/case/EN/C_413_23_P); [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2025/09/pseudonymized-data-after-edps-v-srb.html); [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/eu-data-protection/eu-court-of-justice-clarifies-the-concept-of-personal-data-in-the-context-of-a-transfer-of-pseudonymised-data-to-third-parties/)
- [COMMENTARY, 23 Oct 2026] The Council's Omnibus mandate leaves GDPR art. 4(1) unchanged. It adds an art. 25a codifying the SRB reasoning: pseudonymised data "shall not be considered personal data for [a] third party" that cannot identify the person. For processors it always stays personal data — [EU Internet Policy blog](https://euinternetpolicy.wordpress.com/2026/10/23/the-councils-digital-omnibus-mandate-what-it-would-change-in-the-gdpr/)

It collects referrer, UTM/campaign parameters, browser, OS and device (versions kept as major.minor, for example "Chrome 153.0"), and country, region and city. It claims GDPR, CCPA and PECR compliance and says no cookie banner or consent is needed. The page does not name CNIL or ePrivacy — [Plausible data policy](https://plausible.io/data-policy)
- [VENDOR] Umami FAQ: "Umami is designed with privacy in mind. GDPR compliance depends on how you configure and use it." The tracker uses no cookies. Whether a cookie notice is needed "depends on the other services you use and applicable requirements". Self-hosted data "is retained indefinitely unless manually deleted" — [Umami FAQ](https://docs.umami.is/docs/faq)
- [VENDOR, undated] GoatCounter "probably doesn't require a GDPR consent notice". Its grounds are that no PII is collected and that there is a legitimate interest. It stores only computed or aggregate data, not the IP or User-Agent. ePrivacy is not discussed. "I am not a lawyer" — [GoatCounter GDPR page](https://www.goatcounter.com/help/gdpr)
- [VENDOR, Oct 2026] Matomo ships a CNIL configuration that maps each of the CNIL's criteria to settings (Q1). Its January 2026 blog says "Matomo can be set up to meet CNIL, GDPR, and PECR requirements for cookieless, consent-free tracking" — [Matomo CNIL guide v3.0](https://matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Configure-Matomo-Analytics-for-CNIL-exemption-User-Guide.pdf); [Matomo blog](https://matomo.org/blog/2026/01/privacy-regulations-changes-2026-analytics/)
- [REGULATOR] No tool can claim CNIL validation: the CNIL forbids presenting a solution as "certifiée" or "validée par la CNIL" — [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-solutions-pour-les-outils-de-mesure-daudience)

### Inferences
- "Server-side, no cookie" does not take the counter out of art. 82. The EDPB reading puts IP plus User-Agent tracking, and URL tracking parameters, within art. 5(3). The French route to no banner is therefore to meet the CNIL audience-measurement exemption, which is what the spec already says. The CNIL grid's own conditions (Q1) are the checklist, including the UTM and acquisition limits.
- A page view itself, served from Dian Dian's own server with the IP used only to deliver the page and then dropped, is the least problematic part. Building a daily identifier from IP and User-Agent is the part the CNIL calls "fingerprinting".
- The daily-salted hash is pseudonymous personal data on the day of collection. The operator holds the salt and sees the IP and User-Agent of each new request, so it can recompute the hash and test whether a given person visited that day. Once the salt is destroyed and the hashes are deleted or reduced to a count, what remains (daily distinct counts) is plausibly anonymous. That fits the CNIL tool's note on reusing effectively anonymised data. SRB's relative approach helps third-party recipients, not Dian Dian as the party holding the key.
- Vendor claims are not CNIL-specific except Matomo's. Plausible CE as documented keeps UTM values, the full referrer source and the city. Self-hosting it would not by default match the CNIL grid's UTM, referrer-host and rounding measures, and I found no CNIL mode in Plausible's docs. Umami by default keeps data forever and rotates its visitor salt monthly (Q5). GoatCounter is closest in spirit (aggregate-first) but documents no CNIL mapping.

### Gaps
- No CNIL text found that applies the EDPB's IP-only analysis to an editor's own server logs or to a first-party server-side counter specifically.
- The *Breyer* and SRB judgments were not re-read in full this session. SRB points come from commentary and search summaries.
- No vendor published a filled-in CNIL self-assessment for Plausible, Umami or GoatCounter in what I found. Matomo said in Jan 2026 that it was preparing one, and its Oct 2026 guide is the closest thing.

## Q3. GDPR basics for this case: legal basis, information notice, record of processing, retention of aggregated stats

### Takeaway
Consent-exempt audience measurement still falls under the GDPR. The lawful basis is legitimate interest (art. 6(1)(f)), and it comes with a duty to offer an easy opt-out. The privacy policy must describe the processing. A record of processing is needed even for a micro-business, because the processing is not occasional. The CNIL provides a simplified template. Raw collected data should be capped at 25 months. Truly anonymous aggregates can be kept longer, but the anonymisation itself is GDPR processing.

### Cited Findings
- [REGULATOR, 29 Apr 2026] CNIL FAQ, Q10: where audience measurement rests on legitimate interest (art. 6.1.f), the controller "devra mettre à la disposition des utilisateurs un mécanisme lui permettant de s'opposer au traitement", easily reachable — [CNIL FAQ](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-et-autres-traceurs/regles/cookies/FAQ)
- [REGULATOR, Sept 2020] Audience-measurement processing is subject to "l'ensemble des dispositions pertinentes du RGPD" (§52) — [CNIL guidelines PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/cnil/files/atoms/files/lignes_directrices_de_la_cnil_sur_les_cookies_et_autres_traceurs.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, 4 Jul 2025] Information "par exemple via la politique de confidentialité"; retention of collected data "vingt-cinq mois" maximum; periodic review — [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-solutions-pour-les-outils-de-mesure-daudience)
- [LAW] GDPR art. 13 lists what the notice must say when data is collected from the person: controller identity and contact, purposes and legal basis, the legitimate interest pursued, recipients, transfers outside the EU, retention period or criteria, and rights including the right to object (art. 21). Art. 21(4) requires the right to object to be "explicitly brought to the attention of the data subject … presented clearly and separately" — [GDPR, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- [REGULATOR, page of 13 Apr 2018] Record of processing: the obligation "concerne tous les organismes … quelle que soit leur taille". The under-250-employee derogation is "limitée à des cas très particuliers". Non-occasional processing must be recorded, and if in doubt you should record it. A "Modèle de registre simplifié" (ODS) is provided for small organisations — [CNIL registre](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/RGPD-le-registre-des-activites-de-traitement)
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] Reusing effectively anonymised data needs no consent, but "Le RGPD reste applicable au traitement d'anonymisation lui-même" — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)
- [VENDOR, Oct 2026] Matomo's CNIL mode deletes raw visit data after 744 days and lets aggregated reports outlive it, noting that retention "over 25 months exceed CNIL recommendations for exempt analytics data" — [Matomo CNIL guide v3.0](https://matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Configure-Matomo-Analytics-for-CNIL-exemption-User-Guide.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, 4 Jul 2025] The CNIL warns about transfers of data outside the EU when choosing a solution — [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/cookies-solutions-pour-les-outils-de-mesure-daudience)

### Inferences
- A notice line such as "mesure d'audience anonyme, sans cookie" is not enough on its own. The privacy policy section should state:
  - purpose: audience statistics for the site only;
  - basis: legitimate interest in knowing how the site is used;
  - data processed transiently: IP used once for a country lookup and a same-day hash, then discarded;
  - data kept: page, host referrer, device class, country, button clicks;
  - retention: raw event rows N months, at most 25; anonymous daily totals kept longer;
  - recipients: none, beyond the hosting provider;
  - the opt-out link (art. 21(4) "clearly and separately").
- The footer line is a good extra but does not replace the policy section.
- Event-level rows (one row per page view with country, device and host referrer) are the "collected information" that the 25-month cap should apply to. Daily aggregates with the hash already discarded can be kept as anonymous statistics, if the small-cell issue from Q1 is handled.
- One entry in the simplified CNIL register ("Mesure d'audience du site vitrine") covers the record obligation.
- Hosting: if the VPS provider is outside the EU or a non-EU company, the transfer question from the CNIL warning applies to the raw rows. Where the VPS is hosted was not checked here.

### Gaps
- GDPR art. 13 and 21 were cited from memory of the text, not re-fetched this session (the EUR-Lex link is the authoritative source).
- I did not find a CNIL statement on how long *anonymous aggregated* audience statistics may be kept. By definition they fall outside the GDPR, but no CNIL page confirms this for audience stats.

## Q4. Country geolocation data: DB-IP Lite, MaxMind GeoLite2, IPinfo Lite, IP2Location LITE (licences, updates, accuracy, size, Node.js use)

### Takeaway
All four are free and can be used commercially with attribution. They differ in friction:
- **DB-IP IP-to-Country Lite**: CC BY 4.0, no account, monthly, 8.1 MB MMDB. Plausible CE ships it by default.
- **IPinfo Lite**: CC BY-SA 4.0, account and token, *daily* updates, country plus continent plus **ASN**. The ASN is handy for recognising datacenter bots.
- **MaxMind GeoLite (EULA updated 12 Feb 2026)**: account and licence key, "internal business purposes" only, no redistribution, old copies must be destroyed within 30 days of each new release.
- **IP2Location LITE DB1**: CC BY-SA 4.0, sign-up, BIN/CSV/MMDB, roughly monthly or twice-monthly updates.
All except IP2Location's BIN format ship MMDB, readable from Node with the MIT `maxmind` package. For a country-only, low-maintenance counter, DB-IP Lite (simplest licence, no account) or IPinfo Lite (fresher data, ASN) fit best. MaxMind adds licence obligations without benefit at country level.

## Q5. Unique visitors without cookies: Plausible, Umami, GoatCounter (and Matomo): how they work and their accuracy limits

### Takeaway
All four build a short-lived key from IP + User-Agent + site, with no client-side storage. They differ in key lifetime:
- **Plausible**: salted hash with the salt deleted every 24 hours, so a daily unique visitor.
- **Umami**: deterministic UUID with a salt rotated **monthly by default** (configurable to daily), plus an hourly visit salt.
- **GoatCounter**: in-memory mapping to a random UUID for 8 hours, no hash stored.
- **Matomo CNIL mode**: site-specific config_id rotated every 24 hours.
The daily-salt approach in Dian Dian's spec matches Plausible and Matomo and the CNIL's "site + time component" wording. Accuracy limits are structural: the same person counts again each day and on each device or browser (including in-app browsers versus Safari or Chrome), and people behind one shared IP with the same User-Agent merge.

### Cited Findings
- [VENDOR, Nov 2026] Plausible: `hash(daily_salt + website_domain + ip_address + user_agent)`. "The salt is rotated and deleted every 24 hours." No cookies, local storage or browser cache. Raw IP and User-Agent are never stored. The same person on different devices is counted separately — [Plausible data policy](https://plausible.io/data-policy)
- [CODE] Umami: `const sessionId = uuid(sourceId, ip, userAgent, sessionSalt, distinctId ?? '')`. `baseSalt = getSalt(process.env.SALT_ROTATION, createdAt)`. The visit is `uuid(sessionId, visitSalt)` with `visitSalt = hash(startOfHour(createdAt).toUTCString())`, regenerated after 30 minutes of inactivity. The IP is not in the cached session object — [Umami collection-session.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umami-software/umami/master/src/lib/collection-session.ts)
- [CODE] Umami `getSalt`: with `SALT_ROTATION` unset, `return hash(startOfMonth(createdAt).toUTCString())`, so the salt rotates monthly. `day`/`daily`, `week`/`weekly` and `month`/`monthly` pick the period. Any other string gives a fixed, never-rotating salt. `uuid(...args)` is a UUIDv5 of a SHA-512 hash of the arguments plus the app secret — [Umami crypto.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umami-software/umami/master/src/lib/crypto.ts)
- [CODE] Umami drops bots with the `isbot` package unless `DISABLE_BOT_CHECK` is set — [Umami send route](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/umami-software/umami/master/src/app/api/send/route.ts)
- [VENDOR, undated] GoatCounter: "A sessionID is created as concat(siteID, User-Agent, IP)", stored "in memory as a sessionID → UUIDv4 mapping for 8 hours". Only the random UUID is written with each pageview. "The IP address and User-Agent are never stored to the database or disk." Revisits of the same path in a session do not add a visit. Sessions can be disabled — [GoatCounter sessions](https://www.goatcounter.com/help/sessions)
- [VENDOR, Oct 2026] Matomo CNIL mode: config_id is "a site-specific hash of device/browser characteristics rotated every 24h" — [Matomo CNIL guide v3.0](https://matomo.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Configure-Matomo-Analytics-for-CNIL-exemption-User-Guide.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, Jul 2025] The CNIL requires that such hashes include a site-specific component (for example the domain) and a time component so the fingerprint has "une durée de vie limitée" — [CNIL tool PDF](https://www.cnil.fr/sites/default/files/2025-07/outil_d_auto-evaluation_mesure_d_audience.pdf)
- [REGULATOR, 7 Oct 2024] CGNAT "groups a number of subscribers under the same public IP address" — [EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 v2.0, fn. 29](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Undercounting (merges):** people behind CGNAT on mobile networks, or behind a household or office IP, with the same User-Agent string count as one visitor. Identical iPhones on the same iOS and Safari version share a User-Agent, so merges are likely on shared Wi-Fi.
- **Overcounting (splits):**
  - A person who changes network during the day (Wi-Fi to 4G), uses iCloud Private Relay or a VPN, or switches browser counts twice.
  - The Pinterest in-app browser has a different User-Agent from Safari. A visitor who opens the pin in-app and then taps "open in Safari" is two daily visitors. This matters directly for Dian Dian's in-app-browser field.
- "Daily unique" figures cannot be added into weekly or monthly uniques. The daily salt makes the same person a new visitor each day. Weekly or monthly uniques would need a longer salt, as Umami's monthly default shows, and the CNIL wants the lifetime kept short. So report the sum of daily uniques, or visits, rather than true weekly uniques.
- At Dian Dian's volumes these errors are of the same order as the signal on any given day. Treat uniques as an indicator, and rely on visits and store taps for decisions.
- Implementation matching the CNIL wording and the Plausible model:
  - Hash `SHA-256(daily_salt ‖ site_domain ‖ ip ‖ user_agent)`.
  - Keep the daily salt in memory only, generated at UTC midnight and never written to disk.
  - Keep only `count(distinct hash)` per day and dimension, and delete the hashes the next day.
  - Never use the hash for anything else, such as linking to accounts or the Play install referrer.
- If Umami is chosen, set `SALT_ROTATION=daily` to get the same daily-visitor semantics and a shorter fingerprint life. Also add a deletion job, since self-hosted data is otherwise kept indefinitely.

### Gaps
- No independent study was found quantifying the error of IP + User-Agent daily uniques against cookie-based uniques. Vendors do not publish one.
- How Plausible's dashboard aggregates daily hashes into multi-day "unique visitors" was not documented on the page read.
- GoatCounter's geolocation source and any salt or hash use beyond the session map are not documented on the pages read.
