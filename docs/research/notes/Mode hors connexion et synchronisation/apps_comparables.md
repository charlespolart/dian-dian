# Offline use, accounts and sync in leading mood / habit / journal / "year in pixels" apps (November 2026)

Context for the report writer: the client is an indie Flutter "year in pixels" tracker (iOS first, Android too). Today it requires an account at first launch and keeps everything on its own server (REST + WebSocket live sync), so it mostly needs the network. The owner is deciding whether to allow offline filling with a later resync. This file covers what comparable apps do.

Evidence labels used below:
- [PRIMARY]: the vendor's own site, help centre, blog, store listing (description, App Privacy label, Data safety, release notes, developer replies) or privacy policy.
- [VENDOR]: a commercially interested claim about one's own or a rival's product, such as a competitor's comparison page.
- [DOCS]: official technical documentation.
- [CODE]: source code, repositories, changelogs, package pages (GitHub, F-Droid).
- [COMMENTARY]: third-party reviews, press, blogs, forum posts, quoted user reviews.
- [SEARCH]: seen only in a search-result snippet; the page itself could not be fetched or was not read. Treat it as unverified.
- [INFERENCE]: my own reasoning from the cited material.

App Privacy labels and Play Data safety sections are self-declared by developers and not verified by Apple or Google. ## Q1. Which apps are local-first, which are server-first, and which require an account?

### Takeaway
Most of the market leaders in this niche are local-first with no account: Daylio, Streaks, Loop, HabitNow (free tier), Way of Life, and How We Feel without an account. The cloud there is either the user's own iCloud or Google Drive (Daylio, Streaks, How We Feel) or an optional paid backup (HabitNow Premium). Several apps are hybrid: data lives on the device and an optional account adds backup or sync on the vendor's server. These are Pixels by Teo Vogel ("Pixels Cloud"), Finch, Day One and How We Feel with an account. The account-centred apps are Habitify (a local-only "Guest" mode exists), Bearable (Firestore, one device at a time) and DailyBean (account data declared in Data safety). Notion is the server-first contrast, and it added an opt-in offline mode in August 2025. None of the local-first leaders requires an account at first launch.

## Q2. What works offline, and what happens when the network returns, especially in server-first apps?

### Takeaway
In the local-first apps, offline is the normal state: Daylio, Loop, HabitNow and Way of Life simply work, and only the backup waits for the network. Streaks says so explicitly ("complete habits without a connection, and they sync the next time you're online"). The two documented server-backed comparators, Day One and Notion, both queue offline edits locally and sync them automatically on reconnect. Notion limits offline to pages downloaded in advance and blocks sharing and permission changes. Day One warns that signing out deletes unsynced entries for good. Habitify, Bearable and DailyBean do not document offline writing. Bearable's release notes show login failing on poor connections and a bug that lost recent changes. Finch is usable offline except for social features.

## Q3. How do they describe sync conflicts and backup restores (overwrite or merge), and are there known data-loss incidents?

### Takeaway
Backup-file apps restore a whole snapshot and replace what is on the device: Daylio says merging two backups is not possible, and Finch's older FAQ says loading a cloud backup erases current data. Daylio and Bearable both state that they are not designed for use on several devices at once. Bearable warns this "can lead to data conflict issues and thus result in data loss". The apps with real sync engines, Day One and Notion, promise automatic merges. Notion merges text automatically but sometimes asks the user to review non-text changes, and uses a CRDT model for offline pages. Day One built its own server after duplicates and losses with iCloud and Dropbox. Its sharpest documented risk is losing unsynced offline entries on sign-out.

- [INFERENCE] Two patterns emerge. Small teams avoid merge logic entirely: whole-snapshot backup that replaces local data (Daylio, Finch) or "one device at a time" (Bearable, DailyBean per a user review). Teams that do multi-device sync either hand it to Apple's CloudKit (Streaks, How We Feel iOS) or build a dedicated engine (Day One Sync, Notion's CRDT). The one-day-one-cell data shape of a pixels tracker is much simpler to merge than rich text — sources above

## Q5. What do the App Store "App Privacy" labels and Google Play "Data safety" sections signal?

No leader in this set shows "Data Not Collected" except Pixels (iOS) and, among the "Year in Pixels" apps, Pixa. Pixa has Sign in with Apple, and Pixels has an optional cloud account.
