# Requiring an account before buying: real apps, review outcomes by design, and what subscription vendors say (as of November 25, 2026)

Scope: (1) well-known apps usable without an account that require one before a purchase; (2) apps that dropped such a gate after a rejection; (3) what subscription SDK vendors document or recommend; (4) reported App Review outcomes for three designs (account before purchase with a cross-device explanation; purchase first, prompt after; account only where needed), including Restore behind a sign-in; (5) subscription versus lifetime unlock. The rule texts and the general run of forum rejections are in another note. Forum threads here are only those that show a design, an outcome, or an Apple engineer's position. Findings reused from the notes in `Utiliser l'app sans compte/` keep their sources. Pages were read on 2026-11-25 unless marked otherwise. Labels: [PRIMARY] = Apple's own text (guidelines, WWDC sessions, Apple engineers on Apple's forums), a first-hand account of a rejection with the message quoted, or an app's own help-centre statement about its own flow; [DOCS] = other official platform or SDK documentation; [VENDOR] = a vendor with a commercial interest (blogs, staff forum answers); [COMMENTARY] = blogs, forums, onboarding galleries, practitioners without primary evidence; [SEARCH] = known from a search snippet only; [INFERENCE] = my own reasoning.

## 1. Counter-examples: well-known apps usable without an account that require one to buy

### Takeaway
I could not confirm a single well-known health, habit, journal or mood app that lets people use it without an account but blocks the purchase until they sign in. The closest is Insight Timer: a guest mode, plus a subscription its help centre says is "tied to your Insight Timer account, not your device". No page shows that a guest is stopped at the paywall, though. The other apps checked fall into three groups. Some are account-first, so they are not counter-examples (Clue since 2019, Bearable, Notion, probably Calm). Some let the guest or anonymous user buy through the store (Duolingo in its recorded iOS onboarding, Day One on iOS and Android, Habitify's phone purchases, Finch, Photomath, GeoGuessr, Nikkei Asia). Some have no accounts at all (Daylio, HabitKit, Streaks, prior note). The confirmed account-gated purchases are in apps that sell a server service also used on the web (ChatGPT) or pure cloud storage (Pixels Cloud+). A tracker's local Premium features are neither. Being listed on the store also proves little: none of these sources shows that App Review saw and approved a gated flow knowingly, and big apps may be treated differently.

### Cited Findings
- Insight Timer: guest mode lets people practise "right away—no sign-up required", and the article lists "MemberPlus features for unlimited ad-free content" among the benefits of an account (updated 2026-03-19). "Your MemberPlus subscription is tied to your Insight Timer account, not your device", "your subscription follows you wherever you go" (updated 2026-03-17). — [PRIMARY] — [use without an account](https://insight-timer.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/67000702468-can-i-use-the-insight-timer-app-without-creating-an-account-), [subscription on a new device](https://insight-timer.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/67000664911-how-do-i-access-my-subscription-on-a-new-device-). Transfer: medium. It uses the same pitch the app plans ("tied to your account, follows you"), but it sells a server content library with web access, and the gate itself is unverified.
- Duolingo: the ScreensDesign recording has "Feature Carousel & Paywall (01:13–02:00)" with "Try for $0.00", and "Profile Creation Prompts (05:06–05:40)" later, "prompting the user to create a profile to save progress". The screenrove capture (2026-09-24) shows "Create a profile" with "Later". — [COMMENTARY] — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/apps/duolingo-language-lessons/), [screenrove](https://screenrove.com/duolingo/flows); 2022–23 web-offer claim: [SEARCH] — [Slickdeals](https://slickdeals.net/f/16346452-duolingo-price-reduction-limited-time-offer-annual-for-60). Transfer: high. The best-known delayed-sign-up app sells its subscription before any profile.
- Day One: "Subscriptions on iPhone and iPad are handled through the App Store". The steps go from plan choice straight to "Face ID, Touch ID, or your Apple ID password"; the web flow starts "Sign in to Day One at dayone.me"; cross-platform access works "as long as you're signed in to the same Day One account". — [PRIMARY] — [Day One guide](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/premium-subscription/subscribing-to-day-one-premium/). Transfer: high. The journal app closest to this one documents no sign-in step in the native purchase. Its account is needed to carry the purchase to other platforms and the web.
- Habitify (help snippets): "When you start your subscription from the iOS app or Android app, your subscription goes with your Apple ID or Google Account … you can easily restore the Premium without re-purchasing". "For the purchase from the website app, the subscription will go with your Habitify account". For multi-device Premium, "Your account is not a Guest account". — [SEARCH] — [Habitify: transfer or restore subscription](https://intercom.help/habitify-app/en/articles/9679836-how-to-transfer-or-restore-habitify-subscription-on-habitify-account). Transfer: high. In a habit tracker with a guest mode, the store purchase follows the store account and the app account is required only for cross-device use.
- Finch: a "Restore Finch Plus Purchase" button in Settings "syncs with the store". — [SEARCH] — [Finch help: restore](https://help.finchcare.com/hc/en-us/articles/38508647189389-How-to-Restore-Your-Finch-Plus-Subscription). Photomath Plus is "an in-app purchase linked to your Apple ID or Google Play Account". — [SEARCH] — [Photomath help](https://support.google.com/photomath/answer/14330572). Nikkei Asia: "There are two ways to subscribe: Through the app. You do not need to sign in to the app." — [SEARCH] — [Nikkei Asia help](https://info.asia.nikkei.com/help-usingapps). Transfer: medium. Large apps sell without requiring an app login.
- ChatGPT iOS upgrade steps begin "Open the ChatGPT app and sign in"; the app "shows the plans and checkout options available to your account". — [PRIMARY] — [OpenAI help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/7905739-chatgpt-ios-app-upgrading-to-a-paid-subscription). Logged-out use was added on the web in April 2024. — [SEARCH] — [TechCrunch, Apr 1, 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/01/chatgpt-no-longer-requires-an-account-but-theres-a-catch/). Transfer: low. A cloud service sold on every platform is "account-based" in the plain sense, and a tracker's local themes and statistics are not.
- Clue: "since 2019, we've been asking new joiners to create an account", because "An account ensures tracked data is securely stored on our EU-based servers" (Jan 11, 2023). — [PRIMARY] — [Clue: why do I need an account](https://helloclue.com/articles/how-to-use-clue/why-do-i-need-an-account-to-use-the-newest-version-of-clue). Transfer: Clue went from optional to *mandatory at entry*. It did not keep a free guest tier with a gated purchase.
- Sleep Cycle and Calm (table rows 7 and 13): [Sleep Cycle support](https://support.sleepcycle.com/hc/en-us/articles/13753649968924-Can-t-access-your-premium-features-with-an-active-subscription) [SEARCH]; [ScreensDesign: Calm onboarding](https://screensdesign.com/articles/calm-onboarding-design/) [COMMENTARY]. Transfer: when Premium is keyed to an app account, the top support issue is a sign-in to the wrong account. That happens with a gated purchase as much as with a later link.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The rarity is itself the finding. In the categories closest to this app, the big apps either ask for the account at entry or let the anonymous user buy through the store and ask for the account afterwards. "Free without an account, but sign in to buy" is not a pattern I could confirm in any well-known tracker or journal app.
- [INFERENCE] Where the gate does exist (ChatGPT, Pixels Cloud+, probably Insight Timer), the paid thing is a server service or server storage that also works off-device, which is the plain-language meaning of "account-based". No source defines Apple's term. Extra trackers, themes, statistics and no ads all run on the device. Only "the same Premium on Android" needs the server account.

## 2. Apps that switched from "account required to buy" to optional after a rejection

### Takeaway
I found no well-known app whose changelog or blog says "you can now buy without an account" after a rejection. The documented switches are smaller apps and an open-source template. The Flutter News Toolkit, a Google/VGV starter kit for news apps, required sign-in before subscribing. Apps built on it were rejected under 5.1.1 from 2023. A developer reported in 2024 that he "had to adjust the implementation to allow subscribing without an account" and to add a Restore button. The maintainers said they heard "mix feedback", meaning some apps got through and some did not. A 2020 cross-platform audio app kept its account gate but added a skip option at the reviewer's request, and was then approved. A game rejected in May 2026 had a guest mode with a silent server account but blocked guest purchases. Its forum thread does not report the fix or the outcome.

### Cited Findings
- Flutter issue "Remove Authentication Requirement for Subscription Flow" (Mar 7, 2023): "Apple has rejected iOS submissions for apps leveraging the Flutter News Toolkit based on required user authentication for the subscription flow." The proposed fix: users "should be prompted with a login option to access their subscription content across all devices and optionally opt out and proceed through the in-app purchasing flow". Maintainer, Oct 11, 2023: "We are currently hearing mix feedback regarding the recommended flow." A developer, Sep 1, 2024: "I had to adjust the implementation to allow subscribing without an account. Additionally, … I needed to add a 'Restore Purchase' button". Re-filed Oct 10, 2025, still open. — [PRIMARY] — [flutter/flutter#122136](https://github.com/flutter/flutter/issues/122136), [VGVentures/news_toolkit#1583](https://github.com/VGVentures/news_toolkit/issues/1583). Transfer: high. The articles are delivered from a server, so server-side delivery alone did not make the purchase "account-based". The fix that worked was buying without an account, an optional login, and a Restore button.
- Cross-platform audio app (Apple forum, May 2020). Users could browse and play free content without an account; the register/login screen "shows up only when the user tries to access premium content", because the subscription "is valid on all platforms (iOS & Web, Android …)". Rejection: "your app requires users to register or log in to access features that are not account-based." After the first appeal: "please revise your app to let users freely access your app's non account-based features." Final post: "We have the app approved! We explained once again why we need an account for premium users as clearly as possible. We also added examples of other apps … all of them forcing the user to log in / register (they don't even have the skip option as we do - added after the reviewer asked us to add)." — [PRIMARY] (first-hand, Apple text quoted) — [Apple forum 132680](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/132680). Transfer: high, and two-sided. The same design as this app's was rejected twice. It passed only after a third explanation, citing competitors, and adding a skip the reviewer requested. What the skip bypassed is not stated.
- PlayFab game (May 19, 2026). The start screen offered Login, Create account (e-mail + password) or Guest mode. Guest mode silently created a PlayFab account from the iOS device ID, and the developer "disabled IAP in Guest mode". Rejection, verbatim: "We noticed that the app requires users to register with personal information to purchase In-App Purchase products that are not account based. … please revise the app to not require users to register before purchasing In-App Purchase products that are not account based. You may explain to the user that registering will enable them to access the purchased content from any of their supported devices and provide them a way to register at any time". The only answer is AI-generated (allow guest IAP, keep the device link, make registration optional). No resolution is reported. — [PRIMARY] (rejection text); answer [COMMENTARY] — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-au/answers/questions/5896293/apple-app-store-my-game-was-rejected-because-i-don). Transfer: **very high**. This is the closest match found: a silent per-device server identity, every free feature open, purchase gated behind registration. It was rejected in 2026 with the template.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Public "we removed the account gate" stories are rare because a rejected developer usually fixes the build and moves on. The evidence that remains is forum threads and issue trackers. Their direction is consistent (make the purchase guest-capable, add Restore), but they cannot measure how often the gate gets through.

## 3. What subscription vendors document and recommend

### Takeaway
No vendor page I found states Apple's rule as such. RevenueCat's docs encode it indirectly. The one setting that stops a purchase moving to another user, "Keep with original App User ID", is "only allowed for apps that require every customer to create an account before purchasing". The default "Transfer to new App User ID" is "Required" when people can buy before an account. RevenueCat staff are inconsistent: an answer on how to force login before purchase gives no review caveat (2024), another says "we've had devs do this with no issues" (2025), and a third warned in 2021 that "Apple may be tightening up restrictions". Adapty, Qonversion and Superwall treat anonymous purchase followed by identification as the normal case, and none tells developers to require login. Apple's engineers and WWDC sessions are more explicit than any vendor: buy first, owned by an anonymous account, and offer account creation later.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat Restore Behavior (prior note): "Keep with original App User ID — Use with caution 🚧" "is only allowed for apps that require every customer to create an account before purchasing". For apps that have "an optional login mechanism and / or allows customers to purchase before creating an account", "Transfer to new App User ID" is "Required to make sure customers that purchase without an account can restore transactions." — [DOCS] — [RevenueCat: Restore Behavior](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/projects/restore-behavior). Transfer: high. If purchases become guest-capable, the transfer setting must stay on the default.
- RevenueCat restore docs (prior note): "It is recommended that all apps have some way for users to trigger the `restorePurchases` method, even if you require all customers to create accounts." — [DOCS] — [RevenueCat: Restoring Purchases](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/getting-started/restoring-purchases). Transfer: high. The Restore button is needed in every design.
- RevenueCat staff "ryan" (Nov 13 and 19, 2021): "RevenueCat links subscription status to App User IDs, not store accounts. This is the only way to allow a customer that purchased through iOS access their same subscription on Android or Web (by logging in and setting the same App User ID)." On Restore: "It's required for compliance unless you require all customers to create an account before purchasing", and "Apple may be tightening up restrictions on what kind of apps are allowed to do this". — [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat community #775](https://community.revenuecat.com/general-questions-7/should-subscriptions-need-to-be-linked-to-stores-ids-or-to-emails-775). Transfer: medium. The cross-platform point is right. The claim that Restore is optional with forced accounts is contradicted by RevenueCat's own docs and by a 2021 rejection (§4).
- RevenueCat staff Haley Pace (Aug 28, 2024, best answer), asked how to require an account before the purchase button: "you can force the user to login by checking to see if they are an anonymous id." The answer says nothing about App Review. — [VENDOR] — [RevenueCat community #5001](https://community.revenuecat.com/sdks-51/how-to-add-user-authentication-check-before-purchase-button-clicked-5001). Same staff member, Mar 19, 2025, on a 5.1.1 rejection of a login-first app: "Apple isn't rejecting you specifically for having user registration", "we've had devs do this with no issues". The developer then reported "the Review has passed" without changes. — [VENDOR] / outcome [PRIMARY] — [RevenueCat community #6041](https://community.revenuecat.com/sdks-51/handling-apple-app-review-rejection-with-revenuecat-s-keep-with-original-app-user-id-setting-6041). Transfer: medium. The vendor's support staff treat forced login as a legitimate option, which is evidence it sometimes passes, not that it is safe.
- RevenueCat elsewhere (prior notes): "It's not required for you to have a log in system for users" (staff, Apr 3, 2024); on Duolingo, "You don't even need an account yet" (blog, Nov 17, 2026); and its CEO on the plumbing: "Being able to take a payment before you have an identity marker, being able to associate those, there's a bunch of plumbing we do to make that possible" (Sub Club, Mar 18, 2026). — [VENDOR] — [#4358](https://community.revenuecat.com/general-questions-7/no-log-in-required-4358), [blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/app-value-paywall), [transcript](https://share.transistor.fm/s/07d90e73/transcript.txt). Transfer: high. The vendor sells exactly the anonymous-then-identified plumbing.
- Adapty docs, "Allow purchases without login": "If your users can make purchases both before and after they log into your app, you need to ensure that they will keep access after they log in." By default "the access level is shared, so both profiles have paid access"; this can be changed to transfer access or turn sharing off. For anonymous users, "their purchases are automatically synced from the App Store on the SDK activation". The page does not recommend login before purchase. — [DOCS] — [Adapty: Identify users (iOS)](https://adapty.io/docs/ios-quickstart-identify). Snippet from the same docs: "Users can purchase before logging in — call identify() after login. If it is a new customer user ID, Adapty assigns the customer user ID to the current profile, so all the purchase history is maintained." — [SEARCH] — [Adapty: Flutter identify](https://adapty.io/docs/flutter-quickstart-identify).
- Qonversion's SDK "creates an anonymous user with a unique random ID upon an app install" and links it to the developer's user after login. — [SEARCH] — [Qonversion: user identifiers](https://documentation.qonversion.io/docs/user-identifiers). Superwall's anonymous ID "persists internally until the user deletes/reinstalls your app", and `identify` "aliases your userId with the anonymous Superwall ID". — [SEARCH] — [Superwall: user management](https://superwall.com/docs/ios/quickstart/user-management). Nothing found for Apphud, Glassfy or Purchasely. Transfer: medium. Anonymous-first is every SDK's default.
- Apple, WWDC18 session 705 "Engineering Subscriptions" (Apple engineer Pete Hare; unofficial transcript that "may contain transcription errors"): "we think it's best to offer in-app purchase before an account creation step", "you can just open the app for the first time and buy the subscription immediately", "users don't have to go through funnels of entering emails and passwords", "you can use the techniques that we just went through by using an anonymous account in these instances", "you can rely on the original transaction ID if you need to associate multiple devices", and later, "when it comes time down the road to actually give the user an account creation flow … you update those email fields". — [PRIMARY] — [WWDC18 705 transcript (nonstrict.eu)](https://nonstrict.eu/wwdcindex/wwdc2018/705/). Transfer: **high**. Apple's own engineering guidance describes this app's "silent server account per device" as the owner of the purchase, with account creation later.
- Apple, WWDC22 session 110404 "Implement proactive in-app purchase restore": "associate the original transaction IDs with an account on your system. It can be either an anonymous account, or an account that the user created with your system." "These transactions are persistent and contain an original transaction ID, which allows you to maintain status across devices and platforms." "No need to require customers to sign-in or tap "Restore Purchases."" "First, continue providing a "Restore Purchases" button within your app." — [PRIMARY] — [WWDC22 110404](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/110404/). Transfer: high.
- Apple App Store Commerce Engineer (Sep 2022), asked whether only logged-in users may buy: "you can associate these purchases (original transaction ID's) to an anonymous account without requiring users to create/sign in to an additional account … In general any additional steps or friction implementation before a purchase that doesn't add value or functionality may impact the customer buy experience. For any policy questions, please refer to the App Store Review guidelines." — [PRIMARY] (Apple engineer, not App Review) — [Apple forum 714338](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/714338).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The vendors' silence on the rule does not mean the gate is safe. Their docs are built around anonymous purchase. RevenueCat reserves its one "block transfers" setting for apps with a mandatory account, which suggests a guest-plus-gated-purchase design is outside the shape they expect.

## 4. Review outcomes by design, and Restore behind a sign-in

### Takeaway
(a) **Account required before purchase, with a cross-device explanation.** It is reported both rejected and approved. The approvals I found came after appeals: a 2020 cross-platform audio app, after a third explanation and an added skip, and a 2025 RevenueCat app, after explaining. The rejections include a 2026 game whose design matches this app's (a silent device account, free play, registration only to buy). Apple's template also says the cross-device explanation is to be shown *with* optional registration, not used as a reason to require it. (b) **Buy first, then a strong prompt to create an account.** This is what Apple's template, WWDC18 and Apple's engineers describe, and I found no report of it being rejected. Its documented costs are support cases: purchases left on a guest identity, wrong-account sign-ins, and transfers after sign-out. (c) **Account only for features that need it (sync, other platforms, web).** This is design (b) seen from the feature side, and Apple's engineer says an account is legitimately needed "to access service off-platform". On Restore: account-first apps have been rejected for *lacking* a Restore button. I found no published rejection specifically for a Restore that demands an app sign-in. Apple DTS said in 2016 that an app "cannot require a user to log in", but may advise that "a login is suggested" for restoring non-renewing subscriptions.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Account before purchase**
- Approved after appeal (2020), rejected twice before that: see §2. — [PRIMARY] — [Apple forum 132680](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/132680). Transfer: high. The argument "the subscription is valid on all platforms, so we need the account" was the developer's own, and it did not persuade review the first two times.
- Approved after explanation, with no change (Mar 2025). The rejection said the app "requires users to register with personal information to purchase in-app purchase products that are not account based", and the developer reports "the Review has passed". — [PRIMARY] — [RevenueCat community #6041](https://community.revenuecat.com/sdks-51/handling-apple-app-review-rejection-with-revenuecat-s-keep-with-original-app-user-id-setting-6041). Transfer: medium. Neither the app type nor the argument is known.
- Rejected (May 2026): the PlayFab guest-mode game in §2. — [PRIMARY] — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-au/answers/questions/5896293/apple-app-store-my-game-was-rejected-because-i-don). Transfer: very high.
- Apple engineer (Jun 2022), asked about forcing account creation right *after* subscribing: "There are apps that require account creation/sign-in before being able to purchase but once the customer has purchased the subscription/product, they are entitled to that service. I'd inquire why this would be required. With the App Store/StoreKit, your app can provide service to this user across all of our platforms without an app account or even anonymous account. But to access service off-platform some sort of accout/authentication would be required." — [PRIMARY] (Apple engineer, not App Review) — [Apple forum 709396](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/709396). Transfer: high. An Apple engineer acknowledges that gated apps exist, and treats StoreKit as enough for every Apple device. Only Android and the web are "off-platform" reasons for an account.
- 3.1.2 rejection (Jan 2022): "it would be appropriate to modify your app to include an optional user registration feature to deliver subscription content to all of the user's iOS devices." Apple engineer: "Restore purchases should meet that criteria but there is no reason a customer ever needs to use that mechanism", since the app can check status at launch with StoreKit 2. — [PRIMARY] — [Apple forum 698017](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/698017). Transfer: high. Review itself asks for *optional* registration as the multi-device mechanism.

**(b) Buy first, prompt after**
- Apple's template prescribes it: "You may explain to the user that registering will enable them to access the purchased content from any of their supported devices and provide them a way to register at any time, if they wish to later extend access to additional devices." — [PRIMARY] — [Microsoft Q&A, May 2026](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-au/answers/questions/5896293/apple-app-store-my-game-was-rejected-because-i-don); also forum threads 731471 (2023) and 819232 (2026) in the prior note. Transfer: high. The app's planned sentence, "Premium is tied to your account: you get it back on all your devices", is the wording Apple allows for an *optional* step.
- Apple DTS (Mar 2016): "an application cannot require a user to log in, however, the app can advise a user, that for purposes of restoring non-renewing subscription purchases, a login is suggested". For iCloud-based tracking, "iCloud login has to be optional and the app should alert the user that iCloud is required to access the subscription content from their other iOS devices - and to provide a way to register later". "I'm not App Review". — [PRIMARY] (old, DTS) — [Apple forum 44086](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/44086).
- Duolingo: paywall and checkout first, then "Create a profile" with "Later" (§1). Its 2024 wording: "Don't forget to create a profile to save your progress! This will also allow you to access your account on other devices." — [PRIMARY] (prior note) — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-101-how-to-learn-a-language-on-duolingo). Coconote (Mar 2026): "you actually don't really need to create an account to make a purchase because you're already signed into your Apple or Google account. So I think the biggest win that we had was moving login to after the paywall". — [PRIMARY] (prior note) — [Sub Club transcript](https://share.transistor.fm/s/07d90e73/transcript.txt).
- Support cost, GeoGuessr (modified 12 Nov 2026): "This can happen if you tap PLAY without signing in and then purchase a subscription on the guest account." Signing in later means you "were moved to a new account instead". The fix: log out on all devices, open the game on the purchase device without signing in, wait until the subscription appears, then log in with e-mail on that same device. — [PRIMARY] — [GeoGuessr help](https://geoguessr.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/206000066667-mobile-lost-access-to-your-pro-subscription-). Transfer: high. This is the failure to design out: sign-in to an *existing* account must carry the guest's purchase.
- RevenueCat community (prior note): users losing the subscription when logging into an existing account is one developer's "most frequent support issue" (2023). Purchases get "reassigned to their current anonymous user ID" when a renewal posts while the user is logged out, and the fix is "calling syncPurchases right after logIn completes". — [VENDOR]/[COMMENTARY] — [#2510](https://community.revenuecat.com/sdks-51/users-often-lose-subscription-when-logging-into-an-existing-account-2510), [#1171](https://community.revenuecat.com/sdks-51/app-ids-transferring-subscriptions-to-anonymous-users-1171).

**(c) Account only where needed**
- Day One and Habitify (§1): the native purchase runs through the store; the account carries Premium to other platforms and the web. HabitKit (no accounts, prior note) moves Pro between iOS and Android by hand through support, using the "RC ID"s. Daylio says purchases "cannot be transferred between Google and Apple products". — [PRIMARY] — [Day One](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/premium-subscription/subscribing-to-day-one-premium/), [HabitKit help](https://habitkit.app/help), [Daylio FAQ](https://daylio.net/faq/docs/daylio-faq/tutorials/moving-daylio-to-new-phone/). Transfer: high. Cross-platform Premium is the one thing the account really adds, and the alternatives are manual.
- 3.1.3(b) Multiplatform Services lets apps "allow users to access content, subscriptions, or features they have acquired in your app on other platforms or your web site … provided those items are also available as in-app purchases" (prior note). — [PRIMARY] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).

**Restore**
- Account-first, multi-platform app (Dec 2021), "the app does not work without signing up, like dropbox", rejected: "Include some mechanism in your app to let the user restore their purchases, such as a Restore Purchases button." — [PRIMARY] — [Apple forum 696375](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/696375). The Flutter developer who removed the gate "needed to add a 'Restore Purchase' button" (§2). Transfer: high. Restore is required whatever the account design.
- WWDC22: Restore "gives customers an opportunity to force an app to restore their Apple ID's transactions in case of an issue or if the customer uses a different Apple ID." — [PRIMARY] — [WWDC22 110404](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/110404/). HIG (prior note): the subscribe screen must include "A way for existing subscribers to sign in or restore purchases". — [PRIMARY] — [HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-in-app-purchase). Transfer: high. Apple presents "sign in" and "restore" as alternatives. Restore is the Apple-ID path and is not meant to depend on an app account.
- I searched for a rejection quoting Restore behind an app login and found none. The nearest are 3.1.1 rejections where a reviewer mistook the native StoreKit prompt for a custom password field. — [PRIMARY] — [Apple forum 827361](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/827361).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] On review risk for the planned design, the evidence points to **moderate-to-high rejection risk, decided by the reviewer**. The flow is a free app without an account and a purchase gated behind sign-in "so Premium follows you". It matches the 2026 PlayFab rejection almost exactly, and the explanation it gives is the one Apple's template reserves for optional registration. It can pass, as the 2020 and 2025 cases show, but those passes came after rejections and appeals. With one release planned, a rejection costs a review round-trip and possibly a redesign under time pressure.
- [INFERENCE] A Restore button that opens the sign-in screen instead of calling the store is, to a reviewer testing on a fresh install with a sandbox Apple ID, "registration required to get the purchase back". It would probably be read under the same 5.1.1 template ("not appropriate to force user registration" to meet the all-devices requirement) or under 3.1.1's restore mechanism. No case confirms this.
- [INFERENCE] The app already creates a silent server account per device. That account can own the purchase, which is Apple's WWDC "anonymous account". Requiring a named sign-in at purchase therefore adds nothing technical for iOS-to-iOS use. A named account is needed only for Android/iOS crossover and for recovering on a new phone *with a different store account*.

## 5. Subscription versus lifetime unlock: does the product type make a purchase "account-based"?

### Takeaway
I found no vendor or developer statement that a cross-platform subscription counts as "account-based" while a one-time unlock does not, or the reverse. Apple's template explicitly rejects the subscription argument: the rule that subscriptions must reach all the user's devices does not justify forcing registration, and that registration "must be optional". StoreKit already restores both auto-renewable subscriptions and non-consumables on every Apple device signed into the same Apple ID. So "the purchase can be restored through the App Store account anyway" weighs against requiring registration for both products. The developer argument that "our subscription is valid on Android and web, so it is account-based" was rejected twice in 2020 before an appeal succeeded.

### Cited Findings
- Template text (Mar 2026 version, prior note): "although guideline 5.1.1 requires an app to make subscription content available to all the supported devices owned by a single user, it is not appropriate to force user registration to meet this requirement; such user registration must be optional." The Jun 2023 version cites 3.1.2. — [PRIMARY] — [Apple forum 819232](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/819232), [731471](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/731471). Transfer: high. Cross-device subscription access is named and set aside as a reason.
- `Transaction.currentEntitlements` returns "A transaction for each non-consumable Apple In-App Purchase" and "The latest transaction for each auto-renewable subscription …"; on reinstall or a new device "the app automatically has all transactions available to it upon initial launch" (prior note). — [DOCS] — [Apple: currentEntitlements](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction/currententitlements), [AppStore.sync()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/appstore/sync()). Transfer: high. Both the subscription and the lifetime product follow the Apple ID without an app account.
- RevenueCat: restoring reactivates content "from the same store account"; "Consumables and non-renewing subscriptions can only be restored by using an account system with custom App User IDs" (prior note). — [DOCS] — [RevenueCat: Restoring Purchases](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/getting-started/restoring-purchases). Transfer: high. In SDK terms, the only product types that need an account to be restorable are consumables and non-renewing subscriptions, and this app sells neither. The 2016 Apple DTS answer that allowed *suggesting* a login concerned non-renewing subscriptions (§4).
- Apple engineer: StoreKit serves the user "across all of our platforms without an app account"; an account is needed "to access service off-platform" (§4). — [PRIMARY] — [Apple forum 709396](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/709396).
- RevenueCat staff: App User IDs are "the only way to allow a customer that purchased through iOS access their same subscription on Android or Web" (§3). — [VENDOR] — [#775](https://community.revenuecat.com/general-questions-7/should-subscriptions-need-to-be-linked-to-stores-ids-or-to-emails-775).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] On product type: the lifetime non-consumable is the weakest candidate for a gate, because it is restorable from the store receipt by design. The subscription is no stronger, because Apple's template answers that argument directly. The defensible "account-based" part is the same for both: using the purchase on Android (and the web). That supports an account *offered* at purchase and afterwards ("to also get Premium on Android or a new phone"), not one *required* to buy.
