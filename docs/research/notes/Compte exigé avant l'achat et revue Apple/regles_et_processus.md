# Requiring an account before an in-app purchase: Apple and Google Play rules, their history, and Apple's dispute process (as of November 25, 2026)

Scope: the current App Review Guidelines sentences that bear on "an account is required to buy Premium / to restore"; whether "apps may not require users to register before purchasing non-account-based IAP" was ever a published Apple rule, and how 5.1.1(v) changed (32 versions of the guidelines page, September 2014 to November 2026, via the Wayback Machine and the live page); the Human Interface Guidelines; other Apple documentation and sessions (StoreKit `appAccountToken`, restore, App Review help, WWDC/Tech Talk transcripts); the 2025 and 2024 App Store Transparency Reports; Apple's dispute process; Google Play policies and billing docs. Pages were read on 2026-11-25 unless marked; Wayback captures are cited with their capture date. Labels: [PRIMARY] = Apple's own text (guidelines, HIG, developer docs, Apple staff, archived copies included) or a first-hand account of a rejection with Apple's message quoted; [DOCS] = other official platform or SDK documentation (Google Play, Android, SDK docs); [VENDOR] = a vendor with a commercial interest; [COMMENTARY] = blogs/forums/practitioners without primary evidence; [SEARCH] = known from a search snippet only; [INFERENCE] = my own reasoning. "The app" is the tracker app under study: usable through a silent identity-less account, but Premium (subscription with trial, or lifetime) can only be bought, or restored from a no-account state, after signing in or creating an account.

## 1. Current App Review Guidelines text bearing on "account required to buy / to restore"

### Takeaway
The guidelines page shows "Last Updated: October 16, 2026". No sentence says in so many words that registration may not be required before a purchase. Two sentences carry that rejection all the same. 5.1.1(v) says to let people use an app without a login unless it has "significant account-based features". 5.1.1(x) says a request for name and e-mail must be optional and that "features and services are not conditional on providing the information". 3.1.1 requires a restore mechanism, and 3.1.2(a) requires subscriptions on "all of the user's devices". Neither makes an account a precondition. App Review files its "register before purchasing" rejections under 5.1.1 (§2).

### Cited Findings
- 5.1.1(v) Account Sign-In: "If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a login. If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app. Apps may not require users to enter personal information to function, except when directly relevant to the core functionality of the app or required by law." — [PRIMARY] — [App Review Guidelines, Last Updated October 16, 2026](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: the silent account meets the "use it without a login" half. The purchase gate is a separate question, and "account-based" is defined nowhere.
- 5.1.1(x): "Apps may request basic contact information (such as name and email address) so long as the request is optional for the user, features and services are not conditional on providing the information, and it complies with all other provisions of these guidelines". — [PRIMARY] — [same page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: Apple, Google and e-mail sign-in all hand over an e-mail address (or a relay address), so making Premium conditional on them matches the plain reading of this clause.
- 5.1.1(iii)–(iv): "should only collect and use data that is required to accomplish the relevant task"; "not attempt to manipulate, trick, or force people to consent to unnecessary data access … Where possible, provide alternative solutions for users who don't grant consent." 5.1.1(ii): "Paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data" (about consented user/usage data). — [PRIMARY] — [same page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: secondary hooks.
- 3.1.1: "you should make sure you have a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases." No mention of accounts. — [PRIMARY] — [same page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: the lifetime product and the subscription are both restorable. A Restore that first demands an app sign-in puts the registration wall in front of restoring too.
- 3.1.2(a): "available across all of the user's devices"; "Subscriptions must work on all of the user's devices where the app is available"; "should allow a user to get what they've paid for without performing additional tasks, such as posting on social media, uploading contacts, checking in to the app a certain number of times, etc."; free trials allowed. 3.1.2(b) (upgrades) says nothing on accounts; 3.1.2(c): "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price." — [PRIMARY] — [same page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: App Review says the "all devices" rule may not be used to force registration (§2). No "additional tasks" example is account creation.
- 3.1.3(b): multiplatform apps "may allow users to access content, subscriptions, or features they have acquired in your app on other platforms or your web site … provided those items are also available as in-app purchases within the app." The only other account mention in 3.1.3 is (a): "Reader apps may offer account creation for free tiers". — [PRIMARY] — [same page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: this is what lets an Android purchase unlock iOS; it is permissive ("may allow") and does not make an account mandatory before an iOS purchase.
- Before You Submit / 2.1(a): "If your app includes account-based features, provide either an active demo account or fully-featured demo mode"; "include demo account info … if your app includes a login." 4.8 requires an equivalent privacy-preserving login next to Google sign-in and says nothing on *when* sign-in may be required. 5.1.2(i): apps "may not require users to enable system functionalities (such as push notifications, location services, tracking) in order to access functionality"; 3.2.2(x): apps "must not force users to rate the app, review the app, download other apps, or other store-related actions in order to access functionality". — [PRIMARY] — [same page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: none names accounts; 5.1.2(i) and 3.2.2(x) show the general "no unrelated precondition" pattern.
- Business preamble: "If we can't understand how your app works or your in-app purchases aren't immediately obvious, it will delay your review and may trigger a rejection." — [PRIMARY] — [same page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
- Deletion FAQ: "People should have the option to delete automatically generated accounts (sometimes called "guest" accounts) and the data associated with those accounts." — [PRIMARY] — [App Review Help: 5.1.1(v) account deletion](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-account-deletion). Transfer: the silent device account needs an in-app delete whatever happens to the gate.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The planned flow fits App Review's template almost word for word: the app "requires users to register with personal information to purchase In-App Purchase products that are not account based". Premium (more trackers, themes, statistics, no ads) is a feature unlock, not an account-specific service. The planned copy, "Premium is tied to your account: you get it back on all your devices", is the argument the template rejects in advance (§2).
- [INFERENCE] There is a counter-argument: the entitlement lives on the server, keyed by account, and spans iOS and Android, so it could be called "account based". Apple defines neither term, and one developer passed after explaining a comparable design (§6). But the closest cross-platform case was rejected (July 2023, §2), and 5.1.1(x) answers the argument textually. Keep it as a fallback, not a plan.
- [INFERENCE] Gating Restore behind sign-in doubles the exposure. StoreKit restores with the Apple ID alone (§4), so a reviewer who taps Restore and meets a sign-in wall has two hooks: the template and 3.1.1.

## 2. History: was "register before purchasing" ever a published rule? When did 5.1.1(v) appear and change?

### Takeaway
No. I searched 32 versions of the published guidelines (September 2014 to the October 16, 2026 page) for "regist". Outside a Siri clause, the only hits are an old privacy-policy rule (2014–2016) and a 2020–2021 sentence about e-mail sent to contacts gathered at registration. The "register before purchasing" wording exists only in App Review's rejection messages. Developers have quoted it verbatim since at least April 2020, first citing 3.1.2 for the "all iOS devices" point and, by 2026, citing 5.1.1 instead. The written basis evolved in three steps:
- old guideline 17.2 (to June 2016);
- 5.1.1(ii) "let people use it without a log-in" (June 2016), renamed 5.1.1(v) "Account Sign-In" in June 2018;
- the account-deletion sentence (June 7, 2021) and the new 5.1.1(x) (October 2021).

### Cited Findings
- Pre-2016 numbered guidelines: "11.6 Content subscriptions using IAP must last a minimum of 7 days and be available to the user from all of their iOS devices"; "17.2 Apps that require users to share personal information, such as email address and date of birth, in order to function will be rejected"; "17.5 Apps that include account registration or access a user's existing account must include a privacy policy or they will be rejected". The same text appears in the 2 April 2016 capture. — [PRIMARY] — [Wayback, 2014-09-03](https://web.archive.org/web/20140903022336/https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Wayback, 2016-04-02](https://web.archive.org/web/20160402024907/https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Transfer: "no personal information as a condition of use" is more than ten years old. There was never a purchase-specific sentence.
- June 2016 rewrite (between the 2 April and 18 June 2016 captures): "5.1.1 (ii) If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a log-in. Apps may not require users to enter personal information to function, except when directly relevant to the core functionality of the app or required by law." — [PRIMARY] — [Wayback, 2016-06-18](https://web.archive.org/web/20160618044341/https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
- By 2 December 2016 the social-network sentence was added. 3.1.2 gained "Subscriptions must work on all of the user's devices where the app is available" and the subscription "additional tasks" sentence, and 3.2.2(vi) applied "get what they've paid for without performing additional tasks" to all apps. — [PRIMARY] — [Wayback, 2016-12-02](https://web.archive.org/web/20161202065800/https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
- June 2018: the 4 June capture still has "(ii) If your app doesn't include…"; the 11 July capture has "(v) Account Sign-In: If your app doesn't include significant account-based features, let people use it without a log-in…". — [PRIMARY] — [Wayback, 2018-06-04](https://web.archive.org/web/20180604060822/https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Wayback, 2018-07-11](https://web.archive.org/web/20180711075024/https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); update date June 4, 2018 per [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2018/06/04/new-app-store-review-guidelines/) [SEARCH].
- June 7, 2021 news: "5.1.1(v): Apps supporting account creation must also offer account deletion" (the same post added appeals for "unfair treatment"). The 4 October 2021 capture shows the sentence, with "log-in" now spelled "login". Enforcement was set for January 31, 2022 (news of October 6, 2021), then June 30, 2022 (news of May 24, 2022). — [PRIMARY] — [news June 7, 2021](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=dovxb62h), [October 6, 2021](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=mdkbobfo), [May 24, 2022](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=12m75xbj), [Wayback, 2021-10-04](https://web.archive.org/web/20211004043843/https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
- 5.1.1(x) ("basic contact information … optional") is absent from the 4 October 2021 capture and present on 1 November 2021. It came with the October 22, 2021 update tied to the US developer settlement. — [PRIMARY] — [Wayback, 2021-11-01](https://web.archive.org/web/20211101080723/https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); date from [MacRumors, October 22, 2021](https://www.macrumors.com/2021/10/22/app-store-review-guidelines-october-2021/) [COMMENTARY].
- January 25, 2024: 3.2.2(vi) deleted; 5.1.2(i) added ("A version of this rule was originally published as Guideline 3.2.2(vi)"); 4.8 rewritten around an equivalent privacy-preserving login. — [PRIMARY] — [news January 25, 2024](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=7j1f99yf). Transfer: since then "additional tasks" survives only in 3.1.2(a).
- Text search across the 31 Wayback captures (2014-09-03 to 2026-07-01) and the live page: the only "regist…" phrases are the Siri/SiriKit vocabulary clause; the 2014–2016 privacy-policy rule (17.5); and in 3.1.3, "communications sent to points of contact obtained from account registration within the app", present only in the captures of 1 October 2020, 1 May 2021 and 1 June 2021. No version contains "register before" or "register prior". — [PRIMARY] (my text search of Apple's archived pages) — [current page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [Wayback index](https://web.archive.org/web/2026*/https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
- The archived In-App Purchase Programming Guide never discouraged accounts. For a server record it advised: "let users identify themselves to your server with an email or user name, plus a password." Non-renewing subscriptions are "often used when your users already have an account on your server". — [PRIMARY] — [Wayback, Delivering Products, 2017-07-10](https://web.archive.org/web/20170710042604/https://developer.apple.com/library/content/documentation/NetworkingInternet/Conceptual/StoreKitGuide/Chapters/DeliverProduct.html), [Products, 2019-04-26](https://web.archive.org/web/20190426043256/https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/NetworkingInternet/Conceptual/StoreKitGuide/Chapters/Products.html). The old "Common App Rejections" page (2016) and App Review page (2018) mention only demo accounts. — [PRIMARY] — [Wayback, 2016-10-30](https://web.archive.org/web/20161030000137/https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/rejections/), [Wayback, 2018-12-27](https://web.archive.org/web/20181227121927/https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/). Transfer: the ban comes from review practice, not from the developer docs.
- **Rejection template, first-hand copies:**
  - **April 2020.** "We noticed that your app requires users to register with personal information to purchase non account-based in-app purchase products … Apps cannot require user registration prior to allowing access to app content and features that are not associated specifically to the user. … User registration that requires the sharing of personal information must be optional or tied to account-specific functionality. To resolve this issue, please make it clear to the user that registering will enable them to access the content from any of their iOS devices and provide them a way to register at any time … Please note that although guideline 3.1.2 … requires an app to make subscription content available to all the iOS devices owned by a single user, it is not appropriate to force user registration to meet this requirement; such user registration must be made optional." — [PRIMARY] — [forum 131751](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/131751).
  - **August 2021.** Same text, under "Guideline 5.1.1 - Legal - Privacy - Data Collection and Storage". — [PRIMARY] — [forum 688899](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/688899).
  - **May 2023, a memory-journal app.** "They can only record 1 memory a day. Once 3 memories are recorder … they are prompted with an upgrade option through an IAP". Users "Continue With Apple at launch". The developer said the login "is used to tie the memories to iCloud so they are backed up when signing in on other devices". Rejected with "please revise your app to not require users to register before purchasing in-app purchase products that are not account based". — [PRIMARY] — [forum 729708](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/729708).
  - **July 2023, a multi-platform product.** "Our app is multi-platform android/web/ios … on iOS before buying subscription we are asking user to login to check whether they already bought subscription through any other platform so that user don't need to pay twice also to sync purchase with our server." Rejected under 5.1.1 with the same template; "Apple keep rejecting". — [PRIMARY] — [forum 733984](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/733984).
  - **September 2023, a services app.** "Registration must then only be required for account-specific features, such as saving items for future reference or placing an order." The first version was "accepted on August 10" and the update was rejected on September 11. — [PRIMARY] — [forum 737212](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/737212).
  - **March 2026.** Same template, now "although guideline 5.1.1 requires an app to make subscription content available to all the supported devices owned by a single user". App Review replied: "Thank you for your post and appeal. We're investigating". — [PRIMARY] — [forum 818946](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/818946).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The ban is review practice derived from 5.1.1(v), and from 5.1.1(x) since 2021, not guideline text. That cuts both ways. An appeal can truthfully say no guideline sentence forbids it, but App Review has applied the same template for at least six years, and the template answers the cross-device argument in advance.
- [INFERENCE] The two 2023 cases are the closest analogs to the app. One is a one-entry-per-day app with a free quota of 3, sign-in for cross-device backup, and a paywall. The other required login before purchase to share one entitlement across iOS, Android and the web. Both were rejected, and no approval is reported.

## 3. Apple Human Interface Guidelines

### Takeaway
For more than ten years Apple's design guidance has been to delay sign-in "as long as possible" and ask for an account only when core functionality needs it. The Sign in with Apple page says that in a commerce app the account should come "after people make a purchase". The In-App Purchase page lists "sign in or restore purchases" on the paywall as a path for *existing* subscribers. One example cuts the other way: Managing accounts has a shopping app "requiring sign-in only when they're ready to make a purchase". That is about buying goods, not IAP. The HIG gives recommendations, not review rules.

### Cited Findings
- Managing accounts (no change log; URL first captured 22 May 2023): "Ask people to create an account only if your core functionality requires it; otherwise, let people enjoy your app or game without one." "Delay sign-in for as long as possible. People often abandon apps when they're forced to sign in before they can do anything useful… For example, a shopping app might let people browse as much as they want, requiring sign-in only when they're ready to make a purchase." — [PRIMARY] — [HIG: Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts).
- Sign in with Apple (latest change-log entry September 14, 2022): "Ask people to sign in only in exchange for value … signing in lets them personalize the app experience, access additional features, or synchronize data." "Delay sign-in as long as possible." "In a commerce app, wait until after people make a purchase before asking them to create an account. If you support a guest checkout system, give people a quick way to create an account after the transaction completes." — [PRIMARY] — [HIG: Sign in with Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sign-in-with-apple). Transfer: high. Purchase first, account after. "Synchronize data" is a legitimate benefit to offer in exchange for signing in.
- Apple In-App Purchase (change log September 17, 2026: "Rebranded as Apple In-App Purchase and refined guidance"): "Let people experience your app before making a purchase." "Simplify initial signup by asking only for necessary information. A lengthy sign-up process may lower your subscription conversion rate." The subscribe screen must include "A way for existing subscribers to sign in or restore purchases". "If you offer the same subscription options in multiple apps or through your website, provide a sign-in option so people don't think they have to pay multiple times". On offer codes: "if you require people to create an account or sign in before they can use your app, make this process as smooth as possible". — [PRIMARY] — [HIG: Apple In-App Purchase](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-in-app-purchase). Transfer: a sign-in for cross-platform subscribers is recommended as an *option* on the paywall.
- Onboarding (change log June 10, 2024): "Prefer letting people experience your app or game before prompting them for ratings or purchases." — [PRIMARY] — [HIG: Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding).
- Older wording: "Delay a login requirement for as long as possible … For example, App Store doesn't ask users to log in until they decide to buy something. Users often abandon apps that force them to log in before they can do anything useful." (iOS HIG, captured 17 March 2016); "Delay sign-in as long as possible … Give them a chance to fall in love with your app before making a commitment to it" (captured 5 August 2017). — [PRIMARY] — [Wayback, 2016-03-17](https://web.archive.org/web/20160317084551/https://developer.apple.com/library/ios/documentation/UserExperience/Conceptual/MobileHIG/StartingStopping.html), [Wayback, 2017-08-05](https://web.archive.org/web/20170805173045/https://developer.apple.com/ios/human-interface-guidelines/interaction/authentication/).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The HIG is consistent with the rejection template: explore first, buy with the Apple ID, then offer an account for sync and cross-device access. Read together with the Sign in with Apple page, the Managing accounts shopping example points to guest checkout followed by an account, not to an account before paying.

## 4. Other Apple documentation and sessions: tying a purchase to an account, restore, App Review pages

### Takeaway
Apple provides `appAccountToken`, an app-generated UUID that ties a purchase to "a user on your own service" without asking the user anything. The server API can set or change the token after the purchase. StoreKit makes past purchases available at launch on a new device, and a 2022 Apple session says customers need not sign in or tap Restore. App Review says it reviews "both with and without an account" and tests purchases in the sandbox, so a gated paywall is likely to be seen. No Apple page I found says in so many words that customers must be able to buy without an account; that point comes only from rejection messages.

### Cited Findings
- `Transaction.appAccountToken`: "A UUID that associates the transaction with a user on your own service." `PurchaseOption.appAccountToken(_:)`: "Sets a UUID to associate the purchase with an account in your system." App Store Server API: "To provide an app account token for a transaction that the customer completes outside of your app, or to update the value of an existing app account token, call the Set App Account Token endpoint." — [PRIMARY] — [Transaction.appAccountToken](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction/appaccounttoken), [appAccountToken(_:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/product/purchaseoption/appaccounttoken(_:)), [App Store Server API: appAccountToken](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appstoreserverapi/appaccounttoken). WWDC25: "the appAccountToken field, which associates a subscription with a customer account on your service. You optionally provide an appAccountToken at the time of purchase". — [PRIMARY] — [WWDC25 session 241](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/241/). Transfer: high. The silent device account's UUID can be the "user on your own service" at purchase, and the token can be re-pointed when the user signs in.
- `AppStore.sync()`: "When users reinstall your app or download it on a new device, the app automatically has all transactions available to it upon initial launch. There's no need for users to ask your app to restore transactions". It "displays a system prompt that asks users to authenticate with their App Store credentials. Call this function only in response to an explicit user action". Also: "Include some mechanism in your app, such as a Restore Purchases button". — [PRIMARY] — [sync()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/appstore/sync()), [Restoring purchased products](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/restoring-purchased-products).
- WWDC22 "Implement proactive in-app purchase restore": "No need to require customers to sign-in or tap "Restore Purchases." It just worked." "continue providing a "Restore Purchases" button … in case of an issue or if the customer uses a different Apple ID." — [PRIMARY] — [WWDC22 session 110404](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/110404/). Transfer: high; the clearest Apple statement that restoring needs no app sign-in.
- WWDC20 "Architecting for subscriptions": "If you're providing a subscription across multiple apps or platforms, this phase should include passing in the subscriber's status that exists outside of the app receipt to ensure they aren't locked out or presented with another subscription to purchase." — [PRIMARY] — [WWDC20 session 10671](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10671/). Transfer: cross-platform status is merged server-side. Nothing in the session says to force a login to do it.
- In-App Purchase page: "If someone redownloads your app or switches to a new device, be sure to immediately provide access to the content or functionality from their past Apple In‑App Purchases … Your app must also include a restore mechanism for any restorable Apple In‑App Purchases, per App Review Guideline 3.1.1." Subscriptions page: "A lengthy sign-up process will lower your subscription conversion rate, so keep the purchase flow simple and only ask for necessary information"; required: "A way for current subscribers to sign in or restore purchases". — [PRIMARY] — [developer.apple.com/in-app-purchase](https://developer.apple.com/in-app-purchase/), [developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/). Transfer: "immediately" on a new device does not fit "sign in first" as the only way back to Premium.
- App Review Tech Talk (published 2021-12-06): "We review the entire app, so if there are account-based features, we need to review the app experience both with and without an account." App Review Help: "If your app offers auto-renewable subscriptions, provide a demo account with an expired subscription to enable review of the entire purchase flow"; "App Review evaluates your In-App Purchases in the same sandbox environment you use for testing." — [PRIMARY] — [Tech Talk 10885](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/10885/), [complete review](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review), [configure IAP](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/configure-in-app-purchases). Transfer: high. Reviewers walk the no-account path, which is where the gate sits.
- App Review page: "On average, over 40% of unresolved issues are related to guideline 2.1". It has no 5.1.1-specific "common rejection" content. — [PRIMARY] — [App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] The server can stay the source of truth without the gate. Let the user buy in the no-account state and tag the transaction with the device account's UUID (`appAccountToken`, or RevenueCat's anonymous id). Move it to the real account at sign-in, and restore through StoreKit or Play Billing into whatever account is current. The account then does its proper job: keeping data and Premium across devices and platforms, offered with that explanation.

## 5. App Store Transparency Report: order of magnitude

### Takeaway
In 2025 App Review looked at 9,100,620 submissions and rejected 2,093,244 (23%). The "Legal" section, which contains 5.1 Privacy, accounted for 495,673 rejections. Apple's press release says "over 443,000" were for privacy violations, about 4.9% of all submissions. The reports break rejections down only by the five sections of the guidelines, with nothing for 5.1.1, 5.1.1(v) or registration walls. 387,087 rejected submissions were later approved.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 report: reviewed 9,100,620; rejected 2,093,244; by section Safety 151,159, Performance 1,354,418, Business 283,820, Design 415,532, Legal 495,673, Other 4,145; "App submissions approved after rejection" 387,087. Footnotes: "Apps may be submitted to App Review multiple times before they are approved"; "App submissions may be rejected for noncompliance with one or more guidelines." — [PRIMARY] — [2025 App Store Transparency Report](https://www.apple.com/legal/app-store/transparency/2025/). Transfer: the section counts sum to 2,704,747, more than the total, because one submission can be counted under several sections.
- Apple Newsroom, May 20, 2026: "the team rejected over 2 million app submissions — including over 1.2 million new apps and nearly 800,000 app updates"; "over 443,000 submissions for privacy violations". — [PRIMARY] — [Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/05/the-app-store-stopped-over-2-point-2-billion-usd-in-fraudulent-transactions-in-2025/).
- 2024 report: reviewed 7,771,599; rejected 1,931,400 (≈24.9%); Legal 445,696; Design 378,300; Business 209,845; approved after rejection 295,109. — [PRIMARY] — [2024 report (PDF)](https://www.apple.com/legal/more-resources/docs/2024-App-Store-Transparency-Report.pdf).
- Guideline numbers appear only for *removals* of live apps. The 2025 supplemental file gives "Guideline 5.1.1 - Data collection & storage" 19 removals, 5.1.2 30, 3.1.1 56, 3.1.2 26, against 90,608 for DPLA 3.2(f) fraud. — [PRIMARY] — [2025 Supplemental Data File](https://www.apple.com/legal/zip/2025-Supplemental-Data-File.zip) ("Total" rows). The report does not give rejections by guideline number or category, the split between new apps and updates per guideline, or App Review Board outcomes for rejections. Only removal appeals are counted: 26,305 appeals and 423 restorations in 2025. — [PRIMARY] — [2025 report](https://www.apple.com/legal/app-store/transparency/2025/).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] About 1 submission in 4 is rejected, and about 1 in 20 for privacy. The rate for login-before-purchase walls is unknown. The useful figure is conditional: when a reviewer meets mandatory registration before a non-account IAP, the forum record from 2020 to 2026 shows the same rejection every time, with one reported pass after explanation (§6). Removal of a live app for this reason is rare (19 removals under 5.1.1 for any reason in 2025). The real cost is a rejected submission.
- [INFERENCE] 18.5% of 2025 rejections ended in approval after a fix or reply. For a release that bundles many changes, a rejection means days of delay on the whole release, not the loss of the app.

## 6. Apple's dispute process

### Takeaway
The documented order is: reply in App Store Connect (and ask for a call if needed), then one appeal to the App Review Board, citing the guideline point by point. For apps already on the store, some rejections come with a Bug Fix Submission offer that lets the update through and defers the issue once. Apple's text excludes "legal or safety issues", yet 5.1.1 rejections have carried the offer. Precedent is not a ground: App Review will not discuss other apps, and an earlier approval did not prevent a later rejection. Review time: at least 50% of submissions in under 24 hours, 90% in under 48 hours.

### Cited Findings
- After You Submit: "Rejections: Our goal is to apply these guidelines fairly and consistently, but nobody's perfect. If your app has been rejected and you have questions or would like to provide additional information, please use App Store Connect to communicate directly with the App Review team." "Appeals: If you disagree with the outcome of your review, please submit an appeal … You may also suggest changes to the guidelines themselves". "if your app is repeatedly rejected for the same guideline violation … review of your app will take longer to complete." — [PRIMARY] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
- Bug Fix Submissions: "For apps that are already on the App Store or alternative distribution, bug fixes will not be delayed over guideline violations except for those related to legal or safety issues." Help: "If a bug-fix update is rejected but introduces no legal or safety concerns, you can choose to resolve the issues in your next submission." Eligible messages start with "Bug Fix Submissions: The issues we've identified below are eligible to be resolved on your next update"; "Do not resubmit the app at this time"; "Issues that were deferred on a previous submission aren't eligible to be deferred again". — [PRIMARY] — [same page](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [App Review Help: Bug Fix Submissions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/after-submitting-for-review/bug-fix-submissions). The rule first appears in the 1 October 2020 capture ("except for those related to legal issues") and is absent on 5 September 2020. — [PRIMARY] — [Wayback, 2020-10-01](https://web.archive.org/web/20201001065224/https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).
- 5.1.1 rejections with the offer anyway: November 2022, "Guideline 5.1.1(v) - Data Collection and Storage" (account deletion) under "Bug Fix Submissions"; June 2025, "Guideline 5.1.1 - Legal - Privacy - Data Collection and Storage" with "eligible to be resolved on your next update". — [PRIMARY] — [GitHub trakt/trakt-api discussion 327](https://github.com/trakt/trakt-api/discussions/327), [GitHub BasedHardware/omi issue 2615](https://github.com/BasedHardware/omi/issues/2615). Transfer: "legal" in the exclusion evidently means legal risk, not the guideline section.
- Contact App Review: "A specialist familiar with your app will reply to your message." "Provide additional information that can help App Review understand the app's concept and intended functionality if you believe your app was misunderstood." "App Review can't discuss other developers' apps with you." Calls "are typically completed within three to five business days." — [PRIMARY] — [App Review Help: Contact App Review](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/after-submitting-for-review/contact-app-review).
- Appeal: appropriate when "Your app was rejected or removed, but you believe it follows the App Review Guidelines and the Apple Developer Program License Agreement" or for "unfair or biased treatment". "Before filing an appeal, reply to the rejection … If this clarification is not sufficient … proceed with filing an appeal." "point to the specific requirements involved and explain how your app satisfies them." "Only submit one appeal per rejected or removed app." "does not guarantee that the App Review Board will overturn the rejection". Resubmitting closes the appeal. No turnaround time is published. — [PRIMARY] — [App Review Help: Appeal](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/after-submitting-for-review/appeal-to-the-app-review-board), [form](https://developer.apple.com/contact/app-store/?topic=appeal).
- Guideline suggestions: "Cite the specific guideline text you're suggesting a change for"; "Don't use this form to appeal a rejection"; no guarantee of change. — [PRIMARY] — [App Review Help: Suggest changes](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/after-submitting-for-review/improve-guidelines).
- Review time: "typically reviewing at least 50% of submissions in less than 24 hours and 90% in less than 48 hours." 30-minute pre-submission App Review appointments are offered. — [PRIMARY] — [App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/). "if it takes multiple submissions to solve the issue, it may take a few days". — [PRIMARY] — [Tech Talk 10885](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/10885/).
- Precedent: the guidelines are "a living document; new apps presenting new questions may result in new rules at any time". The September 2023 case: first version accepted, update rejected for the login wall; App Review's thread reply only pointed to an appeal. — [PRIMARY] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [forum 737212](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/737212).
- Outcomes when disputing this template. March 2025: a developer rejected with the template (RevenueCat restore setting "Keep with original App User ID", which in their words made purchase require registration) wrote: "We have explained to Apple that we have adopted a design that utilizes RevenueCat … and that a major redesign would be required … and the Review has passed." — [PRIMARY] (rejection quoted; outcome self-reported) — [RevenueCat Community 6041](https://community.revenuecat.com/sdks-51/handling-apple-app-review-rejection-with-revenuecat-s-keep-with-original-app-user-id-setting-6041); staff: "we've had devs do this with no issues" — [VENDOR]. February 2023: the board's reply, as quoted by the developer, accepted registration "for tracking users' purchases and game progresses" but not "before entering the game", and allowed warning that playing without an account "may result in losing game progress or in-app purchase items". — [PRIMARY] — [forum 724336](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/724336). The 2020, 2021, 2023 and 2026 threads report no approval.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] If the gate ships and is rejected, the cheapest route is a reply. Explain that the app works fully without an account, that the purchase is attached to an identity-less account, and that the account carries Premium to Android. One 2025 case passed this way. But the template answers the "all devices" point in advance, and the 2023 cross-platform case did not pass. An appeal can say truthfully that no guideline sentence forbids registration before purchase, but 5.1.1(x) gives the Board an easy textual answer.
- [INFERENCE] A Bug Fix deferral is a stopgap at best. Apple describes it for "bug-fix" updates, a feature release may not qualify, eligibility is printed in the rejection message, and it works only once.
- [INFERENCE] Each rejection costs about one to two days of review plus the rebuild, and repeated rejections slow review further. The current app's approval, with its login wall, protects nothing.

## 7. Google Play

### Takeaway
None of the Play policies I read forbids or regulates requiring an account before a purchase (Payments, Subscriptions, User Data, Deceptive Behavior, Device and Network Abuse). The Payments policy mentions "account creation or sign-up flows" only as a way of steering users to other payment methods, which it forbids. The nearest general limit is the User Data rule to keep collection to what users "reasonably expect". Review only needs sign-in details. In Play Billing the app-side account is optional, and purchases follow the Google account.

### Cited Findings
- Payments: no leading users to other payment methods via "In-app user interface flows, including account creation or sign-up flows, that lead users from an app to a payment method other than Google Play's billing system". — [DOCS] — [Play Console Help: Payments](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en).
- Subscriptions policy: disclose "whether a subscription is required to use the app … Users should not have to perform any additional action to review the information"; violations include "Dismiss button is missing or not clearly visible". Nothing on accounts. — [DOCS] — [Play Console Help: Subscriptions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en). Transfer: that "additional action" concerns reading the terms, not signing in.
- User Data: "Limit the access, collection, use and sharing of personal and sensitive user data … to app and service functionality and policy-conforming purposes reasonably expected by the user"; "Only collect and use data that is necessary for your app's functionality." — [DOCS] — [User Data](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en).
- Account deletion: "An app account is a unique user identity that developers provide as a user-facing feature to serve the user across applications and/or devices"; "If your app offers account creation in any part of the app experience, then you still need to offer app account deletion even if some features can be accessed without an account." — [DOCS] — [Play Console Help 13327111](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en). Transfer: deletion is required anyway since sign-in exists. Whether the silent device account is itself an "app account" (not user-facing) is arguable and moot.
- Deceptive Behavior / Device and Network Abuse: nothing on login requirements beyond impersonation ("trick users into disclosing personal or authentication information"). — [DOCS] — [Deceptive Behavior](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888077?hl=en), [Device and Network Abuse](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888379?hl=en).
- App access: "If your entire app or parts of your app are restricted based on login credentials … you must provide all required details to enable access to your app." — [DOCS] — [Prepare your app for review](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859455?hl=en).
- Billing: `setObfuscatedAccountId` "Specifies an optional obfuscated string that is uniquely associated with the purchaser's user account in your app … Storing PII in this field results in purchases being blocked." (updated 2026-05-19). Query purchases at each connection because "A user may buy an item on one device and then expect to see the item when they switch devices" (updated 2026-11-13). — [DOCS] — [BillingFlowParams.Builder](https://developer.android.com/reference/com/android/billingclient/api/BillingFlowParams.Builder), [Integrate Play Billing](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate).
- No Google phone design or policy page found that says to let people buy before signing in. The only "delay sign-in" guidance is for Wear OS. — [DOCS] — [Wear OS sign-in](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/behaviors-and-patterns/sign-in).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] On Play the gate is a conversion choice, not a review risk. All the review risk is on Apple's side. A per-platform design (gate on Android only) would split code and product for no review benefit.

## Gaps / could not verify

- **Pre-September-2014 guidelines** (behind the developer login; Wayback has only the sign-in page for 2012) could not be read. The first public version (2014) has no such sentence, and that numbered set ran continuously from 2010 [INFERENCE].
- **Coverage:** 32 versions from 2014 to 2026 were checked, not every revision. A sentence added and removed between two captures would be missed (unlikely, since edits were incremental and announced).
- **First use of the template:** the earliest first-hand copy found is April 2020. Older uses may exist elsewhere (Stack Overflow, non-English forums).
- **"Account based":** Apple defines neither "significant account-based features" nor "products that are not account based". There is no Apple statement on cross-platform server-held entitlements. The evidence is one reported pass (2025) and one rejection (2023).
- **Frequency:** there are no per-guideline rejection counts, so the rejection rate for login-before-purchase is unknown. Forum cases are self-selected and rarely report outcomes.
- **App Review Board:** no published turnaround time or overturn rate for rejections.
- **Bug Fix eligibility for feature releases:** not documented. The two 5.1.1 cases found do not say what the update contained.
- **App Store Connect Help pages** on in-app purchases and subscriptions were not read in full (App Review Help and developer.apple.com pages were).
- **HIG Managing accounts** has no change log; the URL dates from at least May 2023, and the wording from at least March 2016 in older pages.
- **Dating sources:** 9to5Mac (June 4, 2018) was seen only in search results, and MacRumors (October 22, 2021) was read. Both were used only to date updates already confirmed by Wayback captures.
- **Forum posts** show month and year only, and some were read through a summarising fetch. The rejection paragraphs agree across the 2020–2026 threads and the earlier note, but the original App Store Connect messages were not seen.
